🌐 TOP STORIES

Lean and Mathlib Communities Certify Claude-Assisted Riemann Hypothesis Proof

New developments: The Lean and Mathlib communities, endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative, said on November 21 they have full confidence that Levent Alpöge's proof of the Riemann Hypothesis is correct. Dozens of people compiled the 17-million-line Lean library, two tools confirmed it proves Mathlib's statement, and several independent checkers validated the export. Alpöge, an Anthropic mathematician, has said he found the proof working with Claude.

METR Finds AI Time Horizons Doubling Roughly Every 3.6 Months

METR's November 20 research update, covering public models up to Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra, finds time horizons on its combined TH1.1 and TH2 task suites doubling roughly every 3.6 months. Models usually match or outperform skilled humans on its coding and optimization tasks but show worse judgment than experts when clear feedback is absent. METR says public evidence cannot rule out drastic AI R&D acceleration within a couple of years.

OpenRouter Warns MiniMax Prompts May Have Been Read by a Third Party

OpenRouter emailed customers that prompts and outputs sent to MiniMax models may have been read by a third party, Privacy Affairs reported. A security researcher's unique test links were opened 40 to 130 hours after requests, including requests forwarded by hosts AtlasCloud, Novita and StreamLake. OpenRouter removed the Zero Data Retention label from the endpoints. According to the email, MiniMax told StreamLake that content moderation had been switched on.

Cursor May Phase Out Chinese Open-Weight Models, House Report Says

Anysphere, developer of the SpaceX-owned coding platform Cursor, told House investigators it plans to explore phasing out Chinese open-weight models because of geopolitical risk, The Next Web reported, citing a joint report released Friday by two House committees. No decision or timetable was given. Cursor built its Composer 2 model on Moonshot AI's Kimi K2.5. The committees also want the federal DeepSeek ban extended to other Chinese developers.

Trump Threatens Tax on US Companies Building Data Centers Abroad

President Trump said Friday that US companies will be banned from building data centers in other countries and will face a "tremendous tax" if they do, CNBC reported. He said he had not yet told his own staff. Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta and Oracle have already built data centers abroad. Amazon, Google, Microsoft and Oracle did not immediately respond to CNBC's requests for comment.
