StepFun Introduces Step 5 Preview at Agent Event

StepFun says it introduced Step 5 Preview at its Agent Night Out event in San Francisco. The discussion focused on agents that run longer, fail less and cost less, with participating companies including TinyFish and Featherless AI. The announcement establishes a preview, not general availability, and supplies no independently verified performance or pricing comparison.

AlphaProof Nexus Helps Prove a Quantum Optics No-Go Theorem

Researchers report an AI-assisted formal proof of a constraint on high-dimensional photonic GHZ states. Using AlphaProof Nexus and Lean, they show that the specified graph construction is impossible for even photon counts of at least four when dimensionality is at least the photon count. The preprint also interprets the mathematical result physically; its scope is this construction, not all entanglement generation.

Gemma 4 Drafting Research Reports Up to 3.1x Inference Speedup

A research team presents a multi-token prediction drafter that shares backbone activations and the existing key-value cache, avoiding a separate drafting prefill. Tests with Gemma 4 report speedups up to 3.1 times on large server configurations and 2.2 times on a smartphone GPU. These are the authors’ configuration-dependent results, not a guarantee for every deployment.

Arena Finds Coding Gains and Chat Tradeoffs for GPT-6.2 Astra

New developments: Arena publishes a task-level comparison following OpenAI’s model release. Its reported coding score rises from 1,288 for GPT-6 Astra to 1,338 for GPT-6.2 Astra, while chat falls from 1,265 to 1,211. The newer model ranks sixth overall in this evaluation. The results illustrate differences within Arena’s measured tasks, not universal superiority or regression.

GAO Examines Safety Gaps in Mental Health Chatbots

The Government Accountability Office says the FDA has not authorized any chatbot for mental health care as of its November review. It highlights uncertain effectiveness, privacy risks and sycophancy, and warns that some guardrails may not work reliably. Purpose-built systems could supplement professional services, the report says, but current evidence does not justify treating them as substitutes for qualified providers.
