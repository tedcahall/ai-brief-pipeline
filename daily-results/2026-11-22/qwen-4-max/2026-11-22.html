🌐 TOP STORIES

Lean and Mathlib Communities Certify Alpöge's Claude-Assisted Riemann Proof

New developments: the Lean and Mathlib communities, endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative, certified that Levent Alpöge's 17-million-line Lean library correctly proves the Riemann hypothesis. Independent checkers, including the formally verified con-leche checker, validated the exported proof against Mathlib's own statement. A rival all-human proof of the generalized hypothesis appeared the same day and quickly drew AI-assisted reports of errors.

OpenRouter Warns MiniMax Prompts May Have Been Read After Canary Test

OpenRouter told customers that prompts sent to MiniMax models may have been read by a third party, after researcher HD Moore planted nearly two million trackable canary secrets in AI requests. Moore found a Chinese lab's systems fetched secret URLs from more than half of test prompts, including many marked zero-data-retention. OpenRouter removed its Zero Data Retention label from MiniMax endpoints.

METR Finds AI R&D Time Horizons Doubling Every 3.6 Months as Judgment Lags

METR's Q4 capability update finds frontier AI time horizons on its combined TH1.1 and TH2 task suites doubling roughly every 3.6 months, with a 95% confidence interval of 2.1 to 4.9 months. Models usually match or beat skilled humans on coding and optimization tasks but show worse judgment than experts on open-ended research choices. METR says public evidence cannot rule out drastic AI R&D acceleration within a couple of years.

GPT-6.2 Astra Takes Top Spot on RoboColosseum's SO-101 Robot Board

GPT-6.2 Astra reached first place on RoboColosseum's SO-101 robot-arm board with a 1,984 difficulty-calibrated rating, ahead of Claude Fable 5.2 at 1,803. The result rests on 122 blind comparisons and a 22% full-task success rate, and the leaderboard files the lead inside error bars. Greg Brockman amplified the ranking; specialist policy pi0.5 placed fourth.

GAO Warns AI Chatbots Are Not FDA-Authorized for Mental Health Care

The U.S. Government Accountability Office published a spotlight warning that the FDA has not authorized any AI chatbot for mental health care. Nearly one-fifth of Americans aged 12 to 21 reported using an AI chatbot for mental health advice in 2025. GAO says sycophantic responses can reinforce delusions or self-harm, and some guardrails may be ineffective.
