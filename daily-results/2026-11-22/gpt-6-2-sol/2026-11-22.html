🌐 TOP STORIES

Lean and Mathlib Verify Claude-Assisted Riemann Hypothesis Proof

New developments: Lean and Mathlib reviewers confirmed that Levent Alpöge’s formal proof establishes the Riemann Hypothesis itself, not a weaker substitute. Independent teams compiled the code and checked the exported proof with multiple proof checkers. Alpöge credits Claude with helping discover and formalize the result; the verification establishes the mathematics, not an autonomous AI solution.

AlphaProof Nexus Finds a Limit on Generating Photonic Entanglement

Researchers report that Google DeepMind’s AlphaProof Nexus found a proof addressing a quantum optics question posed in 2017. Under the paper’s minimal setup, linear optics and pair sources cannot generate the target entangled state when there are at least four photons, the photon count is even, and the dimensionality is at least that count. The authors explain the proof’s physical meaning in a preprint.

METR Expands Its AI Task Evaluations as Models Improve

METR added tasks that are harder to grade or require judgment, beyond work with answers that can be checked cheaply. Its combined evaluation suite estimates that frontier models’ task horizons doubled about every 3.6 months. That is a trend on the measured tasks, not proof that AI can independently run a research lab or that all capabilities are improving at the same rate.

U.S. Cloud Providers Help Chinese AI Models Reach Global Users

Fortune reports that providers including Together AI, Baseten and Fireworks serve Chinese models on U.S.-designed chips. The distinction matters after new U.S. remote-access restrictions: an overseas customer renting restricted computing power is a different transaction from an independent cloud operator hosting a Chinese open-weight model for its own customers. Amazon Bedrock also lists DeepSeek models.

GAO Finds No FDA-Authorized Chatbots for Mental Health Care

The Government Accountability Office says nearly one in five surveyed Americans aged 12 to 21 reported using an AI chatbot for mental health advice in 2025. Its review says the FDA has not authorized any chatbot for mental health care and warns that chatbots may reinforce users’ beliefs rather than challenge them. Some purpose-built tools are in trials, but evidence of effectiveness remains limited.
