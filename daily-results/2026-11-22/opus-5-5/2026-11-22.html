🌐 TOP STORIES

Lean and Mathlib Communities Certify Claude-Assisted Riemann Hypothesis Proof

New developments: the Lean and Mathlib communities, in a statement endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative, said they have full confidence that Levent Alpöge's 17-million-line Lean proof is correct. Two independent tools confirmed it proves Mathlib's exact Riemann hypothesis statement, and several proof checkers accepted the export. MIT's Andrew Sutherland reported some checks still running as of Nov. 22.

OpenRouter Off-Boards Three Providers After Dragos Finds Prompt Theft

OpenRouter off-boarded three inference providers, removed 125 endpoints from Zero Data Retention eligibility and notified affected customers after research by security firm Dragos. Dragos said it sent about two million prompts containing canary secrets from July through November, found evidence that at least three hosting providers were breached, and saw prompts sent to U.S. resellers fetched by a Chinese AI lab's systems.

METR Says AI Time Horizons Are Doubling Every 3.6 Months

METR's November capability update, covering public models up to Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra, found 50% time horizons on its combined TH1.1 and TH2 task suites doubling roughly every 3.6 months. Models usually matched or beat skilled humans on coding and optimization tasks but showed weaker judgment than experts where feedback is slow, and METR said current evidence cannot rule out drastic AI R&D acceleration.

AlphaProof Nexus Settles a Quantum Optics Question With a Lean Proof

Researchers from the University of Tübingen, Google DeepMind and other institutions posted a paper proving that high-dimensional photonic GHZ states cannot be generated with only linear optics and pair sources for any even photon count of four or more when dimensions are at least the photon count. Google DeepMind's AlphaProof Nexus found the proof through AI-assisted formal proof search in Lean.

Musk Warns of a 'Nuts' AI Cash Shortage Next Year

Elon Musk, replying to François Chollet's argument that demand for AI compute remains strong, wrote that getting large amounts of compute turned on and working reliably is incredibly hard. He said the cash shortage will be "nuts" next year, because even a few months of industry delay between paying for compute and earning revenue from it would require hundreds of billions of dollars in working capital.
