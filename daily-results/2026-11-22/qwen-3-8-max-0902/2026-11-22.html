🌐 TOP STORIES

Lean and Mathlib Communities Verify Claude-Assisted Riemann Proof

New developments: the proof claimed Friday is now machine-certified. The Lean FRO and Mathlib Initiative said Saturday they have full confidence that Levent Alpöge's proof of the Riemann Hypothesis is correct, after community members reproduced compilation of the 17-million-line library and two independent tools confirmed it proves Mathlib's exact statement. Lean's kernel and several third-party checkers validated an inert proof export.

Tesla Begins Rolling Out FSD v15 With Reality View and 25x Claim

Tesla started rolling out FSD v15 on Saturday to early-access AI4 cars, headlined by Reality View, a live visualization Tesla says is generated in real time without a game engine using about half of one AI4 TRIP accelerator. Release notes cite a new vision encoder shared with Robotaxi and Optimus that Tesla claims improves the critical intervention rate 25x, with no baseline published.

OpenAI Merges Codex Desktop Into Unified ChatGPT App

OpenAI is folding the standalone Codex desktop app into a unified ChatGPT desktop app bundling Chat, Work, and Codex on macOS and Windows, according to a help-center guide published Saturday. Existing Codex chats and projects carry over, the prior app remains available as ChatGPT Classic, and cloud Work chats sync across web, mobile, and desktop.

Canary Tests Expose Prompt Leaks Across the AI Inference Supply Chain

Researcher HD Moore planted unique canary secrets in nearly 2 million prompts sent through AI routers and inference providers and found at least three providers compromised, resellers passing prompts along, and zero-data-retention flags ignored. Ars Technica reported Saturday that OpenRouter emailed customers that prompts routed to MiniMax, directly or via three resellers, may have been read, and removed MiniMax's ZDR labels.

METR Finds AI Task Horizons Still Doubling Every 3.6 Months

METR's Q4 2026 capability update, covering public models up to Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra, found time horizons on AI R&D-relevant tasks doubling roughly every 3.6 months, extrapolating naively to 200-hour tasks by July 2027. METR said public evidence is insufficient to rule out drastic AI R&D acceleration in the next couple of years and documented continued reward hacking.
