🌐 TOP STORIES

Lean and Mathlib Communities Certify Claude-Assisted Riemann Hypothesis Proof

A day after Levent Alpöge, an Anthropic mathematician who said he worked with Claude, released a 17-million-line Lean proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, the Lean and Mathlib communities issued a joint statement certifying it. They report that dozens of people reproduced the compilation, two independent tools confirmed the theorem matches Mathlib's own statement, and several proof checkers accepted the exported proof.

METR Says Frontier AI Time Horizons Double Roughly Every 3.6 Months

METR's Q4 capability update, covering public models up to Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra, reports that frontier time horizons on its TH1.1 and TH2 task suites double roughly every 3.6 months (95% CI 2.1 to 4.9). METR says models usually match or beat skilled human baseliners on its coding and optimization tasks, and that public evidence is insufficient to rule out drastic AI R&D acceleration within a couple of years.

Tesla Begins Rolling Out FSD v15 to Early Access Members

Tesla said Full Self-Driving v15 has started rolling out to Early Access Program members, after Elon Musk said earlier that day that wide release would come in December. Tesla's release notes describe a vision encoder shared across FSD, Robotaxi and Optimus that it says improves the critical intervention rate by 25x, plus end-to-end learned navigation for trip starts and ends. The 25x figure is Tesla's own claim.

Fortune: US-Chip Data Centers Are Helping Spread Chinese AI Models

A Fortune article argues that U.S.-chip data centers rented to inference providers such as Together AI, Baseten and Fireworks are helping Chinese models spread. It notes DeepSeek's latest model is offered by at least 16 providers headquartered outside China, including Amazon Bedrock, and that Moonshot requires a revenue-sharing license from companies serving Kimi K3 that exceed $20 million in annual sales.

Musk Warns of a 2027 AI Cash Shortage as Chollet Argues Compute Demand Holds

Replying to François Chollet's post that GPU spot prices keep rising and HBM and DRAM output is sold out through 2029, Elon Musk wrote that the AI cash shortage is "going to be nuts next year." He said a few months of delay between paying for compute and getting it to produce revenue would require hundreds of billions of dollars in working capital. UBS separately forecasts $1.4 trillion of global AI investment in 2027.
