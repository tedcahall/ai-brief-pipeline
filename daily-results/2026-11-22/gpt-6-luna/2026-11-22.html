Lean and Mathlib Certify a Claude-Assisted Riemann Hypothesis Proof

New developments: The Lean and Mathlib communities say they verified the formal statement and proof, report that the library compiles, and cite independent checkers. The work is Levent Alpöge's proof produced with Claude and then formalized in Lean; the statement confirms the Riemann Hypothesis, not the broader Generalized Riemann Hypothesis.

Google DeepMind's Pro² Agent Reaches 76% on the Complete MLE-Bench

The paper proposes a Problem State representation that tracks suspected blockers and candidate fixes while an agent tests research hypotheses. On the complete MLE-Bench, its authors report a 70.7% medal rate with 24 hours and 76.0% with 48 hours, the highest reported under that longer budget. These benchmark results do not establish general autonomous research capability.

Dragos Finds Prompt Exposure Across the AI Inference Supply Chain

Dragos says its five-month study sent roughly two million prompts containing unique canaries to AI services. It reports evidence of exposure at three or more providers, marketplace resellers accessing prompts through standard logs, and apparent failures to follow customer privacy settings. The report identifies observed indicators and does not claim every provider or request was compromised.

Anthropic Shares Fall 8.5% on Their First Full Trading Day

New developments: On Friday, Anthropic shares fell 8.5% to $97.17 in the first full trading session, after opening at $111.50 on Thursday, CNBC reports. The company raised $81.3 billion in its IPO, CNBC says; Anthropic's closing notice separately reports about $93.5 billion gross including the exercised option. These are proceeds, not company valuation.

U.S.-Owned Data Centers Also Serve Chinese AI Models

New developments: Fortune reports U.S.-owned data centers with U.S.-designed chips provide capacity to inference services that make Chinese language models available globally. It cites DeepSeek on multiple providers outside China, including Amazon Bedrock. This highlights how U.S. compute infrastructure can support competing Chinese models; it is an investigation, not a government estimate.
