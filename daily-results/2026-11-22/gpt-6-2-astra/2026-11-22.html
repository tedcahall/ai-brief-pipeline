📊 AI INDUSTRY PULSE

Independent proof checkers now agree that Levent Alpöge’s Claude-assisted work proves the Riemann hypothesis as stated in Mathlib. In quantum optics, researchers used a different system, AlphaProof Nexus, to show why a proposed arrangement of photon sources cannot produce certain entangled states.

🌐 TOP STORIES

Lean and Mathlib Verify Alpöge’s Claude-Assisted Riemann Proof

New developments: The Lean and Mathlib communities say they verified Levent Alpöge’s proof, not just that his code compiles. Two tools independently confirmed that the theorem in his library matches Mathlib’s statement of the Riemann hypothesis. Multiple checkers then verified the exported proof. Those checks establish what the code proves without requiring readers to trust Claude’s reasoning.

AlphaProof Nexus Finds a Limit on Entangled Photon Sources

Researchers used AlphaProof Nexus to find a proof in Lean that rules out one way of producing entangled photons. With pair sources and linear optics alone, their setup cannot generate the target GHZ state for an even number of photons starting at four when the dimension is at least the photon count. The result does not rule out setups with additional resources.

Astra Gains Five Places in Coding but Slips in Chat

Arena reports that GPT-6.2 Astra at its Max setting ranks third in coding, up from eighth for its predecessor. On chat tasks, which include tutoring and writing, it falls from eighth to eleventh. The overall agent ranking moves only one place, to sixth, obscuring a tradeoff for teams choosing a model for a particular job.

NDIF Opens Inkling’s Repeated Computation to Inspection

New developments: Thinking Machines says NDIF is hosting both Inkling 2 Mini and its variable-loop variant. Its guide shows researchers how to read, change and steer activations across loops. That lets researchers test what changes on successive passes through the model rather than trying to infer the effect of extra computation solely from the final answer.

One Gemini Drawing Costs Six Times More at Its Highest Effort

Simon Willison ran the same SVG drawing prompt across Gemini models and reasoning settings. On Hydrogen, switching from high to extra-high effort took output from 5,311 tokens to 31,477 and cost from 3.99 cents to 23.61 cents. The grid makes the price and latency tradeoff visible for this drawing, not a general estimate for production workloads.
