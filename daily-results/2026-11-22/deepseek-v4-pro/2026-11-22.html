🌐 TOP STORIES

Claude Helps Prove the Riemann Hypothesis

Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpoge claimed on November 20 that he used Claude to discover a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, the most famous open problem in mathematics. Two independent groups - the Lean and Mathlib communities - verified the proof with a joint statement of 'full confidence.' The proof comprises a 250k+ page 'book' and a 17.4-million-line Lean formalization. On the same day, a separate 940-page human-written proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis also emerged.

Google Releases Gemini 4 Neon with Frontier Reasoning at Flash Speeds

New developments: Early user reports confirm Gemini 4 Neon delivers frontier reasoning at Flash speeds with remarkably human-sounding output. Google engineering lead Jaana Dogan, after 8+ hours of use, said it "articulates my thoughts better than I do." The model, Google"s fourth Gemini 4 release in under two months, competes directly with Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.2 Sol in the mid-tier.

OpenAI Rolls Out GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol with Robotics, Disease Research

OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol on November 17. Astra targets complex professional work; Sol handles everyday tasks. OpenAI president Greg Brockman demonstrated GPT-6.2 robotics capabilities, and the company showcased GPT-Rosalind, applying the model to neurodegenerative disease research with Scripps Research. GPT-6.2 Astra ranks #6 on the Agent Arena Index, with OpenAI models now topping alignment benchmarks.

Thinking Machines Releases Inkling 2 Mini Open-Weight Looped Transformer

New developments: The 26B open-weight looped transformer is now available on Ollama with single-command local installation and Unsloth has released 4-bit quantization. AI researcher Nathan Lambert praised Thinking Machines for "shouldering so much pressure of the US open model ecosystem." The model runs on a laptop with 5.4B active parameters and leads small models on agentic coding benchmarks.

US Tightens AI Chip Controls While Chinese Models Run on American Infrastructure

The Trump administration tightened AI chip export controls on November 16 to block China from accessing compute remotely, but a CSET investigation published November 21 found at least 16 inference providers including Together AI and Amazon Bedrock serving Chinese open-weight models on US-chip-powered data centers. The House Foreign Affairs Committee disclosed nearly 900,000 advanced AI chips were approved for China export from December 2025 through May 2026, highlighting enforcement gaps.
