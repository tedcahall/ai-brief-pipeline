AI-assisted science is moving from headline claims toward inspectable evidence: Andrew Sutherland's latest verification report adds independent-kernel acceptance and a reproducible sandboxed build for the Claude-assisted Riemann proof, while retaining unfinished checks and a precise statement of scope. 🌐 TOP STORIES

Independent Kernels Accept Claude-Assisted Riemann Proof

New developments: MIT mathematician Andrew Sutherland reports that two independent kernels accept the exported proof, additional statement comparisons pass, and a sandboxed rebuild reproduces the export byte for byte. His verification page also records checks still running. The verified generalized result covers Dirichlet L-functions, not every family of L-functions.

Gemma 4 Drafting Research Reports Up to 3.1x Inference Speedup

Researchers describe a shared multi-token drafting head that reuses the target model's activations and KV cache. Their Gemma 4 tests report speedups up to 3.1x on large server configurations and 2.2x on a smartphone GPU. These are workload-specific research results, not a guarantee that every application becomes equally faster.

AlphaProof Nexus Helps Prove a Quantum-Optics No-Go Theorem

A new preprint reports an AI-assisted formal proof in Lean of a limit on constructing high-dimensional photonic GHZ states. The theorem rules out a specified graph construction for even photon counts of at least four when dimension is at least the photon count. The authors explain the physical interpretation; this is not a general ban on quantum entanglement.

Pro² Agent Makes Research Problems an Explicit Working State

Pro² Agent tracks suspected problems and candidate solutions rather than treating the codebase alone as its research state. The authors report a 70.7% medal rate on the complete MLE-Bench with 24 hours per task and 76.0% with 48 hours. The higher result uses twice the time budget, an important qualification when comparing agents.

GAO Warns Mental-Health Chatbots Lack FDA Authorization

The Government Accountability Office says no AI chatbot has FDA marketing authorization for mental-health care as of November. Its technology spotlight distinguishes accelerated-review designations from authorization and warns about sycophancy, privacy and uncertain effectiveness. Purpose-built systems may eventually complement care, but GAO says more high-quality evidence is needed before substituting them for professional treatment.
