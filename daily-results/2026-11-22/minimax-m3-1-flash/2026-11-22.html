🌐 TOP STORIES

Lean and Mathlib Groups Certify Claude-Assisted Riemann Hypothesis Proof

New developments: on Saturday the Lean Focused Research Organization and the Mathlib Initiative said they had independently verified Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpoge's proof, stating they have full confidence it is correct. Alpoge posted a note on Friday saying Claude helped develop the proof over roughly six weeks, and released the Lean artifact rather than a written argument. The announcement came one day after Anthropic's Nasdaq listing, timing Alpoge called horrible.

US Data Centers on American Chips Serve Chinese AI Models Worldwide

US-owned data centers filled with US-designed chips rent capacity to inference providers that serve Chinese language models to users outside China, Fortune reported. DeepSeek's latest model is offered by at least 16 inference providers headquartered outside China, including Amazon Bedrock, and Moonshot AI has reportedly asked Microsoft, Amazon and Google for 30 percent of Kimi K3 revenue. Commerce issued a rule this week closing a remote-access export-control gap.

Google DeepMind Agents Derive a Machine-Checked Quantum No-Go Theorem

Researchers at Google DeepMind and the University of Tubingen published a proof that no complex-weighted monochromatic quantum graph exists for any even photon count of four or more when the dimension is at least the photon count, answering a 2017 question about high-dimensional GHZ states built with linear optics and pair sources. AlphaProof Nexus, a formal proof search system combining language models with Lean compiler feedback, found the proof, and a computer verifies it.

OpenAI Flagship Ranks Sixth on Agent Arena Index With Weaker Chat Scores

New developments: Arena.ai's first Agent Arena Index placed OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra sixth with a score of 1,291, nine points above its predecessor. Coding improved to 1,338 points from 1,288 while chat fell to 1,211 from 1,265, and median task cost dropped to 2.94 dollars from 4.91 dollars. Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2 took the top spot, with several Google DeepMind models also ahead of OpenAI.

GAO Finds No FDA-Authorized AI Chatbot for Mental Health Support

The US Government Accountability Office reported that no AI chatbot has FDA authorization to support mental health care as of November 2026, and that nearly one fifth of Americans aged 12 to 21 used an AI chatbot for mental health advice in 2025. The agency warned that general purpose chatbots tend to be agreeable and flattering, that some guardrails may be ineffective, and that no universal off switch exists.
