🌐 TOP STORIES

Lean and Mathlib Communities Certify Claude-Assisted Riemann Proof

New developments: The Lean and Mathlib communities say they have verified the statement and proof of Levent Alpöge’s Claude-assisted Riemann Hypothesis work. Their joint statement cites multiple proof checkers and independent comparisons against the standard theorem statement. The certification follows yesterday’s coverage of the initial claim; the Clay Mathematics Institute still lists the problem as unsolved.

AlphaProof Nexus Finds a Quantum Optics No-Go Theorem

Researchers from Tübingen, Google DeepMind and other institutions describe a quantum optics result found through formal proof search using AlphaProof Nexus. Their preprint shows a limitation on constructing certain high-dimensional entangled photon states from specified resources. The authors interpret the AI-discovered mathematical proof physically; the paper is a preprint, not an independent replication.

Pro² Agent Researchers Report 76% Medal Rate With 48 Hours

A research team’s Pro² Agent maintains an explicit record of obstacles and candidate solutions while conducting experiments. The authors report a 70.7% medal rate on the complete MLE-Bench with a 24-hour budget and 76.0% with 48 hours. These are the researchers’ reported benchmark results, not evidence that the agent independently replaces human researchers.

GAO Warns of Risks in Mental Health Chatbots

The US Government Accountability Office says general-purpose chatbots can be dangerous when used for mental health advice and that some safeguards may be ineffective. It reports that the FDA had not granted marketing authorization to any mental health chatbot as of November. The agency calls for more research and considers privacy, effectiveness and regulation among the unresolved issues.

US Lawmakers Seek Stronger Controls on AI Chip Access

US lawmakers are calling for additional safeguards on advanced AI chips after a Commerce Department rule aimed at restricting remote access by Chinese entities. Al Jazeera reports that officials are also scrutinizing how controls are enforced abroad. The proposed additional measures are distinct from the existing rule, and their eventual scope remains uncertain.
