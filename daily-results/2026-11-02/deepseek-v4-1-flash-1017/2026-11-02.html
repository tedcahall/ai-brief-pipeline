🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic's amended S-1 shows $17 billion in quarterly revenue and $895.4 billion in compute commitments

Anthropic filed an amended Form S-1 with the SEC on November 2, disclosing roughly $17 billion in third-quarter revenue, up about 1,127% year over year, and $33.24 billion for the nine months ended September 30. The filing reports about $895.4 billion in aggregate purchase commitments and lease agreements, substantially all related to compute and infrastructure. Quarter-over-quarter growth slowed to about 47% from 144% in Q2.

OpenAI releases GPT-6.1 Sol at one-fifth of Astra's token price

OpenAI introduced GPT-6.1 Sol, an upgrade to GPT-6 Sol that the company says nearly matches GPT-6 Astra on agentic coding, computer use and professional work at one-fifth of Astra's standard input and output token prices. OpenAI reports GPT-6.1 Sol matches Astra on DeepSWE v1.1 at roughly one-fifth the cost and beats Opus 5.5 on AutomationBench at medium effort. Cached input is $0.10 per million tokens. These are vendor-reported evaluations, not independently replicated.

OpenAI signs four Abu Dhabi agreements spanning government, energy and investment

OpenAI signed agreements with Abu Dhabi entities at the ADIPEC conference, including a multi-year deal making state oil company ADNOC the first energy company to deploy OpenAI's Frontier agent platform, plus cooperation with G42 unit Inception42 on AI products for large UAE organizations. The deals sit inside Abu Dhabi's 2025-2027 digital strategy, which allocates about $3.5 billion toward becoming what it calls the world's first AI-native government.

South Korea proposes $16 billion for AI and chips in its 2027 budget

President Lee Jae Myung told the National Assembly that South Korea will allocate 21.3 trillion won, about $16 billion, to semiconductors, physical AI and AI data centers in the 2027 budget, nearly double the 2026 level. Total proposed spending rises 12.8% to 820.9 trillion won, or about $610 billion. Lee framed the goal as a sovereign AI model with world-leading performance.

DeepSeek open-sources the architecture behind its Super-agent

DeepSeek's Harness team published dsh-superagent-prototype on GitHub under an MIT license, a proactive coding agent for a single repository that listens to GitHub and optionally Feishu, opens work items, runs each in its own agent session, and answers in the original conversation. The accompanying design write-up argues human supervision caps productivity at roughly three to five concurrent agent sessions. DeepSeek labels it research code, not an official product.
