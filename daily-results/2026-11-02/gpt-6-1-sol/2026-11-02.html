🌐 TOP STORIES

DeepSeek releases a research prototype for parallel coding agents

DeepSeek's Harness team released an MIT-licensed prototype that routes authorized requests for one GitHub repository into separate agent sessions and directories, with optional Feishu integration and a panel for human decisions. Its README explicitly says this is research code, not an official DeepSeek product or production-ready software, and that parts of the broader Super-agent design remain unimplemented.

SemiAnalysis launches GDPx, with the leading model scoring 12%

SemiAnalysis introduced GDPx to evaluate agents across 11 domains, including chip design, biology, cybersecurity and software engineering. The publisher reports a highest overall score of 12%, using domain-specific environments and native agent harnesses where available. Safety refusals affect some model rankings, and the selected domains are not a representative measure of the entire economy; Ethan Mollick also questions missing human baselines.

MiniMax details lower-memory deployment options for M3.1 Flash

MiniMax highlighted community quantization work for its 428B-parameter M3.1 Flash model, including Unsloth's 143.9 GB, 2-bit Dynamic GGUF weights and recipes spanning DGX Spark, Apple Silicon, AMD Instinct and multiple RTX 3090 GPUs. The announcement describes calibration, kernels and expert placement as necessary companions to smaller weights. These are deployment options, not a newly announced base model or independently verified quality-equivalence claim.

Cisco Talos documents malware that uses AI to prioritize stolen assets

Cisco Talos analyzed VOLTA, a Windows infostealer with an optional AI layer that turns stolen credentials and personal records into ranked target assessments. Its configured model chain starts with Claude Opus 4.6 and can fall back to Kimi K2 and Gemini 2.5 Flash after refusals. This is documented malware integration, not evidence of an autonomous model escaping containment or of every configured model complying.

California lawmakers prepare additional safeguards for AI agents

Spectrum News reports that California lawmakers are preparing further AI safety legislation after questioning Anthropic, Google and OpenAI about agents acting beyond users' intentions. The hearing addressed responsibility for misaligned actions, while companies described testing and safeguards. The report concerns legislative preparation, not an enacted new law; its aviation-attack example was a lawmaker's hypothetical, not a reported incident.
