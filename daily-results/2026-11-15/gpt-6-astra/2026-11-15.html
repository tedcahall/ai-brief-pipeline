🌐 TOP STORIES

Perplexity Adds Exact Text Extraction to Its Open-Weight Decider Models

Perplexity’s Decider v1.3 and Decider Flash v1.3 add “locate,” which returns matching text and character positions rather than generating an answer. The models combine extraction, classification and scoring over shared inputs and are available under an Apache license. Artificial Analysis subsequently measured Decision Index scores of 70 and 64; Perplexity’s separate internal performance figures remain company-reported results.

Thinking Machines Opens Inkling 2 Small Weights and Deployment Options

New developments: Thinking Machines’ November 12 update makes Inkling 2 Small’s weights downloadable, following its earlier closed-weight introduction. The multimodal mixture-of-experts model has 594 billion total parameters and 17 billion active. Its model card lists local inference integrations and a minimum 500GB of aggregate GPU memory for the native NVFP4 checkpoint, making “Small” a relative model-family label, not a consumer-hardware promise.

Artificial Analysis Refreshes Coding-Agent Tests, With Claude Models Tied at the Top

Artificial Analysis’ updated Coding Agent Index puts Claude Fable 5.2 and Opus 5.5 configurations jointly at 58. The revision introduces harder FrontierCode tasks and updated Terminal-Bench verifiers; scores fall by five points on average. These are agent-and-harness results, not isolated model rankings. Reported costs among configurations scoring at least 48 range from $1.51 to $44.22 per task.

Cloudflare Reports Rapid Growth in AI-Orchestrated Intrusions

Cloudflare reports seeing more than 50 AI penetration-testing framework families scan its customers over three days, with tracked daily requests up more than thirtyfold since October 1. Its incident responders describe stolen credentials being used to move between cloud environments. The observations concern attacks on exposed infrastructure, not models escaping containment, and come from Cloudflare’s own network and investigations.

Checkpoint Forensics Finds Blackwell Fingerprints in Chinese AI Models

Unemployment Labs reports numerical fingerprints matching Nvidia Blackwell hardware in six open models from DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax and Ant Group. The method identifies how weights were initialized and supports, but does not prove, an inference about training hardware. The researchers explicitly say it cannot establish chip location, the means of access, or whether export controls were violated.
