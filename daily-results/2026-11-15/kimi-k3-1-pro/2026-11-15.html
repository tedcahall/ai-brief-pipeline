🌐 TOP STORIES

Thinking Machines Releases 594B Inkling 2 Small Open Weights

Thinking Machines has released open weights for Inkling 2 Small, a multimodal mixture-of-experts model with 594B total and 17B active parameters, a one-million-token context window, and text and image input. The model card says NVFP4 is its native precision and lists local-inference recipes and third-party providers. NVIDIA said the model was pretrained in 68 hours on GB300 NVL72 racks.

Perplexity Launches Open-Weight Decider v1.3 Models With Text Extraction

Perplexity released Decider v1.3 and the distilled Decider Flash v1.3 under an Apache license on Hugging Face, OpenRouter, and its Decisions API. The 27B and 4B models add locate, which returns exact text spans and character positions for extraction tasks. Perplexity said bidirectional attention, isolated question handling, curated training data, and a revised training recipe improve robustness across text and vision tasks.

Coding Agent Index v2.1 Raises the Bar for Repository Patches

Artificial Analysis updated its Coding Agent Index with FrontierCode-AA 2.0 and Terminal-Bench 4.1, reporting Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Opus 5.5 configurations tied at 58. The mean FrontierCode score fell from 45% to 29% across 23 comparable configurations under the stricter merge-focused test. Estimated costs among configurations scoring at least 48 varied roughly 30-fold, making reasoning settings and harness behavior material to comparisons.

Weight Analysis Finds Signs Chinese Models Used Restricted NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs

TechCrunch reported that Unemployment Labs researcher Will DePue found signs in released model weights that DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax, and Ant Group models were trained using NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs. The analysis does not establish that training occurred entirely on NVIDIA hardware or that export laws were violated. Offshore data centers remain a possible explanation, complicating enforcement aimed at Chinese laboratories’ access to advanced compute.

Goldman Sachs Estimates AI-Related Debt Issuance at $687 Billion

Goldman Sachs estimated global AI-related debt issuance at $687 billion year-to-date as of November 3, according to The Economic Times. Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, and Oracle accounted for $296 billion, alongside $1.7 trillion in lease commitments and $1.9 trillion in other unconditional purchase commitments. The bank warned that market saturation could constrain further borrowing and that monetization evidence by 2028 would matter for credit ratings.
