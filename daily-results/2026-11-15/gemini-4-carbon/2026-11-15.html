🌐 TOP STORIES

Thinking Machines Lab Releases Open-Weight Inkling 2 Small Trained End-to-End in NVFP4

Thinking Machines Lab released open weights for Inkling 2 Small, a 594-billion-parameter multimodal Mixture-of-Experts model with 17 billion active parameters and a 1-million-token context window. By combining native NVFP4 expert forward passes with stochastic-rounded MXFP8 backward passes on Nvidia GB300 NVL72 racks, the lab reported pretraining in 68 hours, delivering an 8% pretraining speedup and a 20% reinforcement-learning speedup over MXFP8.

OpenAI Disbands Standalone Robotics Team as General-Purpose Models Take On Robot Control

OpenAI confirmed to Fortune that it dissolved its standalone robotics unit, reassigning robotics researchers to Chief Research Officer Mark Chen's core research group and moving applied hardware staff under President Greg Brockman. OpenAI said general-purpose frontier models such as GPT-6 Astra now transfer directly to robotic manipulation, citing a simulated Nvidia benchmark where an Astra agent reached 74.8% success versus 51.0% for specialized models.

Cloudflare Reports Over 50 AI Penetration-Testing Frameworks Scanning Customer Infrastructure

Cloudflare reported tracking more than 50 autonomous LLM penetration-testing framework families scanning customer networks globally over three days, with daily AI exploitation request volume up over 30-fold since October 1. Although its creator removed the open-source ARTEX framework on October 8, over 12,000 repositories and 204 modified public forks have proliferated, fueling incidents involving stolen CI/CD tokens and automated lateral movement.

Weight Forensics Links Leading Chinese Open-Weight Models to Restricted Nvidia Blackwell Chips

Checkpoint-forensics research led by former OpenAI researcher Will DePue at Unemployment Labs found structural weight signatures indicating open models from DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax, and Ant Group, including Kimi K3 and DeepSeek V4 releases, were trained on export-restricted Nvidia Blackwell GPUs. TechCrunch noted the findings do not prove export violations because training could occur in offshore hubs like Malaysia, though scrutiny is intensifying.

Claude Helps Logician Extract 663 Theorems From Kurt Gödel's Shorthand Notebooks

University of Helsinki logician Jan von Plato used Anthropic's Claude to analyze transcriptions of 1,009 pages of 1940s Gabelsberger-shorthand notebooks by Kurt Gödel after nine years of slow manual decoding. Claude produced a 650-page digest in days and extracted 663 theorem candidates; when checked in the Coq proof assistant, roughly 400 verified as stated, about 200 held after minor repairs, and around 50 proved false.
