🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic Rebuilt Its Messages API in Rust, Cutting CPU Use 70%

Anthropic migrated the Messages API, which serves customer traffic as well as Claude Code and Cowork, from Python to Rust. The company reports about 70% less CPU per request and a 20% reduction in median time to first token, while moving production traffic without downtime or a pause in feature work. Claude agents helped compare the new service against the changing production implementation and resolve mismatches.

OpenAI Tests Confidential-Computing Protections for Private Inference

OpenAI president Sam Altman said the company is testing Private Inference with a small group of customers as part of OpenAI Private Intelligence. He described stronger privacy protections for frontier-model use, while Greg Brockman specified strict controls over prompts and responses during processing. OpenAI also says zero data retention with Private Safety Processing can support automated safety review without staff access to the underlying content.

House Introduces Bill to Track AI-Automated Research and Development

Representatives Josh Gottheimer and Nathaniel Moran introduced the bipartisan RSI Safety Act, H.R. 11157, on November 24. The bill would require large AI developers to disclose quarterly how AI is used in data preparation, model design, experimentation, and safety work, and to notify officials of material changes in automated research. It was referred to the House Science, Space, and Technology Committee and has not become law.

Artificial Analysis Opens a Simulated Humanoid Benchmark to Language Models

Artificial Analysis released AA-Robotics-Sim v0.1, a research preview in which language models control a simulated Unitree G1 across ten tasks. Each model receives five practice episodes, then runs ten new scenes with changed layouts and conditions. The publisher reports wide performance variation and notes that episodes take 17 to 39 minutes even though the robot moves for less than a minute; these are simulator results, not real-world robot trials.

Alibaba Expands Qwen-Audio-3.1 Support Across Languages and Chinese Dialects

Alibaba’s Qwen team announced expanded multilingual capabilities for the Qwen-Audio-3.1 speech-model series. Its post lists automatic speech recognition across 59 languages and eight Chinese dialects, text-to-speech across 65 languages and six dialects, and real-time speech across 53 languages and six dialects. The team said the updated ASR, TTS, and real-time variants were available; the figures are the developer’s own specifications.
