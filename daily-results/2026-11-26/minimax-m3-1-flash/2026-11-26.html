📊 AI INDUSTRY PULSE

OpenAI has begun testing Private Inference, a confidential-computing offering for business customers that keeps prompts and responses out of OpenAI's hands while a model processes them, and Anthropic says it rewrote its Messages API in Rust to use about 70% less CPU per request after API traffic grew roughly 1,800 times since early 2024. On capability, SemiAnalysis' GDPx benchmark places OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra first at 19.3%, ahead of Claude Fable 5.2 at 16.4%, with Gemini 4 Neon sixth.

Infrastructure competition tightened, as SemiAnalysis added AWS Trainium3 and Google TPU systems to its InferenceX/AgentX agentic inference benchmark and reported 1.2x to 2.5x the performance per dollar of NVIDIA GPUs, while Alibaba's Qwen team shipped a multilingual upgrade to Qwen-Audio-3.1 that takes speech recognition to 59 languages and 8 Chinese dialects.

Independent evaluators also published two new benchmarks in the window: Artificial Analysis' FrontierPhysics leaderboard, built on 54 physicist-written research tasks where Claude Fable 5.2 leads at 32% and Gemini 4 Neon passes numeric checks in 54% of runs but only 28% of runs survive reviewer scrutiny, and AA-Robotics-Sim v0.1, where frontier models drive a simulated Unitree G1 humanoid and GPT-6.2 Astra leads at 71.0 points.

🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI Starts Testing Private Inference for Business Customers

Sam Altman said OpenAI is testing Private Inference, an offering in its Private Intelligence line that combines confidential computing with strict controls so customer prompts and responses stay protected while a model processes them, initially with a small group of customers. OpenAI's Private Intelligence page sets strict limits on access to business content and offers ZDR and Private Inference for eligible workflows, and Altman said automated safety reviews can run without giving OpenAI personnel access to the underlying content.

Anthropic Rewrites Its Messages API in Rust

Anthropic says it migrated its Messages API from Python to Rust, using about 70% less CPU per request and cutting median time to first token by about 20% without taking the service offline or freezing feature development. The API is the path used by Claude Code and Cowork, and Anthropic says traffic grew roughly 1,800 times from early 2024 to fall 2026. To build trust, Anthropic used the live Python service as a verifier and dispatched Claude agents to find and fix differences found on production traffic.

OpenAI's Astra Tops SemiAnalysis' GDPx Leaderboard

SemiAnalysis' GDPx benchmark, launched November 1 with 206 tasks built with 14 data partners across ten domains, now places OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra first at 19.3%, ahead of Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2 at 16.4%, with Gemini 4 Carbon fifth at 10.9% and Gemini 4 Neon sixth at 10.1%. The firm says safety classifiers depress some overall scores, and that OpenAI models dominate the cost-performance frontier, with DeepSeek v4.1 Flash appearing only at the low end.

Alibaba Extends Qwen-Audio Speech Models to Chinese Dialects

Alibaba's Qwen team said its Qwen-Audio-3.1 speech foundation models gained broader multilingual coverage, with speech recognition now covering 59 languages and 8 Chinese dialects, text to speech 65 languages and 6 dialects, and realtime speech 53 languages and 6 dialects. The vendor model page for the recognition endpoint documents multilingual and multi-regional Chinese dialect recognition at $0.15 per million input tokens and $0.47 per million output tokens. Alibaba published no benchmarks with the update.

Amazon's Trainium3 and Google's TPU Beat Nvidia GPUs on Cost

SemiAnalysis announced that AWS Trainium3 and Google TPU systems are now benchmarked on its InferenceX/AgentX agentic inference benchmark, seven months after Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang said they would never appear there. The firm reports 1.2x to 2.5x the performance per dollar of NVIDIA GPUs, and said earlier on November 25 that Amazon's first Trainium3 UltraServer submission reached 2.28x against GB300 NVL72, B300 and MI355. Amazon attributes its result to a wide expert-parallel approach for external customers.
