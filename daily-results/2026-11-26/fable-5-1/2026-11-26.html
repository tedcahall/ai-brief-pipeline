🌐 TOP STORIES

Google's Cogentic Agent Posts Six AI-Written Math Papers, Including a Factoring Bound

Google Research added six papers to its public GitHub repository on November 25, all credited to Cogentic, a Gemini 4-based agentic framework for mathematical discovery, with no human interaction beyond the initial prompt. The lead paper claims an unconditional integer factoring algorithm running in expected time L_N[1/3, O(1)]. Google says Lean 4 formalizations are complete but not yet released, and none of the papers has been peer reviewed.

Anthropic Moves Its Messages API From Python to Rust, Verified by Claude Agents

Anthropic said its Messages API, the main path for customers and its own Claude Code and Cowork apps, now runs in Rust instead of Python after traffic grew roughly 1,800 times since early 2024. The post describes turning the production Python API into a verifier on live traffic, with Claude agents dispatched to fix differences. Anthropic reports about 70% less CPU per request and roughly 20% lower median time to first token, with no downtime.

OpenAI Begins Testing Private Inference With a Small Group of Customers

Sam Altman said OpenAI is testing Private Inference, part of its Private Intelligence offering for businesses, with a small group of customers. OpenAI's product page labels the feature an alpha and says it uses confidential computing with strict controls to protect prompts and responses while a model processes them. It joins Zero Data Retention with Private Safety Processing, which began rolling out in September. No general availability date was given.

SemiAnalysis Says Amazon's Trainium3 Posts 2.28x Performance per Dollar on Its AgentX Benchmark

SemiAnalysis said Amazon submitted its first Trainium3 UltraServer results to the firm's AgentX agentic inference benchmark and showed 2.28x better performance per dollar than Nvidia's GB300 NVL72 and B300 and AMD's MI355, which it attributed to a wide expert-parallel serving approach. The figure is SemiAnalysis's benchmark claim rather than an AWS announcement, and independent reruns were not available at publication.

House Bill Would Require Frontier Labs to Report How AI Builds Their Next Models

Reps. Josh Gottheimer and Nathaniel Moran introduced H.R. 11157, which would require developers training models above compute thresholds and $100 million in annual training spend to report how much research and development is automated, how often humans review it, and how much AI accelerates new models. A new Commerce Department office would publish reports and propose restrictions for Congress, but the bill would not ban autonomous AI research.
