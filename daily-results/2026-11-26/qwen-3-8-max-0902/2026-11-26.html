🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI Starts Testing Private Inference for Business Customers

OpenAI is testing Private Inference with a small group of business customers, Sam Altman announced November 25. The alpha combines confidential computing on sealed chips with strict verifiable controls to protect prompts and responses during processing, and joins Private Safety Processing in the OpenAI Private Intelligence package. OpenAI says a larger rollout depends on securing more computing capacity.

Anthropic Migrated Its Messages API From Python to Rust Using Claude Agents

Anthropic published details of its Messages API migration from Python to Rust, which it verified on live production traffic without downtime. Teams of Claude agents compared both versions on real requests, triaged mismatch fingerprints and shipped fixes, taking production inputs from roughly a quarter matching to 99.6%. The Rust API uses about 70% less CPU per request and cuts median time to first token about 20%.

OpenAI Admits GPT-6.2 Launch Billing Mistake; Paid Limits Will Arrive Faster

New developments: Days after OpenAI brought chat-tuned GPT-6.2 Sol to ChatGPT, the company's Thibault Sottiaux admitted a billing mistake at launch incorrectly discounted Astra and Sol usage for most users. With the fix, limits arrive about 40% sooner on Astra and 25% sooner on Sol. Past usage will not be charged, and every Go, Plus, Pro and Business account receives one banked reset.

Amazon Trainium3 Claims 2.28x Performance per Dollar in SemiAnalysis Benchmark

SemiAnalysis reported Amazon's first Trainium3 UltraServer submission to its Agentic Inference Benchmark, claiming 2.28x better performance per dollar than NVIDIA GB300 NVL72, B300 and AMD MI355 thanks to a wide expert-parallelism approach. The same day SemiAnalysis announced TPU and Trainium joined InferenceX/AgentX, delivering 1.2x to 2.5x per-dollar results versus NVIDIA GPUs. Results are vendor-submitted.

Alibaba Expands Qwen-Audio-3.1 to Dozens of Languages and Chinese Dialects

Alibaba's Qwen team shipped a multilingual upgrade to its Qwen-Audio-3.1 speech foundation models with a strong focus on Chinese dialects. ASR now covers 59 languages and 8 Chinese dialects, TTS covers 65 languages and 6 dialects, and the Realtime model covers 53 languages and 6 dialects. All three variants are available now; no independent benchmarks were published.
