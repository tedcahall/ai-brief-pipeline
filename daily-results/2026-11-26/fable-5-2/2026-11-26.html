🌐 TOP STORIES

Google's Cogentic Posts Six AI-Generated Math Papers, Including a Proven Factoring Bound

Google Research posted six papers to its public GitHub repository on November 25 that it says were produced by Cogentic, a multi-agent system running on Gemini 4. The lead paper claims an unconditional integer factoring algorithm in the L[1/3] class; OfficeChai notes its large constant makes it a theory result, not a faster way to break encryption. None is peer reviewed, and the promised Lean 4 formalizations are unreleased.

OpenAI Begins Testing Private Inference With Enterprise Customers

Sam Altman said OpenAI is testing Private Inference, part of its OpenAI Private Intelligence offering, with a small group of customers. According to The Decoder, queries run on isolated hardware whose security can be independently verified, so OpenAI cannot access the data. Altman also pointed to Private Safety Processing, which enables automated safety reviews without giving OpenAI personnel access to the underlying content.

House Bill Would Require Labs to Report How Much AI Builds Its Successors

Reps. Josh Gottheimer, D-N.J., and Nathaniel Moran, R-Texas, introduced a bill requiring the largest AI developers to report how much research and development is done autonomously, how often humans review it and how much AI accelerates new models. It covers companies spending $100 million a year on training, creates a Commerce Department office and gives the agency no authority to halt development, The National News Desk reported.

Nvidia Halts AI Server Shipments to Four Southeast Asian Countries

Nvidia asked manufacturing partners to halt shipments of servers containing its AI chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, The National reported, citing the Financial Times. Dell and Supermicro told customers Nvidia will not authorise their orders, and it is unclear whether the suspension is temporary. The move follows new U.S. rules meant to stop Chinese companies from renting computing power from cloud providers that use Nvidia chips.

Researcher Says MiniMax Systems Opened Private Links in User Prompts

Dragos chief research officer HD Moore told Reuters that MiniMax's systems opened secret links embedded in more than half of the test prompts his team routed to the Chinese AI company, including about two-thirds of requests marked for no data collection, according to AI Weekly's account of the report. MiniMax said it found no evidence that prompt content was disclosed to third parties. OpenRouter removed three providers.
