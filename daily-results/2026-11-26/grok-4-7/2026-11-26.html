🌐 TOP STORIES

Nvidia Told Partners to Stop Shipping AI Servers to Four Southeast Asian Countries

Reuters, citing the Financial Times, reported Thursday that Nvidia told manufacturing partners to stop shipping servers containing its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia. The report tied the halt to new US export-license rules for advanced chips sent to several Southeast Asian countries. Dell and Supermicro told buyers those orders would not ship, the FT said. Nvidia, Dell and Supermicro had not commented, and it was unclear whether the halt would be temporary.

OpenAI Starts Testing Private Inference for Business Customers

OpenAI has started testing Private Inference with a small group of business customers, CEO Sam Altman said on Wednesday. The Next Web reported that the alpha uses confidential computing so prompts and replies stay on sealed-off chips while a model runs. OpenAI's product page describes strict verifiable controls. It is the second part of the Private Intelligence package, after Private Safety Processing.

Sand AI Launches 480B-Parameter MAGI-2 Video Model

Sand AI said it launched MAGI-2, scaling its video model from about 114 billion parameters in an August preview to 480 billion total and about 28 billion active. The company said it generates up to 30 seconds of 1080p, 25 fps video with native audio, and that open-source releases and a technical report will follow. Artificial Analysis separately ranked MAGI-2 first on its image-to-video board, tied with Dreamina Seedance 2.5.

Alibaba Expands Qwen-Audio-3.1 Across Languages and Chinese Dialects

Alibaba's Qwen team said Thursday that Qwen-Audio-3.1 now covers more languages, with a focus on Chinese dialects. The team said speech recognition covers 59 languages and 8 Chinese dialects, speech synthesis covers 65 languages and 6 dialects, and realtime covers 53 languages and 6 dialects. Linked pages list the ASR, TTS and realtime flash variants as available. The coverage counts are the company's figures.

Japan Weighs AI Act Safety Duties and Penalties for Frontier Providers

Japan is reviewing revisions to its AI Act that would require large AI providers to put safety measures in place, the Yomiuri Shimbun reported, according to Seoul Economic Daily. Measures under review include blocking answers on cyberattack methods and harmful substances, checking users of highly cyber-capable models, and reporting flaws that could be exploited. Penalties would apply only after a firm ignores a recommendation and then a corrective order. Specific penalties are not set.
