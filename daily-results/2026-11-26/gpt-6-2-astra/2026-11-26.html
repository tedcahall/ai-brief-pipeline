🌐 TOP STORIES

Qwen expands voice coverage to 65 languages for synthesis and 59 for transcription

Alibaba says the three Qwen-Audio-3.1 services now cover different sets of languages. Speech recognition supports 59 languages and eight Chinese dialects; speech synthesis supports 65 languages and six dialects. The real-time service covers 53 languages and six dialects. Developers building a two-way voice interface should use the real-time figure, not the synthesis count.

Sand AI ties for first in an image-to-video test at $4.80 per generated minute

Artificial Analysis found MAGI-2 statistically tied with Dreamina Seedance 2.5 on its image-to-video leaderboard. At the prices it tested, a minute of output cost $4.80 for MAGI-2 versus $34.12 for Seedance. The ranking does not extend to every video task: MAGI-2 placed seventh when given text rather than a starting image.

OpenAI corrects an undercount of GPT-6.2 plan usage

New developments: OpenAI’s Thibault Sottiaux said most users had received an unintended usage discount on Astra and Sol. Compared with last week, users doing the same work will reach their limits 40% faster on Astra and 25% faster on Sol, he said. Past usage will not be billed retroactively, and four subscription tiers will receive a banked reset.

Amazon submits its first Trainium3 results to an agent inference benchmark

SemiAnalysis reported that Amazon’s Trainium3 UltraServer submission delivered 2.28 times the performance per dollar of the competing accelerator systems it cited in its agentic inference test. The result concerns the benchmark’s multi-turn workload and its cost assumptions. It does not establish that the same advantage applies to training or to every inference deployment.

Claude Fable leads a physics benchmark that checks more than the final number

Artificial Analysis ran 54 physicist-written tasks that require a model to investigate a question and submit a manuscript. Claude Fable 5.2 led with 32%, followed by GPT-6.2 Astra at 26%. Gemini passed the most numerical checks, but reviewers rejected nearly half those runs for flaws such as an invalid simulation or an unsupported conclusion.
