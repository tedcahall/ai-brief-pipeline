🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic Migrates Messages API From Python to Rust Using Claude Agents

Anthropic reported that it migrated its high-traffic Messages API from Python to Rust without downtime after API volume grew roughly 1,800 times between early 2024 and fall 2026. Using the live Python service as a parity verifier and dispatching Claude agents to resolve divergences, Anthropic cut CPU usage per request by about 70% and lowered median time to first token by roughly 20%.

OpenAI Begins Alpha Testing Private Inference Under Private Intelligence

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman announced that the company has started alpha testing Private Inference with a small group of business customers under its OpenAI Private Intelligence framework. According to OpenAI documentation and i10x reporting, the feature uses confidential computing hardware enclaves to protect prompts and responses during processing, though OpenAI's own systems still verify enclave attestation rather than customer software.

Amazon Trainium3 and Google TPU Beat Nvidia GPUs on SemiAnalysis AgentX Benchmark

SemiAnalysis announced that both Amazon's Trainium and Google's TPU are now listed on its InferenceX and agentic AgentX benchmarks, seven months after Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang said neither would appear. In an official-preview submission running Z.ai's 744B-parameter GLM-5.2/GLM-5.3 on vLLM in FP4, Amazon's Trainium3 UltraServer delivered up to 2.28x better performance per dollar than Nvidia's GB300 NVL72, B300, and AMD's MI355X.

Bipartisan House Bill Would Require AI Labs to Report on AI-Built Models

Representatives Josh Gottheimer and Nathaniel Moran introduced the bipartisan RSI Safety Act on November 24 to track recursive self-improvement at frontier labs. The bill would require developers spending at least $100 million annually on training above a defined compute threshold to file quarterly reports on AI involvement and human oversight with a new Commerce Department office, backed by civil penalties up to $10 million.

Japan Weighs Mandatory AI Safety Duties and Penalties for Global Model Providers

Japan's government is preparing revisions to its AI Act that would require major providers including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Alibaba Group, and DeepSeek to adopt binding safety controls, Yomiuri Shimbun reported. The draft rules would mandate blocking cyberattack and harmful-substance instructions, reporting exploitable flaws, and verifying user attributes for high-capability cyber tools, backed by corrective orders and financial penalties.
