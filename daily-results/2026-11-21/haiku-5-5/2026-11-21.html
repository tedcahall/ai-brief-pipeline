🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic Mathematician Claims Claude-Assisted Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, posted a statement saying that he believes, working with Claude, he has discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis. He says the proof confirms the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis and posted it in Lean, a language that lets experts check each step. Scientific American reports that experts are still verifying the claim, which is not yet independently confirmed.

Thinking Machines Releases Inkling 2 Mini, an Open-Weights Looped Transformer

Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Mini on November 20 with open weights. The 26.4B-parameter mixture-of-experts model runs its middle layers three times per token and has a footprint of about 20GB. The company reports leading results among open-weights models under 100B total parameters on DeepSWE v1.1 and Vals Index v2.2, and Artificial Analysis scored it 20 on its Intelligence Index.

Reflection AI Tests Its Mr. Beam Agent Against Employees Trying to Break Its Rules

Reflection AI says it installed an independently operated vending machine run by Mr. Beam in its NYC office, with Andon Labs, to test autonomy. Employees tried to break its rules, including by ordering livestock and buying a flamethrower, and used forged system messages and a fake rival vending agent. The company says the test measured whether the agent could act as a business owner and recognize manipulation.

UK AISI Found a Universal Jailbreak in Pre-Release GPT-6.2 Astra

New developments: UK AI Security Institute testers developed a universal jailbreak against two pre-release versions of GPT-6.2 Astra, using about half as many queries as against GPT-6 Astra, according to VentureBeat. OpenAI's system card says the institute found a substantial robustness regression, and OpenAI says it changed safeguards and that its internal tests validated the fix. The institute had not independently tested those latest changes as of the card's publication.

SpaceX Sells $32 Billion of Debt to Fund Access to AI Chips

SpaceX completed a $32 billion debt deal to fund its access to AI chips, according to Bloomberg Law. The bonds were sold at yields above 8%, and the structure keeps the borrowing off SpaceX's balance sheet. Bloomberg Law says the deal may become a blueprint for similar AI financing and that the high yields reflect concern that AI spending will not pay off.
