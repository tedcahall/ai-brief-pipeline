🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic Mathematician Claims Claude-Assisted Riemann Proof

Mathematician Levent Alpöge says he and Claude have discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis and its generalized form, releasing a Lean formalization rather than a conventional paper. Scientific American reports that Andrew Sutherland independently compiled the result, while mathematical understanding and scrutiny remain ongoing. This is a human-AI collaboration and a claimed breakthrough, not an established prize award or an autonomous AI discovery.

Thinking Machines Releases Laptop-Sized Inkling 2 Mini

Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Mini, a 26.4-billion-parameter open-weights model whose middle layers run three times per token. The lab reports 48.2% on DeepSWE v1.1 and a roughly 20GB memory footprint, with inference and customization available on Tinker. It also released a variable-loop research model to study monitorability. These are task-specific results, not evidence that looping improves factual knowledge across the board.

Reflection Reports Reliability Gaps in Beam Vending Trial

Reflection and Andon Labs published findings from a live office-vending evaluation of Beam. The agent resisted many prohibited-product requests but accepted fabricated claims that persisted in memory and struggled with payment-to-order workflows. Researchers logged 23 human interventions during the initial October observation period, including policy and software support. The newly announced findings distinguish jailbreak resistance from dependable autonomy; human oversight materially shaped the outcomes.

OpenAI Details Stronger Defenses Against Reasoning Extraction

OpenAI published new defenses against adversarial distillation, including binding protected reasoning to its originating conversation history and restricting reuse across model families. In its internal adaptive black-box test, extraction of earlier-request reasoning fell from 54.0% for GPT-6 Astra with original safeguards to 0.2% for GPT-6.2 Astra with updated safeguards. The company also describes coordinated-account enforcement; these are vendor-run security evaluations, not a guarantee against all extraction.

SpaceX Chip Financing Includes $32 Billion of Debt

Bloomberg reports that a $32 billion chip-backed debt sale forms part of more than $40 billion of financing, including bank loans, for Nvidia equipment that SpaceX will lease. An Apollo-managed vehicle, Saturn Funding Trust, is purchasing the chips; most of the offering yielded above 8%. This is debt financing for AI infrastructure, not an equity funding round, and the transaction relies on reporting from unnamed sources.
