🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic Mathematician Claims Claude-Assisted Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, said Friday that he found a proof of the Riemann hypothesis and its generalized form working with Claude, and posted it as Lean code. Scientific American reports that MIT's Andrew Sutherland and others independently compiled the proof and rechecked it with the Lean kernel. A final comparator run was still pending, and Terence Tao wrote that digesting the proof will take some time.

Thinking Machines Releases Inkling 2 Mini, an Open-Weights Looped Transformer

Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Mini, a mixture-of-experts model with 26.4B total and 3.4B active parameters that runs its middle layers three times per token. The lab reports 48.2% on DeepSWE v1.1, which it says leads open-weights models under 100B total parameters, and a memory footprint of about 20GB. Artificial Analysis scored it 20 on its Intelligence Index and said it carries an Apache 2.0 license.

OpenAI Details Defenses Against Adversarial Distillation

OpenAI published research on how it detects and disrupts unauthorized distillation of its models. In an internal automated red-teaming evaluation, the success rate of attacks recovering protected reasoning from a prior request fell from 54.0% on GPT-6 Astra to 0.2% on GPT-6.2 Astra. In an internal study, student models trained on exact reasoning traces generally outperformed those trained on reasoning summaries on cyber tasks after supervised fine-tuning.

Anthropic Falls 8.5% as Nvidia and AMD Drop More Than 13%

New developments: Anthropic shares fell 8.5% on Friday, their first full day of trading, after closing Thursday at $97.17 against an $83 IPO price, CNBC reported. Nvidia and AMD each fell more than 13%, and Amazon, Google and Microsoft also closed lower. Separately, Reuters reported that the SEC is examining potential accounting issues at Nvidia, citing two people briefed on the matter.

SpaceX Sells $32 Billion of Debt to Fund AI Chips

SpaceX sold $32 billion of debt on Thursday to fund Nvidia chip purchases through a special-purpose vehicle, the Saturn Funding Trust, managed by an Apollo entity, Bloomberg reported. About $25.2 billion of Class A3 notes priced at a yield of 8.259%, above the 5.95% average for US high-grade bonds. The deal is part of a financing of more than $40 billion, and SpaceX will lease the equipment for about five years.
