📊 AI INDUSTRY PULSE

Thinking Machines released a model that runs its middle layers three times for each token yet fits on a laptop. At the other end of the compute market, SpaceX closed a $32 billion chip-access financing deal, with most of the debt yielding more than 8%.

Two new evaluations put model scores in context: OpenAI asked models to reason from medicinal chemistry experiments, while Reflection tested whether a vending agent would stick to purchasing rules under pressure from its own customers.

🌐 TOP STORIES

Thinking Machines releases Inkling 2 Mini, a looped model that fits on a laptop

Inkling 2 Mini runs its middle layers three times per token. Thinking Machines says the model has 26.4 billion parameters but needs about 20 GB of memory, putting it within reach of some laptops. The lab also released a research variant that lets researchers vary the number of passes and measure how that changes reasoning.

SpaceX closes $32 billion debt deal for AI chip access

Bloomberg reports that SpaceX closed a $32 billion debt transaction to fund access to AI chips. Most of the offering sold at yields above 8%. The structure was designed to keep the borrowing off the company’s balance sheet: this was financing for compute, not an equity investment in a model developer.

OpenAI tests whether models can choose promising drug candidates

MedChemBench asks models to interpret molecular structures and experimental results across 99 problems drawn from published research. OpenAI reports that GPT-6.2 Astra earned 84.5% of the available credit at its highest tested reasoning setting. The score measures performance on past research problems, not success in developing a new medicine.

Reflection tests an autonomous vending agent in its New York office

Reflection and Andon Labs installed a vending machine run by an agent called Mr. Beam. Employees tried to persuade it to break its purchasing rules. Reflection says the agent resisted a range of attempts, but fabricated claims influenced later decisions and multistep tasks proved less reliable. This was a controlled workplace experiment, not a reported containment escape.

Perplexity brings Computer into Chromium browser tabs

Perplexity says users of its Chromium extension can now hand tasks to Computer directly from a browser side panel. The agent works across tabs and tools while the user watches. The announcement demonstrates a change in where people can use the agent; it does not establish how reliably it completes those tasks.
