🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic Mathematician Claims Claude-Assisted Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

Levent Alpoge, a mathematician at Anthropic, said on November 20 that he discovered a proof of the Riemann hypothesis with Claude and released a Lean version of it. The claim has not been independently verified or reviewed by outside experts. Riemann specialist Brian Conrey said he had not seen a manuscript, and Stanford's Ravi Vakil said a postdoc in his department had separately submitted a proof.

OpenAI Publishes Research on Combating Adversarial Distillation

OpenAI published research on detecting and disrupting adversarial distillation, in which another model is trained on a frontier model's outputs without inheriting its safeguards. The company says it strengthened protections for protected reasoning in GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol, and that the average success rate of adaptive attacks fell from 54.0% against GPT-6 Astra with original safeguards to 0.2% against GPT-6.2 Astra with updated safeguards.

Thinking Machines Releases a Small Open-Weight Inkling Model

Thinking Machines Lab released Inkling 2 Mini, a looped mixture-of-experts model with 26.4B total parameters and 3.4B active, under an Apache 2.0 license with open weights. The company says running the middle layers three times per token lets the model reason more without writing its reasoning out, and that it leads open-weight models below 100B total parameters on DeepSWE v1.1 at 48.2%. Artificial Analysis scored it 20 on its Intelligence Index.

SpaceX Raises $32 Billion in AI Chip Debt

SpaceX sold $32 billion of bonds through a trust that buys Nvidia chips and leases them to the company, with the largest piece, about $25.2 billion, priced at a yield of 8.259% against a 5.95% average for high-grade US corporate debt that day. The deal is part of a financing package above $40 billion, and the chips are described as a terrestrial compute build-out for SpaceX's AI business.

Alibaba Trains Its Next Qwen Model on Its Own Chips

Alibaba said it is training its next-generation Qwen model on chips from its semiconductor subsidiary T-Head, according to chief executive Eddie Wu on the company's quarterly earnings call. Alibaba's Bailian model platform also provides inference services using Chinese-made chips. Wu said coordinating the company's own chips with its models would improve computing costs and give it an advantage in China's domestic supply chain.
