🌐 TOP STORIES

Independent Mathematicians Say Claude-Assisted Riemann Proof Holds Up

Levent Alpoge, a mathematician at Anthropic, posted a claimed proof of the Riemann hypothesis and its generalized form on Nov. 20, developed with Claude and released as a Lean 4 archive. Scientific American reported that independent researchers, including MIT's Andrew Sutherland, compiled the code with only the three standard axioms; Sutherland put the chance anything is fishy near zero. Buzzard of Imperial College said a comparator run is still pending.

Thinking Machines Releases 26B Inkling 2 Mini With Open Weights

Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Mini, a 26.4B-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 3.4B active parameters that loops the middle 12 of its 24 layers three times per token. The company reports 48.2% on DeepSWE v1.1 and 20.1 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, claiming leadership among open-weights models under 100B total parameters. It ships under Apache 2.0 at roughly 20GB. The benchmark claim is the lab's own and not independently replicated.

OpenAI Introduces MedChemBench for Drug-Discovery Reasoning

OpenAI published MedChemBench, a 99-problem benchmark drawn from 117 papers and patents covering 761 molecular structures, grading structure reconstruction, scientific reasoning, compound ranking and property prediction. At the xhigh reasoning setting, GPT-6.2 Astra earned 84.5% of available credit versus 83.3% for GPT-6 Astra and 58.2% for GPT-5.6 Sol. OpenAI says the scores do not establish that models can discover drugs, and the benchmark is vendor-run.

Nvidia Nears $500 Million-Plus Deal for Diffusion Lab Inception

The Information reported that Nvidia is in advanced talks to acquire Inception, a two-year-old Bay Area startup with more than 30 employees, for at least $500 million plus performance bonuses. Inception builds diffusion-based language models that refine a whole response step by step rather than token by token, most recently Mercury 3. The reported rationale is strengthening Nvidia's open Nemotron models and robotics work. Neither company commented initially and the deal is unconfirmed.

California Proposes Emergency Shutdown Power Over Dangerous AI

Governor Gavin Newsom released expert recommendations on Nov. 18 to give California officials emergency shutdown authority over AI systems they deem dangerous, set state baseline standards, and require independent safety testing through training, development and deployment. The plan also proposes licensed independent verification organizations, expanded incident reporting and authority to cap recursive self-improvement. These are recommendations, not enacted law, and legislators return Dec. 7. The proposal follows the state's Nov. 16 lawsuit against OpenAI.
