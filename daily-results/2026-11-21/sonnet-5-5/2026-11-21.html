🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic Mathematician Claims Claude-Assisted Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, posted a note on November 20 saying he found a proof of the Riemann hypothesis with Claude, which he says extends to the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. He published the proof in Lean. Scientific American reports that mathematician Andrew Sutherland independently compiled the file, but experts have not yet confirmed that the formal statement and argument are correct.

Thinking Machines Releases Open-Weights Inkling 2 Mini, a 26B Looped Model

Thinking Machines Lab released Inkling 2 Mini with open weights. It is a mixture-of-experts looped transformer with 26.4B total and 3.4B active parameters that runs its middle layers three times per token. The lab says it leads open-weights models under 100B parameters on DeepSWE v1.1 at 48.2% and fits in about 20GB of memory. Artificial Analysis scores it 20 on its Intelligence Index.

SpaceX Closes $32 Billion Chip-Backed Debt Deal at Yields Above 8%

Bloomberg reports that SpaceX sold $32 billion of debt on Thursday, November 19, as part of a financing of more than $40 billion that also includes bank loans. A special-purpose vehicle managed by an Apollo entity buys the Nvidia chips, which SpaceX leases for about five years. The $25.2 billion Class A3 tranche priced at a yield of 8.259%, against a 5.95% average US high-grade yield.

TSMC Halts Some Advanced Orders From Chinese Chip Designers After New US Rules

Reuters, citing two people familiar with the matter, reports that TSMC suspended some previously permitted advanced orders from Chinese chip designers. Orders at 16nm and below are halted unless customers obtain US export licenses, and affected designers are generally limited to 28nm and 40nm processes without HBM or advanced packaging. The move follows Commerce Department rules that took effect November 19 and removed a verification route.

Reflection AI Reports Mixed Results From Live Vending-Machine Test of Beam

Reflection AI and Andon Labs ran Beam as the operator of vending businesses in Reflection's New York and San Francisco offices, with employees trying to manipulate it. Reflection says Mr. Beam resisted direct jailbreaks and showed strong judgment, but was less reliable on multi-step workflows and was influenced by fabricated claims. This is a self-reported evaluation, and a third phase with adversarial participants has not started.
