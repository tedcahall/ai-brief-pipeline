🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic Mathematician Claims Claude-Assisted Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, posted a statement on Friday saying he believes that, working with Claude, he discovered a proof of the Riemann hypothesis, and that it extends to the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. He released the proof coded in Lean, which checks each step. Scientific American reports that experts still need to verify the formalization and the proof; the claim remains unconfirmed.

Thinking Machines Releases Inkling 2 Mini, a Laptop-Sized Open-Weights Model

Thinking Machines Lab released Inkling 2 Mini, a mixture-of-experts looped transformer with 26.4B total and 3.4B active parameters that runs its middle layers three times per token. The lab says it leads open-weights models under 100B parameters on DeepSWE v1.1 at 48.2%, needs about 20GB of memory, and showed no meaningful loss of chain-of-thought legibility in its tests.

OpenAI Details Its Defenses Against Adversarial Distillation

OpenAI published research on how it detects and disrupts unauthorized distillation, in which a model’s outputs are used to train another model that may lack comparable safeguards. OpenAI says it strengthened protections for reasoning traces in GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol; in an internal adaptive evaluation, attack success fell from 54.0% against GPT-6 Astra to 0.2% against GPT-6.2 Astra.

SEC Probe of Nvidia Examines Potential Accounting Issues, Reuters Reports

The US Securities and Exchange Commission is probing Nvidia’s accounting practices, according to two people briefed on the matter who spoke to Reuters. The SEC declined to comment, and Reuters could not determine which issues are under review. Reuters noted that investigations are not evidence of wrongdoing; the probe adds to pressure on Nvidia shares, which have fallen more than a third during the company’s leadership crisis.

SpaceX Sells $32 Billion of Debt to Fund Nvidia AI Chips

SpaceX sold $32 billion of bonds to fund AI chips, part of a financing of more than $40 billion, structured to keep the borrowing off its balance sheet. Goldman Sachs and Apollo ran the three-part sale. The chips will be bought through Saturn Funding Trust, an Apollo-managed vehicle, and leased to SpaceX for about five years. The largest tranche priced at an 8.259% yield.
