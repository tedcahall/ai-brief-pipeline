🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic Mathematician Claims Riemann Hypothesis Proof With Claude

Levent Alpöge, an Anthropic mathematician, said he worked with Claude to discover a proof of the Riemann hypothesis and released Lean-encoded proof artifacts. Scientific American reported that Andrew Sutherland independently compiled the result, while emphasizing that mathematicians still need to examine the details. The claim remains unverified, so it is not yet an accepted solution.

Thinking Machines Releases Laptop-Sized Inkling 2 Mini

Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Mini, an open-weight, multimodal looped transformer with 26.4 billion total parameters and a roughly 20GB memory footprint. Its middle 12 layers run three times per token, an approach intended to improve reasoning without more stored parameters. Artificial Analysis scored it 20 on its Intelligence Index and highlighted its efficiency for its size.

Apple Releases Depth Pro 2 for Photos, Video and Live Feeds

Apple released Depth Pro 2, which estimates metric depth at full input resolution from photos, videos and live camera feeds. Four variants range from 24 million to 872 million parameters, alongside a shared 23.5 million-parameter focal-length estimator. Apple says the multi-view transformer improves edge sharpness and frame-to-frame consistency. The weights are restricted to noncommercial research and academic development.

OpenAI Details Defenses Against Adversarial Model Distillation

OpenAI published research and enforcement methods for preventing unauthorized distillation of protected model reasoning. In its internal adaptive black-box evaluation, the average success rate for extracting reasoning from an earlier request fell from 54.0% with GPT-6 Astra's original safeguards to 0.2% with GPT-6.2 Astra's updated protections. These are company-reported results, not an independent evaluation.

SpaceX Sells $32 Billion of Debt to Fund AI Chips

SpaceX sold $32 billion of bonds to finance Nvidia chips through the Saturn Funding Trust, part of more than $40 billion in financing including bank loans. Most of the offering yielded above 8%, with $25.2 billion of Class A3 notes priced at 8.259%. The structure keeps borrowing off SpaceX's balance sheet, and the company will lease the chips for about five years.
