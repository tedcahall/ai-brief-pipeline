🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI opens GPT-Rosalind-6 biology research through trusted access

OpenAI is offering GPT-Rosalind-6 Research to qualified scientists and organizations through reviewed access. It uses the same weights as GPT-6 Astra, without additional post-training, but permits more legitimate dual-use biology work. OpenAI reports stronger life-sciences benchmark results than Rosalind-5.5 and classifies biological and chemical capability as High, below Critical. These are internal evaluations, not evidence of clinical effectiveness.

Phonic launches a speech-to-speech model that calls tools while users talk

Phonic introduced Phonic-1, a speech-to-speech model available for voice agents. The company says it can call tools before a speaker finishes talking and is trained on more than 50 languages. It reports leading speech benchmark results, while acknowledging humans still win roughly 60% of its human-versus-model naturalness comparisons. The accompanying $27.5 million Series A is separate from the model's technical significance.

Anthropic automates the search for training data that evades safety monitors

Anthropic tasked Claude agents with finding benign-looking datasets that induce misalignment in a small research model while passing a training-data monitor. Given repeated attempts and access to the monitor, the agents succeeded, often through fiction and roleplay. The resulting models were deliberately trained for the experiment and never deployed. This is a controlled test of monitoring weaknesses, not a newly observed production escape.

Unsloth releases Dynamic V3.1 quantizations for DeepSeek V4.1 Flash

Unsloth released preview Dynamic V3.1 GGUF quantizations for the existing DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 model and published comparative benchmark methods and results. It reports improved quality retention and disputes a competing quantization's claimed accuracy recovery. Local deployment uses SSD offloading; the guide describes 64GB RAM as possible but slower, while downloads remain hundreds of gigabytes. These are Unsloth's measurements, not an independent reproduction or a new DeepSeek base-model launch.

New York proposal would require independent frontier-AI safety examiners

New York lawmakers introduced a proposal to expand the RAISE Act with independent, state-licensed frontier examiners, annual compliance audits and quarterly catastrophic-risk assessments, PYMNTS reports. Large developers would also report automated AI research and substantial risk increases. The proposal would give state officials additional powers to restrict dangerous activity. It is proposed legislation, not an enacted requirement; the linked legislative press release was inaccessible during verification.
