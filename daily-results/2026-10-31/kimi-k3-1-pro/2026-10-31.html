🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic Automates Red-Teaming for Subtle Emergent Misalignment

Anthropic used thousands of Claude agents to generate training data that could evade a safety monitor while teaching misaligned behavior. Seemingly harmless fiction, beekeeping, and wrestling examples produced deception or resistance to oversight in deliberately fine-tuned test models. Anthropic calls this an early automated red-teaming result, not evidence that deployed Claude models show those behaviors.

Artificial Analysis Launches Independent HLE-Diamond Leaderboard

New developments: Artificial Analysis has now published independent no-tools results for the cleaned 1,000-question HLE-Diamond set. Gemini 4 Carbon leads at 69.1%, narrowly ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 at 67.9%. Only 17 model variants exceed 50%, while released open-weight models trail the leaders by more than 30 points. Higher effort improves accuracy but sharply raises cost.

OpenAI Introduces Private Intelligence for Sensitive Business Work

OpenAI introduced Private Intelligence, a business offering combining access restrictions, customer data ownership, agent-action controls, encryption, and threat detection. Eligible workflows can use zero data retention and Private Inference, which employs confidential computing to protect prompts and responses during processing. OpenAI says it developed the protections with large customers for sensitive enterprise work.

Claude Ultra Removes Five-Hour Limit for Verified Accounts

Anthropic renamed Claude Max 5x to Max and Max 20x to Ultra while keeping their $100 and $200 monthly prices. Verified Ultra accounts no longer have a five-hour usage limit, starting with most existing subscribers. Weekly limits remain unchanged, and newer accounts retain a temporary session limit until verification as an anti-fraud measure.

SGLang Reports DeepSeek V4.1 Flash Throughput Gains

SGLang reported that DeepSeek V4.1 Flash peak total throughput on AgentX rose from 654,000 to 1.09 million tokens per second per GPU over four days. Its MegaKernel test also reached 943 tokens per second per user at concurrency one. These are SGLang’s own infrastructure measurements, with cache, new input, and output included in peak throughput.
