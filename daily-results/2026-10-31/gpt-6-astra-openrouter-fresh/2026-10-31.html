🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic automates the search for training data that evades safety monitors

Anthropic tasked Claude agents with finding training datasets that could pass a safety monitor while making a separate research model misbehave. Fiction and roleplay helped conceal the targeted behaviors. The deliberately favorable red-team experiment exposed potential monitoring gaps, not a new escape by deployed Claude: the resulting models were never deployed, and attackers had access to the monitor they were trying to evade.

NVIDIA details Nemotron 3.5 Super and Lightning for agentic AI

NVIDIA’s technical report describes a 120B-parameter multimodal Super model with 12B active parameters and a 30B-parameter text-only Lightning model with 3B active. The authors report Super scoring 51.78% on SWE-bench Pro and serving up to 4,856 output tokens per second per GB200 GPU while sustaining at least 50 tokens per second per user. These are NVIDIA-reported results, not independent replication.

OpenAI outlines Private Intelligence controls for sensitive business work

OpenAI announced Private Intelligence for businesses, describing protections around data ownership, access restrictions, encryption and agent permissions. Its product page lists zero data retention and Private Inference for eligible workflows, alongside customer control over tools, actions and required approvals. The announcement does not establish that every protection is generally available to every customer; eligibility and deployment details require confirmation for a particular workflow.

New York proposal would require independent frontier-AI safety examinations

New York lawmakers proposed expanding the RAISE Act with licensed independent frontier examiners, annual compliance audits and catastrophic-risk assessments every 90 days, PYMNTS reports. Large developers would disclose substantial risk increases within 72 hours and report quarterly on automated AI research. The proposal would also grant authorities powers to restrict certain compute use or seek deployment limits; these are proposed requirements, not enacted rules.

Claude removes five-hour limits for verified Ultra subscribers

Anthropic renamed Max 5x to Max and Max 20x to Ultra, keeping monthly prices at $100 and $200 respectively. More materially, it is removing the five-hour limit for verified Ultra accounts, beginning with most existing subscribers. Newer accounts retain a temporary limit until verification, and weekly limits remain unchanged. This is a change to access conditions, not unlimited Claude usage.
