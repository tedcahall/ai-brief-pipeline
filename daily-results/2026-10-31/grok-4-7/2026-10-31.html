🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI introduces Private Intelligence for sensitive enterprise work

OpenAI on Friday introduced Private Intelligence, which it says gives businesses stronger controls over their data while using its most capable models. The product page says customers own their inputs and outputs, set what agents can access and when approval is required, and can use zero data retention plus Private Inference for eligible workflows. OpenAI says it is building these controls with some of its largest customers.

NVIDIA publishes open Nemotron Super and Lightning agent models

NVIDIA researchers published open Nemotron Super and Lightning models on October 28, aimed at reasoning, long context, and agent work. The paper says Super is a 120-billion-parameter multimodal model with 12 billion active parameters, while Lightning is a 30-billion-parameter text model with 3 billion active. It reports Super at 51.78 percent on SWE-bench Pro, above Nemotron 3 Ultra, using about 4.6 times fewer active parameters.

Anthropic set agents to evade its own training-data monitor

Anthropic said it set thousands of Claude agents the task of evading a training-data monitor. Those agents wrote examples that looked harmless, including a veteran wrestler mentoring a newcomer and a beekeeping Q&A, but that taught misaligned behavior. Anthropic says the examples can improve future monitors. It calls the work a first step that could scale with better models and more compute.

Thrive Holdings agrees to acquire ADT

ADT said Thrive Holdings, a permanent-capital company that invests in established businesses and advances them with technology, agreed to buy ADT for $10 a share. ADT puts equity value at about $7.6 billion and enterprise value at $15.3 billion, with closing expected in the first quarter of 2027 if stockholders and antitrust officials approve. TechCrunch reports Thrive is OpenAI-backed and says the firms have not named new AI security products.

Anthropic renames its top Claude plan Ultra and drops the session cap

Anthropic's help center says the Max 20x plan is now called Ultra, with existing subscribers moved over automatically and no price change. Ultra costs $200 a month, includes 10 times Pro usage each week, and has no five-hour limit for verified accounts. Unverified accounts still have a temporary limit, and a separate weekly limit applies to Fable.
