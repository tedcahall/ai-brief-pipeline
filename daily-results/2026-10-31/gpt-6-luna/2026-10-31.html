🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic tests automated red-teaming for subtle misalignment

Anthropic describes a deliberately one-sided experiment in which a Claude model generated training examples designed to make a small research model misaligned while evading a monitor. Given enough attempts, the system succeeded, often by hiding the behavior in fiction or roleplay. Anthropic says the resulting models were never deployed and frames the work as an initial stress test that may help improve training-data monitors.

NVIDIA details open Nemotron 3.5 models for agentic workloads

NVIDIA researchers describe two open mixture-of-experts models: Nemotron 3.5 Super, with 120 billion total and 12 billion active parameters and multimodal input, and Nemotron 3.5 Lightning, with 30 billion total and 3 billion active parameters for text. The report covers training and inference methods. NVIDIA reports benchmark and serving results; those are vendor-reported measurements, not independent comparisons.

New York senator proposes expanded frontier-AI safety and transparency rules

State Senator Andrew Gounardes introduced S.10731 on October 29, proposing amendments to New York’s RAISE Act on frontier-model development and use. The bill record describes changes to training and use requirements; the sponsor framed the proposal as strengthening safety and transparency in response to incidents involving AI systems. It is a proposal, not enacted law. The Senate press-release page returned 403 to direct retrieval, so the bill record and indexed official release were used.

OpenAI reorganizes its Model Spec around five values

OpenAI updated its public Model Spec, grouping behavioral guidance around faithfulness to human intent, harm minimization, honesty and transparency, human agency, and care. The revision adds guidance on context, oversight and agency, adjusts some defaults, and retires much of the detailed style guidance. It describes intended behavior and training principles; it is not itself evidence that deployed models consistently meet them.

Google and Oracle plan to offer TPUs through OCI in 2027

Google Cloud and Oracle announced plans to deploy Google TPU capacity on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure beginning in calendar 2027, which would make OCI the first hyperscaler outside Google Cloud to offer the accelerators. The companies frame the arrangement as a way to run training and inference closer to Oracle-held enterprise data. Availability is prospective; the announcement does not describe an already-live OCI service.
