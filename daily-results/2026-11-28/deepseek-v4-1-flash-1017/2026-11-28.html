📊 AI INDUSTRY PULSE

Reports that frontier AI labs may hold algorithms capable of breaking RSA encryption moved from rumor to mainstream science coverage this weekend, with Nature weighing what a polynomial-time factoring result would mean for banking, browsers and government communications.

The same period brought a $300 million Polish defense contract for Palantir's Maven Smart System, Los Angeles schools disabling Google's AI Overview, a photonic processor pilot inside IONOS data centers, and Liquid AI's new decision model tying the top of a third-party index.

Underneath the headlines, the open question is less about any single release than about how fast capability is arriving relative to the safeguards, procurement rules and classroom policies meant to absorb it.

🌐 TOP STORIES

Rumored lab results could break the encryption behind banking and browsers

Computer scientists including Scott Aaronson say they have heard that leading labs, among them OpenAI and Anthropic, may have found polynomial-time algorithms against public-key cryptography such as RSA. Nature reported the rumors could not be confirmed, and noted that both companies published formal-math results this week suggesting many related problems stay hard. No practical attack has been demonstrated.

Poland signs five-year, $300M deal for Palantir's Maven battlefield AI

Poland's defense ministry signed a five-year contract worth about $300 million (1.2 billion zloty) for Palantir's Maven Smart System, an AI platform for battlefield analysis already used by the US military and NATO. Defense minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz said servers and data will stay with Polish forces, and Palantir CEO Alex Karp attended the signing.

Los Angeles schools disable Google AI Overview on student devices

Los Angeles Unified, the largest US district to do so, quietly disabled Google's AI-generated search summaries on school-issued devices used by roughly half a million students. The district cited age-appropriate and safe experiences. Google says search and AI cannot be separated, and other districts are weighing alternatives to Chromebooks and Google Workspace.

Photonic chips move from the lab into a cloud data center pilot

German startup Q.ANT has deployed photonic processors, which compute with light instead of electrons, at cloud provider IONOS. CEO Michael Fortsch told WIRED the chips act as a coprocessor for nonlinear math and that simulations show four to five times lower energy use, though real hardware has not yet demonstrated the gain. More than 30 engineering teams are optimizing models for the hardware.

Liquid AI's d1.3-ultra ties the top of a third-party decision index

Liquid AI released d1.3-ultra, a decision model it says scores 76.4 on Artificial Analysis' Decision Index v2.1, tying Bison 31B for first place at a lower cost per task. The company reports gains on policy-following decisions, document and audio extraction, and refusal when uncertain. OpenRouter and Vercel access were listed as coming soon.
