🌐 TOP STORIES

Liquid AI’s d1.3-ultra Ties Bison 31B at Lower Cost on Decision Benchmark

Liquid AI released d1.3-ultra, a specialized model for tasks such as ranking choices and extracting dates or numbers. Artificial Analysis found that it tied Bison 31B for the highest score on its Decision Index v2.1 at roughly 60% lower cost per million benchmark tasks. The result measures performance on that test suite, not the cost of arbitrary workloads. The model is available through Liquid AI’s own API.

Tencent Makes Its 102-Language Translation Model Available in GGUF

Tencent announced a GGUF build of Hy-MT3-30B-A3B that can run locally with llama.cpp at several quantization levels. The model uses about 3 billion active parameters out of 30 billion and supports 102 languages, up from 38 in the previous family. Tencent released the broader Hy-MT3 family on November 24; the November 27 announcement highlighted the local build, not a new model generation.

Trainium3 Beats Nvidia GB300 on Cost in a Coding-Agent Benchmark

New developments: SemiAnalysis replayed coding-agent sessions and found that a Trainium3 configuration with vLLM delivered better performance per dollar than Nvidia’s GB300 in its test. Nvidia’s newer Vera Rubin still led the cost-performance comparison, and GB300 beat Trainium3 on time to first token in some cases. Results depend on the workload, software and cost assumptions.

Los Angeles Unified Temporarily Disables Google’s AI Overviews on School Devices

Los Angeles Unified told the Associated Press it had temporarily suspended Google’s AI-generated search summaries on school-issued devices to keep students’ experience age-appropriate. The district had already restricted Google’s separate AI Mode on student devices. Google says AI and search are deeply integrated, making it hard for districts to keep the search engine while opting out of generative answers.

SGLang Makes Speaking a Tool Call for Voice Agents

SGLang Omni now lets a language model choose which text to say aloud instead of reading its entire response. The runtime can stop playback when someone interrupts and tell the model how much the listener heard. In the project’s tests on three models, the average of their median times to first audio fell from 1,826 to 567 milliseconds on three-sentence requests.
