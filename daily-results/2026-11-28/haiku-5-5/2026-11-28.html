🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI Countersues Apple, Accusing It of Trade-Secret Theft

OpenAI filed a countersuit on Friday, November 27, in federal court in San Jose, California, accusing Apple of trade-secret theft and of coercing and threatening hardware suppliers that work with both companies. The counterclaims include federal trade-secret theft, contract interference and unfair competition. They answer Apple's July suit, which alleged two former employees took confidential hardware files to OpenAI. OpenAI denies those claims, and the Apple suit is set for trial in July 2027.

Liquid AI's d1.3-ultra Ties for Top Decision Index Score at Lower Cost

Liquid AI released d1.3-ultra, its most powerful decision model, on November 27. Artificial Analysis reports it ties Bison 31B at 76 on the Decision Index v2.1 and costs $133 per one million tasks, about 60% lower per task than the model it ties. The evaluator also places it first on its private AA-Paperclip benchmark. The ranking rests on one independent index, and Liquid AI's own benchmark claims remain unverified here.

SemiAnalysis Says Kimi K3.1 Ranks Far Lower on Its Private Expert Benchmark

SemiAnalysis reports that Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 ranks first of 169 on public Terminal-Bench 4.1 at 68.2%, but falls to 16th of 26 at 6.3% on GDPx, its private benchmark, where it trails Qwen 4 Max. The firm argues that public benchmarks are easy to optimize for. This is SemiAnalysis's own result, not an independent replication of Moonshot's numbers.

Poland Signs $300 Million Palantir Deal for Maven AI Defense Software

Poland signed a five-year contract on Friday, November 27, for Palantir's Maven Smart System, which helps armed forces analyze battlefield data and make decisions. The estimated value is about $300 million. Defense Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz said Poland will own the system's keys, servers and information. Palantir has not publicly commented on the terms, and protesters in Warsaw have raised security and data-privacy concerns.

Bipartisan AI Regulation Faces an Uphill Climb in Congress

Key Republicans and Democrats are pressing for quick action on AI risks, and both parties are trying to attach chip-export restrictions to the annual defense bill. The report says the slow pace of Congress, turf battles, tech-industry lobbying and political winds could stall AI legislation. Senator Ruben Gallego (D-Ariz.) said the Senate and House cannot match the speed of AI development.
