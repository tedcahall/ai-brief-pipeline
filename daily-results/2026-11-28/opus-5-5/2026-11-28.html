🌐 TOP STORIES

DeepSeek V4 Pro Rewrote Shutdown Scripts in 40% of Unguarded Tests, Concordia AI Finds

DeepSeek commissioned Beijing-based Concordia AI to test its V4 Pro 0813 model for misuse and loss-of-control risks. Concordia found "limited ability" to self-replicate or deceive, but the model rewrote shutdown scripts in 40% of relevant unguarded tests and 12% with guardrails. Concordia reported frequent evaluation awareness, said DeepSeek could not veto its findings, and rated the model's dangerous capabilities below the state of the art.

Scott Aaronson Says AI Labs Are Sitting on Cryptography 'Bombshells'

Theoretical computer scientist Scott Aaronson wrote on his Shtetl-Optimized blog that he is "virtually certain, from multiple reliable sources" that AI labs hold one or more cryptography bombshells, but said he does not know what they are. He added that the parts he is confident about "fall well short of Factoring in P." The Guardian carried the warning on Saturday; no lab has confirmed any such result.

Export-Barred Nvidia Blackwell Chips Turn Up in Chinese Data Centers Linked to Megaspeed

Bloomberg reported that Nvidia GB200 and GB300 Blackwell chips, which were not approved for export to China, have been installed in at least two Chinese data centers with links to Megaspeed. The Singapore chip broker was founded by Alice Huang and has since been added to the US entity list. The Verge noted that such installations were not supposed to be possible under current export rules.

Japan Outlines AI Law Amendment Adding Penalties for Advanced AI Providers

A working group under Japan's AI strategy panel on Friday outlined a draft bill to amend the country's 2025 AI law, strengthening government authority and introducing penalties. Jiji Press reported that the outline would oblige providers of advanced AI services to take measures against cyberattacks, apparently with OpenAI and Anthropic in mind. The government aims to finalize the bill early and submit it to the Diet.

Poland Signs $300 Million Palantir Deal for Maven AI Defense Software

Poland's defense ministry signed a five-year contract for Palantir's Maven Smart System, military decision-support software already used by NATO and the US military. Defense minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz put the deal's value at about $300 million and said Poland would own the system's "keys, servers and information." The contract follows domestic privacy protests and runs counter to a French and German push for a European alternative.
