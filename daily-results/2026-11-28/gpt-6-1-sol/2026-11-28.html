🌐 TOP STORIES

Liquid AI Releases d1.3-ultra With New Ranking and Extraction Capabilities

Liquid AI released d1.3-ultra in its console, adding option rankings, number and date extraction from images and audio, and audio time-span extraction. Artificial Analysis reports a tie with Bison 31B atop its Decision Index v2.1, at a lower measured task cost. Those evaluation results describe this decision benchmark, not general-purpose model superiority.

SemiAnalysis Finds Trainium3 Competitive With GB300 on Inference Cost

SemiAnalysis published Trainium3 inference and microarchitecture tests, reporting favorable token-generation cost against Nvidia GB300 under its tested configurations. Its GLM-5.3 analysis separates prefill and decode, with some time-to-first-token cases still favoring GB300. The reported economics depend on workload, latency target, rental assumptions and utilization; they are not a blanket ranking of every accelerator or cloud service.

Tencent Makes Hy-MT3 Translation Available in GGUF for Local Use

Tencent announced GGUF availability for Hy-MT3-30B-A3B, its 30-billion-parameter translation model with 3 billion active parameters and support for 102 languages. The package runs with llama.cpp in several quantizations. This is a local-deployment update to the model family open-sourced earlier in the week, not a new foundation-model launch; the performance comparisons remain Tencent’s own evaluations.

Q.ANT Pilots Photonic AI Coprocessors With IONOS

WIRED reports that Q.ANT has placed photonic processors in IONOS facilities for a pilot, with more than 30 engineering teams developing compatible AI models, according to the company. The chips are intended to complement GPUs on nonlinear calculations. CEO Michael Förtsch says simulations suggest four- to fivefold energy reductions, but explicitly says those gains have not yet been demonstrated in real hardware.

Los Angeles Schools Disable Google AI Overviews on Student Devices

Los Angeles Unified has temporarily suspended Google’s AI-generated search summaries on student devices, according to reporting by The Next Web citing AP and the district. Google does not offer schools a search-without-AI option, and other districts are using filters or considering alternatives. The restrictions began before this report; the fresh coverage documents an institutional adoption dispute rather than a newly launched Google feature.
