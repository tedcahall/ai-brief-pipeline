🌐 TOP STORIES

Experts Warn AI Labs May Be Sitting on Cryptography Breakthroughs

The Guardian reported on November 28 that computer scientist Scott Aaronson is "virtually certain, from multiple reliable sources" that AI labs hold one or more cryptography results, though no lab has announced one. Experts told the paper such a result could either weaken or confirm current encryption, and Bank of England governor Andrew Bailey said the companies are right to be cautious about releasing new models. The claims rest on unnamed sources and remain unconfirmed.

OpenAI Countersues Apple Over Trade Secrets and Supplier Pressure

OpenAI filed counterclaims on Friday, November 27, in federal court in San Jose, accusing Apple of stealing trade secrets, pressuring shared suppliers to limit work with OpenAI, and using restrictive employment agreements to block hiring. OpenAI says it is building connected devices with former Apple design chief Jony Ive. The counterclaims answer a lawsuit Apple filed in July. The allegations are unproven and no court has ruled.

Poland Signs $300 Million Deal for Palantir's Maven AI Software

Poland's defence ministry signed a five-year contract worth about $300 million (1.2 billion zloty) for Palantir's Maven Smart System, an AI platform for battlefield data analysis already used by the US military and NATO's Allied Command Operations. Defence minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz said Poland will keep the servers and data, and that no European provider could offer the same capabilities.

Los Angeles Schools Disable Google's AI Overview on Student Devices

Los Angeles Unified quietly disabled AI Overview in Google searches on school-issued devices, making it the largest district to sideline the feature, according to a report carried by ABC7. Google has said it will not separate search from its AI tools for education, and some districts are weighing other search providers. The district said the suspension is temporary and meant to keep student experiences age-appropriate and safe.

Liquid AI Releases d1.3-ultra, Tying for Top Decision Index Score

Liquid AI released d1.3-ultra on November 27, a decision model that scored 76.4 on the Artificial Analysis Decision Index v2.1, tied with Bison 31B, at roughly 60% lower cost per million tasks according to Artificial Analysis. It adds ranking and extraction from images and audio, has a 64K context window, and costs $0.042 per million input tokens with no charge for output.
