🌐 TOP STORIES

AI Labs May Hold Cryptography Breakthroughs That Banks and Governments Are Not Ready For

New developments: The Guardian reports that Scott Aaronson and other mathematicians believe frontier AI labs are holding unannounced breakthroughs in the maths that underpins encryption, though no lab has confirmed anything. Aaronson called it 'well-attested rumours' and said any finding would still need human verification. Bank of England governor Andrew Bailey said labs should be cautious about releasing models. Experts said deployment hurdles would remain even if a result exists.

Liquid AI Releases d1.3-ultra, Its Most Powerful Decision Model to Date

Liquid AI released d1.3-ultra, which it describes as its most powerful decision model, recursively improved from d1-ultra, with added ranking, extraction and audio-span capabilities and training on scanned documents, receipts and local date formats. The vendor says it scores 76.4 on the Artificial Analysis Decision Index v2.1 and is first of 27 models on that index. It ships in the Liquid console, with OpenRouter and Vercel listed as coming soon.

Poland Signs Five-Year, $300M Palantir Deal for Maven AI Defence Software

Poland signed a five-year contract for Palantir's Maven Smart System, estimated at about $300 million, to use AI software that analyses battlefield data, defence minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz said. He said Poland will own the system's keys, servers and information. The deal has drawn home opposition over data privacy and runs against European plans to build sovereign alternatives to US defence software.

Photonic Processors Pitched as a Cheaper Path for AI Inference

German startup Q.ANT has rolled out photonic processors at IONOS facilities as a pilot, arguing that light-based chips can handle complex non-linear maths alongside GPUs at lower energy and cooling cost. CEO Michael Fortsch says simulation shows four to five times lower energy, while conceding real hardware has not yet demonstrated that gain. He says more than 30 teams are now training models for photonic computing.

SemiAnalysis Says Kimi K3.1 Tops Terminal-Bench but Trails on Its Private Board

SemiAnalysis reported that Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 ranks first of 169 models on Terminal-Bench 4.1 with 68.2 percent, yet falls to sixteenth of 26 on its private GDPx board at 6.3 percent, behind Qwen 4 Max. The firm's reading is that public agentic benchmarks can be optimised for directly, while private expert software engineering tasks are harder to target.
