📊 AI INDUSTRY PULSE

Liquid AI released d1.3-ultra with new decision and extraction capabilities, while Tencent announced local GGUF deployment for its multilingual translation model. Both developments expand specialized model options, with performance claims tied to their stated evaluations.

SGLang is redesigning voice interaction around tool calls, and NVIDIA researchers are compressing scene information to accelerate view synthesis. Q.ANT’s photonic-chip pilot offers another approach to AI compute, but its projected energy savings remain simulation results rather than demonstrated hardware gains.

🌐 TOP STORIES

Liquid AI Releases d1.3-ultra With New Ranking and Extraction Capabilities

Liquid AI says d1.3-ultra is available in its console, adding ranking, image and audio extraction, and audio time-span capabilities. Artificial Analysis separately reports a Decision Index score of 76, tying Bison 31B at lower cost per task. OpenRouter and Vercel availability remained forthcoming in the announcement. These evaluations do not establish superiority on unrelated workloads.

Tencent Brings Hy-MT3 Translation to Local GGUF Inference

Tencent announced GGUF availability for Hy-MT3-30B-A3B, supporting local llama.cpp inference with IQ2, Q4_K_M and Q8_0 quantizations. The translation model covers 102 languages and activates 3 billion parameters from a 30-billion-parameter mixture of experts. This is a deployment update to the family released earlier, not a new foundational release; comparative quality claims come from Tencent.

SGLang Omni Makes Speech a Tool Call

SGLang describes a speech pipeline in which the language model chooses what to say and the runtime records what the listener actually heard. Its reported average of median first-audio measurements falls from 1,826 to 567 milliseconds across three models on three-sentence requests. The design supports interruptions, but the system still handles interaction decisions the models cannot reliably make.

NVIDIA Highlights Test-time Baking for Faster View Synthesis

NVIDIA highlighted Test-time Baking, a NeurIPS spotlight project that compresses cross-view attention into small neural networks for rendering new viewpoints. The researchers report up to 23-fold faster rendering than their KV-cache baseline on tested scenes, with an upfront reconstruction cost. This is a research result with released resources, not a general speedup for all graphics workloads.

Q.ANT Pilots Photonic AI Chips With IONOS

WIRED reports that Q.ANT has deployed photonic chips in an IONOS pilot, aiming to complement rather than replace GPUs. Chief executive Michael Förtsch says simulations suggest four- to five-fold energy reductions, but explicitly says those savings have not yet been demonstrated in real hardware. Model adaptation and a less mature supply chain remain obstacles to wider deployment.
