🌐 TOP STORIES

Liquid AI Releases d1.3-ultra Decision Model

Liquid AI said on November 27 that it released d1.3-ultra, a decision model it described as recursively improved from d1-ultra and available in the Liquid console. Liquid reported a 76.4 score and a first-place claim on the Artificial Analysis Decision Index v2.1. Artificial Analysis separately posted a score of 76, tying Bison 31B, at $0.042 per million input tokens and no output charge. Those figures are the publishers' own measurements.

Guardian: Experts Warn of Unreleased AI Cryptography Results

The Guardian reported on November 28 that Scott Aaronson said he was virtually certain labs are holding unreleased cryptography results he called bombshells, based on sources he described as reliable. He wrote that he had not seen the details. The paper said no lab had announced that encryption was broken, and that a result could weaken systems or show they are stronger. This is a warning, not a demonstrated break.

SemiAnalysis Says Trainium3 Leads Its Cost Curve, Except Versus Rubin

SemiAnalysis said Trainium3 and TPUs are now on its InferenceX benchmark, and that Trainium3 with vLLM beat every other accelerator it measured except Vera Rubin on a cost-versus-interactivity curve. At 135 tokens per second per user, it reported a 73.5 percent margin for Trainium3 versus 53 percent for an Nvidia GB300 with SGLang. Those figures use Z.ai list prices, 60 percent utilization, and three-year rental assumptions. They are SemiAnalysis measurements, not a vendor press release.

Moonshot Opens Kimi for Science With Up to $20,000 in API Credits

Moonshot's Kimi for Science page says selected research teams can receive up to $20,000 in Kimi API credits, about 7 billion tokens, plus technical support and early access to new models. The credits are for model use, not cash. Moonshot's November 27 post said the program was opening that day, set a December 8 deadline for the first round, and linked the page.

Poland Signs a Five-Year Palantir Deal for Military AI Software

Poland's defense ministry said it signed an agreement with Palantir in Warsaw on November 27 for AI software that analyzes military data and recommends options, while a person keeps the decision. The software is to run on Polish military infrastructure. POLITICO reported the contract as a five-year Maven deal worth about $300 million. The ministry page confirms the signing and purpose, but the dollar figure comes from POLITICO.
