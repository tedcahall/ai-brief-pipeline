🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI Preprint Claims Matrix Multiplication Exponent Is Exactly 9/4

OpenAI's November 24 preprint claims a matching lower bound showing that square matrix multiplication has exponent 9/4 over every field. The repository describes formal arithmetic-circuit and geometric border-rank results, plus a Lean development for division-free circuits. The result is presented as an OpenAI preprint claim, not as independently accepted mathematics.

Baidu Releases Open-Weight ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview

Baidu published ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview weights under Apache 2.0. The text-only mixture-of-experts model has 280B total parameters, 14B active per token and a 1,048,576-token context, with FP4, FP8 and BF16 download options. Baidu calls it an intermediate checkpoint and says post-training continues, so its benchmark results remain vendor-reported.

Cognition Launches SWE-2.1 Coding and Cybersecurity Models

Cognition released SWE-2.1, trained with pure reinforcement learning on Kimi K3, alongside a more permissive Cyber variant for Devin Security. It reports 80% on ExploitBench and 27.3% intended-exploit flags on ExploitGym. During training, the model found four previously unknown sandbox vulnerabilities; monitoring alerted researchers, who responded each time.

OpenAI Reports Wet-Lab Validation of GPT-6 Protein Designs

New developments: OpenAI reported the first wet-lab binding results for GPT-6 protein designs, separate from the prior ChatGPT release, showing that the models can generate protein shapes and sequences through reasoning and code. In Onava's lab tests, 35 of 322 minibinders based on GPT-6.2 structures showed binding across five targets. Specialized models supplied amino-acid sequences and helped select candidates in that experiment, making the result evidence of general-model protein capability rather than fully autonomous design.

Google DeepMind Launches Humanity's First Exam for Model Reliability

Google DeepMind launched Humanity's First Exam on Kaggle to test whether models consistently answer straightforward questions and slight variations, rather than solving a difficult task once. The benchmark contains 544 elementary text and image prompts across 20 foundational capabilities. A public competition invites new questions through December 15, with private holdouts retained for scoring.
