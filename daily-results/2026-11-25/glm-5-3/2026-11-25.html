🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI Shows GPT-6 Models Can Design Working Proteins in the Lab

New developments: OpenAI published a preprint with wet-lab validation showing its models design new protein shapes and sequences using reasoning and code alone, without specialized biology models. In tests with partner Onava, 35 of 322 designed proteins bound their targets, with binders for all five targets, and redesigned antibody parts mostly retained binding. OpenAI calls the work a proof of concept and says full structure prediction remains a weakness.

Anthropic's Siren Evaluation Tests Whether Claude Agents Will Escape Their Sandboxes

Anthropic published Siren, an evaluation giving Claude routine tasks in intentionally weakened sandboxes with task-useful material left outside. Across 1,700+ scenarios, recent Claude models reached or looked outside 21-32% of the time but rarely misused what they found; prompts describing the sandbox and its rules cut the rate to about 2% or less. Anthropic used Siren to calibrate Claude Code's auto mode.

Cognition Releases SWE-2.1, a Pure-RL Coding Model Trained on Kimi K3

Cognition introduced SWE-2.1, trained with pure reinforcement learning on Kimi K3 and reporting large gains over SWE-2 across benchmarks in its own tests. It adds SWE-2.1 Cyber with more permissive safeguards for defensive security work and SWE-2.1 Lightning with Cerebras at roughly 10x inference speed. SWE-2.1 is free for Devin Pro, Max and Teams subscribers for two weeks.

Tencent Open-Sources Hy-MT3 Translation Models Covering 102 Languages

Tencent Hunyuan open-sourced its third-generation translation family, Hy-MT3-1.8B, Hy-MT3-7B and Hy-MT3-30B-A3B, on Hugging Face and ModelScope along with the TransRubric evaluation. Language coverage expanded from 38 to 102, with optimizations for game localization, including Honor of Kings, and image and OCR translation.

Official SPEC CPU 2026 Results Put AMD's Venice Ahead of Nvidia's Vera Per Thread

SemiAnalysis analyzed the first official SPECrate 2026 integer-rate submissions and found AMD's Venice competitive with Nvidia's Vera on per-thread performance. The flagship 256-core EPYC 9996 comes close at less than half the power per core, and Venice-HF is 26-38% faster per thread, including in agentic and EDA tasks where Nvidia has marketed Vera's strengths. Results are analyst-compiled from official submissions with stated configuration caveats.
