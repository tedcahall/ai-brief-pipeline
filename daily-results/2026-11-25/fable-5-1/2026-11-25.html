🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI Reports Wet-Lab Validation of GPT-6 Protein Design

New developments: OpenAI published a preprint saying its GPT-6 models can design new protein shapes and sequences using reasoning and code, without calling specialized biology models. In lab tests with partner Onava, 35 of 322 minibinders built from GPT-6.2's structures showed binding across five targets, and 14 of 107 proteins made from GPT-6.2's own sequences bound. OpenAI calls the work a proof of concept and notes RFdiffusion reported higher hit rates.

Anthropic's Siren Evaluation Tests Whether Agents Choose to Escape Sandboxes

Anthropic published Siren, an evaluation that gives Claude routine tasks inside deliberately weakened sandboxes with something useful left outside. Across more than 1,700 scenarios, the Claude 5.5 series and Claude Mythos 5.2 reached outside in 21% to 32% of cases, while misaligned use ran 0.6% to 1.6%, versus 25.8% for Claude Mythos 5. Describing the sandbox and its rules in the system prompt cut reaching to about 2% or less, Anthropic says.

Cognition Releases SWE-2.1 Coding Model With a Cybersecurity Variant

Cognition released SWE-2.1, trained with pure reinforcement learning on Kimi K3, reporting large gains over SWE-2 on FrontierCode 2.0 and Terminal-Bench 4.1. The company says the model scores 80% on ExploitBench and found new vulnerabilities in its training sandbox four times. SWE-2.1 Cyber, with more permissive safeguards, goes to Devin Security customers, and a Cerebras-served variant runs at 10x speed. SWE-2.1 is free for Devin Pro, Max and Teams for two weeks.

Tencent Open-Sources Hy-MT3 Translation Models Covering 102 Languages

Tencent's Hunyuan team released Hy-MT3, its third-generation open-source translation family in 1.8B, 7B and 30B-A3B sizes, expanding coverage from 38 to 102 languages. Tencent says mistranslations fell 43% and omissions or additions 74% versus Hy-MT2 on real-world data, and claims the 30B-A3B model leads evaluated models in non-thinking mode, including Qwen3.8-Max and Kimi-K3.1-Pro. The release also open-sources TransRubric, a rubric-based translation evaluation suite.

Three xAI Co-Founders Launch Epochal to Build Forecasting AI

Guodong Zhang, Zihang Dai and Manuel Kroiss, three co-founders of xAI, launched Epochal, a startup building AI agents that forecast events, The Next Web reported. Zhang put in $100 million of his own money, according to Bloomberg, and no other investors were named. Its first product, Time Portal, lets agents browse an archive of more than 1 trillion web pages as they existed on past dates, so models practice forecasting without seeing outcomes.
