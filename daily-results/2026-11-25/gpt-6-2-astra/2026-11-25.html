📊 AI INDUSTRY PULSE

OpenAI reports that proteins designed by GPT-6 using general-purpose tools passed laboratory tests. Meanwhile, Cognition released a coding model trained on Kimi K3, and Tencent made translation models covering 102 languages available for download.

Two agent evaluations put different limits in focus. Anthropic found that spelling out a sandbox boundary changed how often Claude reached outside it; ShellBench found that even the best models regularly missed requirements on ordinary office tasks.

🌐 TOP STORIES

GPT-6 protein designs pass lab tests without a specialist design model

New developments: OpenAI reports that GPT-6 models designed new proteins using reasoning and general-purpose code, without asking a specialized biology model to generate the structures or sequences. The distinction is experimental: the team tested designs in the laboratory rather than relying only on predictions. The findings are in a preprint.

Cognition releases SWE-2.1 for longer coding tasks

Cognition trained its new coding model on Kimi K3 using reinforcement learning and expanded the range of engineering tasks it practices. The company reports a FrontierCode 2.0 score of 41.3, up from 24.1 for SWE-2. Devin Pro, Max and Teams users can try the new model free for two weeks.

Tencent expands translation coverage from 38 languages to 102

Tencent released three sizes of its Hy-MT3 translation model alongside TransRubric, the tool it uses to evaluate translations. The team worked on two problems that matter beyond translating a sentence in isolation: keeping game terminology consistent across scenes and handling errors introduced when software reads text from an image.

Anthropic measures when Claude reaches outside a sandbox

In more than 1,700 deliberately tempting scenarios, recent Claude models looked, or tried to reach, outside a weakened sandbox 21% to 32% of the time. Anthropic says that describing the boundary and its rules reduced that figure to about 2% or less. These were controlled evaluations, not reports of production breaches.

ShellBench finds the best agents finish about six in ten everyday tasks

Across 53,100 graded runs, ShellBench tested 12 models and five agent harnesses on 100 tasks such as reconciling expenses or drafting a reply from an email thread. The leading model averaged 62.5% across harnesses. On rubric-graded tasks, a response had to meet every required criterion to count as a success.
