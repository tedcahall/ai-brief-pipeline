🌐 TOP STORIES

Nous Releases Arete-27B, a Post-Trained Qwen Model

Nous Research released Arete-27B, post-trained from Alibaba’s Qwen3.8-27B, alongside model weights, training data and an evaluation set. On its own 803-prompt evaluation, Nous reports that the rate of answers adopting Chinese state framing fell from 22% for the base model to 0.9% for Arete with thinking off. These are lab-defined measures, not independent proof of neutrality.

Human-Guided DeepMind Agents Find Weaknesses in Legacy RSA

Google DeepMind says human-guided AI research identified two weaknesses affecting legacy 1024-bit RSA, including a signature-forgery issue and a lower-cost batch factoring method for related moduli. Researchers Nadia Heninger and Emmanuel Thomé collaborated on the work. The report says the findings do not establish an immediate threat to modern 2048-bit or larger keys.

Anthropic Maps How Scientists Use Claude

Anthropic analyzed approximately 207,000 consumer Claude and Claude Code research sessions from August and September. Data analysis and writing featured prominently, while idea generation and data gathering saw less use. Its projections for faster research are modeled scenarios, not measurements of realized scientific output; usage in physical and health sciences was lower relative to researcher numbers.

Bank of England Sees Modest AI Productivity Gains So Far

The Bank of England’s November report says surveyed firms estimate AI raised their productivity by around 0.5% over three years. The bank estimates novel AI-related activity contributed just under a fifth of UK GDP growth since the start of 2026, largely through development and deployment rather than broad adoption gains. Its sector-based proxy also includes non-AI activity.

Judge Allows Some Claude Max Marketing Claims to Proceed

A California magistrate allowed negligent misrepresentation and contract claims over Anthropic’s former Claude Max usage advertising to proceed while dismissing other claims. The dispute concerns how five-hour usage multipliers interacted with weekly limits. The ruling addresses whether claims are plausible at this stage; it is not a finding that Anthropic misled customers, and no class has been certified.
