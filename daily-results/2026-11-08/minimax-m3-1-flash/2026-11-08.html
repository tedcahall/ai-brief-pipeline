🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic's IPO Buyers Push for a Lower Price After the AI Rout

Bloomberg reports that investors in Anthropic's IPO are now discussing a valuation of roughly $1.2 trillion to $1.3 trillion, with some buyers weighing $1.1 trillion, against the $2 trillion expected about a month earlier. The Wall Street Journal separately reported that the valuation will likely need to fall and that the November listing could be delayed. Anthropic filed publicly on Oct 26 and plans to list on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT. No final price or date exists.

OpenAI Closes About $11 Billion of a Round Priced at $1.25 Trillion

Bloomberg reported on Nov 6 that roughly $11 billion of new OpenAI funding was wired to the company as of Friday, the first tranche of a round valuing it at $1.25 trillion, and that the round could total about $20 billion once it closes in coming weeks. That is well below the $30 billion OpenAI was reported to be targeting in late September at a $1.4 trillion valuation. OpenAI has separately postponed its own IPO plans.

Moonshot AI Sought Blackwell Capacity From European Neoclouds

CNBC reports, citing four people familiar with the matter, that Beijing-based Moonshot AI, maker of the Kimi models, discussed leasing Nvidia Blackwell capacity from European data centers this year, including clusters of 8,000 or more GPUs, after the White House accused the lab in July of reaching Blackwell chips remotely through Thailand. One source said Moonshot was willing to pay 30 to 50 percent more upfront than a non-Chinese offtaker.

US AI Safety Center Now Reports Informally to the White House

The Associated Press reports that officials at the Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence, an independent Commerce Department division with a $10 million annual budget, were asked to report informally to a White House office. The center has had no permanent leader since an April hire was fired within days and his successor resigned, and it stopped publishing its safety research, which lawmakers began asking about in August. Critics fear politicized evaluation.

Judge Lets Anthropic Face Claims Its Claude Max Ads Misled Buyers

A federal magistrate judge in California ruled that Anthropic must face negligent misrepresentation and breach of contract claims over its 5x or 20x more usage advertising for Claude Max, finding that customers paying monthly could reasonably read the promise as applying to a full week, while the multipliers applied only to five-hour session limits. The judge dismissed most California false advertising claims. The ruling comes weeks after Anthropic renamed its Max plans.
