🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI Closes $11B at $1.25T Valuation, Ships GPT Image 2.6

OpenAI closed roughly $11 billion in new funding at a $1.25 trillion valuation, the initial portion of what Bloomberg reports as the largest AI fundraising round to date. The same week, the company released GPT Image 2.6 with Flare and Sunburst models that took #1 and #2 in Artificial Analysis benchmarks, adding GIF and SVG output for the first time at unchanged pricing of ~$0.21 per 1024x1024 image.

OpenAI Ships GPT Image 2.6 — Flare and Sunburst Take #1 and #2 in Benchmarks

OpenAI released GPT Image 2.6 on November 4 with two API models. Flare delivers nearly 2x faster generation (30s vs 55s median) while Sunburst targets maximum quality at ~2min 15s per image. Both add animated GIF and SVG output, and both rank #1 and #2 across Artificial Analysis Text-to-Image and Image Editing leaderboards at unchanged pricing.

Meta's Muse AI Agent Gains Voice Chat and Animated Avatars in US

Meta launched Muse Voice on November 5, adding real-time spoken conversation and expressive animated avatars to its consumer AI agent. Available to US adults across mobile, web, and WhatsApp, the feature is powered by Muse Spark 1.3 and represents Meta's most significant consumer-facing AI interaction upgrade since Muse's debut.

Nous Research's Arete-27B Strips Chinese Propaganda from Qwen While Preserving Performance

Nous Research released Arete-27B on November 6, fine-tuning Alibaba's Qwen3.8-27B on ~53M tokens to reduce Chinese state-aligned propaganda from 22% to 0.9%. An 803-prompt eval across seven categories showed no significant regression on Hermes Bench while Terminal-Bench-2.1 improved from 67% to 70%, suggesting propaganda layers are shallow and removable.

White House Finalizes AI Bioweapon Safety Policy as Governance Questions Mount

The White House finalized screening requirements for molecular material vendors to reduce AI-assisted bioweapon risks. The move comes as the AP reports the US AI Safety Institute has no permanent leader and has stopped publishing research, highlighting tension between federal AI safety ambitions and institutional capacity.
