🌐 TOP STORIES

Nous Research releases Arete-27B open weights

Nous Research published Arete-27B on Nov. 6, an open-weight supervised fine-tune of Alibaba's Qwen3.8-27B aimed at politically sensitive answers. It used supervised fine-tuning only, with Inkling as the teacher and no reinforcement learning. On its 803-prompt evaluation, scored by an LLM judge it identified as Grok 4.7, the fact score rose from 2.32 to 2.98 out of 5 and propaganda-containing answers fell from 22% to 0.9%, with no meaningful drop on the benchmarks it ran.

SemiAnalysis reports on an 800MW Inner Mongolia AI campus

SemiAnalysis published a Nov. 7 report from a September visit to Galaxy, an Envision campus in Ulanqab planned for up to 800MW of IT capacity. The firm says the first 120MW phase was delivered within a year at a fifth of US cost and came online in August 2026. In a 5MW hall, solid-state transformers converted 10kV AC directly to 800V DC, without a central chiller. It is built-to-suit for two unnamed internet giants.

US AI safety staff asked to report to the White House

The Associated Press, carried by U.S. News, reported that Commerce's Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence has no permanent leader and has stopped publishing research. Two former officials told AP that staff were asked to informally report to a White House office. The center gets $10 million a year, about one-eighth of its UK counterpart, AP said. The White House did not answer specific questions.

Judge lets Claude Max usage claims proceed

The Next Web reported that Magistrate Judge Alex Tse let negligent-misrepresentation and breach-of-contract claims proceed over Anthropic's ads for Claude Max as five or 20 times Pro usage. Plaintiffs say weekly limits are lower than that session multiplier. Citing Courthouse News, TNW quoted Tse that the per-session disclosure sits two hyperlinks from the sales pages, and said it had not read the ruling.

Prosecutors charge an AI executive-impersonation scheme

NBC News reported that Manhattan prosecutors charged Jacob Soall, Sharon Hazan and Guy Sheetrit in an alleged scheme that used AI to impersonate executives and lawyers and steal more than $129 million. The indictment says the scheme ran from July 2024 to February 2026, with earlier arrests in Spain and the United Kingdom. Prosecutors say the group mimicked CEO voices and converted proceeds to cryptocurrency.
