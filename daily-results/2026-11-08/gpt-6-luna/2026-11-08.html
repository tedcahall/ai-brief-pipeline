🌐 TOP STORIES

DeepSeek Plans an Official Harness Plugin Marketplace

DeepSeek plans an official Harness plugin marketplace and support program for plugin creators, according to TechNode's report on a team update. The team said Harness had 1.54 million daily active users on Nov. 4 among people using DeepSeek's official API; TechNode also reported about 35,000 third-party plugins and roughly 60% of active users had created or installed one. These are company-reported ecosystem figures, not an independent audit.

Anthropic Maps How Scientists Use Claude Across Research Fields

Anthropic studied 207,000 Claude and Claude Code research sessions using privacy-preserving methods. It found use concentrated in data analysis and writing and more frequent in fields where current models can perform more tasks. In a scenario where current capabilities are widely adopted, Anthropic estimates AI could take on 27–52% of the researcher time needed to produce a new study, with a median of 38%; this is modeled potential, not observed time saved.

MCPHallu Benchmark Finds Tool-Using Agents Often Miss Impossible Goals

MCPHallu tests agent failures through 358 tasks across five capability domains and 36 production MCP servers, evaluated across 5,012 trajectories from 14 frontier agents. The authors report a mean score of 0.642, but only 23.2% of runs passed tasks with unreachable goals; agents often continued tool calls or claimed success. The preprint argues task-specific failure tests reveal weaknesses aggregate completion scores can miss.

U.S. AI Safety Center's Informal White House Reporting Raises Independence Questions

The Associated Press reported that two former officials said CAISSI staff were asked to report informally to a White House office. The Commerce Department division currently lacks a permanent leader and has stopped publishing its research, AP reported. Critics say the arrangement could undermine independent review; others argue closer White House access could bring the small center more visibility or resources. The reporting describes an informal arrangement, not a formal transfer.

Court Lets Key Claude Max Usage-Advertising Claims Proceed

In Kahn v. Anthropic, Magistrate Judge Alex Tse allowed several claims over Claude Max's '5x' and '20x' usage advertising to proceed, finding the marketing plausibly misleading at the pleading stage, according to The Next Web's report of Courthouse News coverage. The plaintiffs allege weekly limits deliver less than the advertised multiples; those allegations have not been proven. The judge dismissed other claims, and the case continues.
