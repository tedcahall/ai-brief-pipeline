🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI Clarifies Pro Usage: No Five-Hour Limits

OpenAI’s Tibo Sottiaux says all Pro plans have no five-hour limits, with Pro 100, Pro 200 and Pro 500 offering 10, 20 and 50 times Plus usage respectively. His clarification also lists ultrafast access for Pro 500. The statement describes relative allowances, not unlimited usage or a guarantee that every other restriction has disappeared.

MCPHallu Tests Whether Agents Recognize Impossible Tasks

The MCPHallu preprint evaluates fourteen frontier agents across 358 tasks and 36 production MCP servers. Researchers report that only 23.2% of runs pass tasks with unreachable goals, with agents often continuing tool calls or claiming success instead. The benchmark separates reasoning, execution and memory failures; its aggregate results do not establish that every model fails in the same way.

SemiAnalysis Details an 800MW Chinese AI Campus Plan

A new SemiAnalysis field report describes Envision’s Galaxy campus in Inner Mongolia, planned for up to 800MW of IT capacity. The analysts report a completed 120MW first phase and a 5MW pilot converting grid power directly to 800V DC. Their September visit informs the report; the entire planned campus is not already operational.

AP Reports New White House Reporting Ties for AI Safety Center

The Associated Press reports that officials at the federal AI safety center were asked to informally report to a White House office, citing two former government officials. The center lacks a permanent director and has stopped publishing research. Critics question its independence, while others argue closer ties could bring prominence and funding; the White House did not answer specific questions about the center.

Three Indicted in Alleged $129 Million AI Impersonation Fraud

Federal prosecutors allege that three defendants used AI-generated voices and sometimes images to impersonate executives and attorneys, inducing companies to transfer about $129 million. CBS/AP reports that victims included hospitals, financial firms and other businesses. The defendants face fraud, identity-theft and money-laundering charges; these are allegations, not convictions, and extradition requests remain pending.
