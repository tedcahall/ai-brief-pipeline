🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI Closes $11 Billion First Tranche at $1.25 Trillion Valuation

OpenAI has closed roughly $11 billion as the first portion of a funding round priced at a $1.25 trillion pre-money valuation, with the funds wired by Friday, Bloomberg reported and TechCrunch relayed on November 6. The round has not fully closed and more is expected in coming weeks; the valuation sits below the roughly $1.4 trillion OpenAI first sought.

US AI Safety Center Staff Now Informally Report to the White House, AP Reports

Officials at CAISSI, the independent Commerce Department division for superintelligence standards, were recently asked to informally report to a White House office, two former officials told AP. Critics worry safety findings on cybersecurity and bioweapons could be politicized or muted in deference to an industry largely entrusted to police itself after voluntary self-policing requests in September.

SemiAnalysis Tours China's Stargate, an 800MW Wind-Powered AI Campus

SemiAnalysis published a firsthand visit to the Galaxy campus in Ulanqab, Inner Mongolia: a wind-powered campus targeting 800MW, with 120MW built within a year, a 5MW SST-native 800V DC pilot, and cooling with no central chillers. The report offers rare ground truth on how China's fastest-growing AI datacenter region is being built.

Anthropic Sizes AI Research Acceleration Across 207,049 Science Sessions

Anthropic analyzed 207,049 Claude research sessions from August 2 to September 30 plus a 528-scientist survey. It estimates that with current capabilities widely adopted, AI could take on 27 to 52 percent of researcher time to produce a new study, median 38 percent, with modeled output acceleration between 1.35x and 2.02x depending on field.

Asahi Linux Moves All Code to GPLv2-or-Later and Rejects AI Contributions

In its Linux 7.3 progress report, Asahi Linux announced M3 Mac support reaching general availability and a move of every repository from permissive MIT to GPL version 2 or later. Developers cited bad actors exploiting permissive licensing, including code rewritten with LLM assistance to hide where it came from, and said the project will not accept AI contributions.
