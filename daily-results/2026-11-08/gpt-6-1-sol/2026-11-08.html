🌐 TOP STORIES

GPT Image 2.6 Leads Artificial Analysis Image Benchmarks

New developments: Artificial Analysis has published an independent evaluation following the previously covered model launch. It places Sunburst and Flare first and second on its text-to-image and editing benchmarks. Flare's median text-to-image time fell from about 55 to 30 seconds versus its predecessor; Sunburst takes about 2 minutes 15 seconds. Both retain the prior models' image-output token price.

Inside Envision’s 800MW AI Campus Plan in Inner Mongolia

SemiAnalysis reports that Envision's Galaxy campus is planned for 800MW of IT capacity, with its first 120MW phase delivered in August. During a September visit, analysts observed a live 5MW pilot converting 10kV AC directly to 800V DC for racks. The report also describes shared cooling without central chillers. The full campus is expected by the end of 2027, not already operational.

MCPHallu Tests Whether Agents Recognize Impossible Tasks

A research preprint introduces 358 tasks across 36 production MCP servers to probe reasoning, execution and memory failures. Across fourteen agents and 5,012 trajectories, the authors report an average score of 0.642 out of one. Only 23.2% of unreachable-goal runs pass. These benchmark results diagnose tool-use failure modes; they do not demonstrate an actual containment escape or establish reliability on all production workflows.

Judge Allows Key Claude Max Usage Claims to Proceed

According to TNW's account of Courthouse News reporting, a California judge allowed key claims over Claude Max's advertised usage multiples to proceed while dismissing others. The dispute concerns whether per-session multipliers misleadingly implied comparable monthly or weekly allowances. This is a procedural ruling in a proposed class action, not a finding that Anthropic is liable; TNW says it has not read the ruling.

AP Reports Changes to Federal AI Safety Reporting Lines

AP reports that staff at the federal AI safety center were asked to informally report to a White House office, citing two former officials familiar with the conversations. The center lacks a permanent leader and has stopped publishing research, AP says. Critics raise independence concerns; others suggest closer access could increase prominence or funding. The White House did not answer AP's specific questions about the center.
