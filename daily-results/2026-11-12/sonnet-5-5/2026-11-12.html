🌐 TOP STORIES

Alibaba Opens Preview of Qwen4-Max With a One-Million-Token Window

Alibaba's Model Studio documentation calls Qwen4-Max-Preview the next-generation flagship multimodal model in the Qwen family. It accepts text, image and video input, supports a one-million-token context, tool calling and structured outputs, and is available only through Alibaba's Token Plan from Nov 11 to Nov 25 (UTC+8), after which the preview model will be retired.

Google Introduces Gemini Decisions API and Gemini Decision 1

Google introduced the Gemini Decisions API, powered by Gemini Decision 1, which scores up to 1,000 yes/no, multiple-choice and rubric questions about text, image, audio, video or PDF input in one forward pass rather than generating text. Google reports 150-250 ms end-to-end latency and $0.10 per million input tokens with no output tokens billed; the benchmark claims are Google's own.

xAI Launches Grok Bot 1.0 With Shared Team Bots in Slack, Teams and Video Calls

xAI, posting as SpaceXSI, launched Grok Bot 1.0, which lets teams share bots and bring them into Slack, Microsoft Teams and video calls. xAI says bots can spot unfinished work and offer to take it on, and are faster and more reliable at browser tasks, documents and presentations. Windows Report says Team Bots require Team or Enterprise plans.

UK AI Chip Startup Fractile Raises $720 Million Series C

Fractile raised a $720 million Series C co-led by Thrive Capital, Founders Fund, Lightspeed Venture Partners and Redpoint, with backing from the UK's Sovereign AI Fund. The UK government put the valuation above £5 billion, which TechCrunch says the Financial Times and a Reuters source reported as about $7 billion. CEO Walter Goodwin said the funding will let Fractile ship its first chips and systems next year.

TSMC Board Approves Up to $1 Billion of Stakes in OpenAI and Anthropic

TSMC's board approved up to $500 million in OpenAI preferred stock and $500 million in Anthropic common shares at its expected IPO price, according to Reuters as cited by Ars Technica. The Wall Street Journal reported that analysts warn circular financing among investors, suppliers and customers makes real demand for AI hardware and services hard to evaluate.
