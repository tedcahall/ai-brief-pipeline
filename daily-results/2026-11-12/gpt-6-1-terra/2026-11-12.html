📊 AI INDUSTRY PULSE

Google DeepMind introduced AlphaGenome 2 to predict genetic-variant effects in specific cell types, while Baidu released a post-trained ERNIE 5.5 Flash preview. Alibaba also opened a Qwen4-Max preview, with availability still limited.

🌐 TOP STORIES

DeepMind unveils AlphaGenome 2 for cell-specific DNA predictions

Google DeepMind introduced AlphaGenome 2, a DNA sequence model that predicts how genetic variants affect particular cell types. The company says it identified 30% of causal variants in one immune-cell dataset, compared with 16% for the previous model. It is available through the AlphaGenome API for non-commercial research, with commercial research in private preview.

Baidu previews post-trained ERNIE 5.5 Flash

Baidu released ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview, a post-trained model built on its earlier Flash Base. Baidu says the mixture-of-experts model has 280 billion total parameters, 14 billion active parameters and a one-million-token context window. The API is available on Qianfan; the company says it plans to open-source the weights, which are not yet released.

Alibaba opens Qwen4-Max preview

Alibaba has made Qwen4-Max available in preview through its Token Plan and several of its applications. Its Model Studio documentation describes multimodal input and a large context window. The preview is not a general release, and Alibaba’s performance claims should be evaluated separately from independently measured results.

Google launches Gemini Decisions API

Google introduced Gemini Decision 1 and the Gemini Decisions API for bounded choices rather than open-ended text generation. The service can score up to 1,000 questions against a shared multimodal input and returns confidence scores. Google lists a price of $0.10 per million input tokens and no output-token charge.

AI-generated voices complicate emergency response

NPR reports that callers have used synthetic voices in swatting hoaxes, including a school bomb threat in California. A survey cited by NPR found that about 14% of 766 responding public-safety communications professionals reported being targeted using AI tools. Emergency communications groups are developing training while continuing to treat reports of life-threatening emergencies seriously.
