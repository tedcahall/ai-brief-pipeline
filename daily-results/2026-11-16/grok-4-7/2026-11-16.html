🌐 TOP STORIES

Alibaba Launches Qwen4-Max, a Closed-Weight Flagship

Alibaba announced Qwen4-Max on November 16 and is keeping the weights closed, The Next Web reported from the launch blog. The company describes a 2.4 trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with native vision and a 1 million-token window, priced at $1.50 per million input tokens and $4.50 per million output tokens. Alibaba's coding charts are mixed versus Opus 5.5 and Kimi K3.1 Pro. TNW did not independently verify them. Smaller Qwen4 weights are promised, with no date.

Tencent Releases Hy4 Flash for Lower-Cost Everyday Work

Tencent launched Hy4 flash on Monday for everyday work, TechNode reported. The official post lists API prices of $0.003 per million cached tokens, $0.070 input and $0.281 output, and says internal blind tests matched Claude Haiku 5.5 (high) with an 18x task-cost cut. TechNode adds 224 billion parameters, 12 billion active, and one-twentieth the cost of full Hy4 on Tencent's WorkIndex. Those figures are Tencent's claims.

OpenAI Science Blog: GPT-6 Astra and a Lattice Standard Model

OpenAI published the first post on a new science blog, also posted at 00:23 UTC on November 16. Physicist Nathaniel Craig writes that an October preprint, prepared with GPT-6 Astra, proposes a candidate lattice definition of the Standard Model. He says Astra turned an incomplete strategy into a construction and led the detailed proof. The post is Craig's account of that collaboration, not an independent replication.

Insurers Hold $85 Billion in AI Debt, Moody's Tells the Times

The New York Times reported on November 16 at 5:00 a.m. ET that insurers are putting customer money into AI deals. Citing Moody's, it said $2.4 billion of chip-lease debt for xAI was sold to Athene. U.S. life insurers held $85.2 billion of AI debt as of June 30, up 24 percent in six months, or 2.1 percent of their debt. Moody's projected that could reach $185 billion by the end of next year.

Mercor Raised $750 Million Series D at a $20 Billion Valuation

On November 10, Mercor announced a $750 million Series D at a $20 billion valuation, a round not used in the prior week's briefs. The company post lists Benchmark, Felicis, Fundomo, Apollo-affiliated funds, NVIDIA and Salesforce. The $750 million is the amount raised, not the valuation. Mercor says the capital will fund evaluation and post-training for enterprise agents. A November 16 roundup repeated the same round.
