🌐 TOP STORIES

Alibaba Launches Qwen4-Max, a Closed-Weight Flagship With a 1M-Token Context Window

Alibaba announced Qwen4-Max on November 16 as a mixture-of-experts model with 2.4 trillion parameters, text, image and video input, and a 1M-token context window. The API costs $1.50 per million input tokens and $4.50 per million output tokens. Alibaba says its internal Work360 average task cost fell from $4.43 to $1.67. Weights are closed, and smaller Qwen4 models will be open-weight.

OpenAI Says GPT-6 Astra Helped Build a Lattice Definition of the Standard Model

In the first post on OpenAI's new science blog, physicist Nathaniel Craig describes working with GPT-6 Astra on a candidate nonperturbative lattice definition of the Standard Model. Craig says Astra helped turn his strategy into a construction and took the lead in developing the detailed proof, which outside lattice theorists reviewed. He calls the result an answer in principle, not yet in practice.

Tencent Releases Hy4 Flash, Priced at $0.281 per Million Output Tokens

Tencent released Hy4 flash on November 16, a mixture-of-experts model with 224 billion total parameters, 12 billion active per token and a 1M-token context window, according to AlphaSignal. Pricing is $0.003 cached, $0.070 input and $0.281 output per million tokens. Tencent says human blind tests show it matching Claude Haiku 5.5 (high) on daily work at 18 times lower task cost.

Cursor Traces Malicious Claude Code Extension to an Access Key Exposed at Fireworks

Cursor's incident update says an access key it issued to inference vendor Fireworks was exposed and used to copy agent traces, a few containing unredacted credentials. On October 29 the attacker placed tampered Anthropic Claude Code extension copies on Cursor's marketplace cache for about 11 hours, reaching at most 5,000 users. Cursor says this was not a vulnerability in Anthropic's product and that the last attacker activity was November 1.

Life Insurers Hold $85.2 Billion of AI Debt as Analysts Warn of Risk

The New York Times, citing Moody's, reports that US life insurers including Athene and Corebridge held $85.2 billion of AI debt as of June 30, up 24% in six months and 2.1% of their total debt. Moody's says holdings could reach $185 billion by the end of next year. One example is a $2.4 billion slice of xAI's chip-lease financing sold to Athene.
