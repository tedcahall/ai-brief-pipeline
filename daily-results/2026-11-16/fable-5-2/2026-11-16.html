🌐 TOP STORIES

Alibaba Launches Qwen4-Max Flagship and Keeps the Weights Closed

Alibaba's Qwen team released Qwen4-Max on November 16, a 2.4-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with a 1M-token context window that reads text, images and video. API pricing is $1.50 per million input tokens and $4.50 per million output tokens. Alibaba reports 75.7 on DeepSWE v1.1, ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 at 74.2, but 48.5 on Terminal-Bench 4.0, behind Opus 5.5 and Kimi K3.1. Smaller Qwen4 models will get open weights, with no date given.

Tencent Launches Hy4 Flash at $0.07 per Million Input Tokens

Tencent on Monday launched Hy4 flash, a lower-cost multimodal model with 224 billion parameters, 12 billion active per forward pass, and a context window of up to one million tokens. List prices are $0.07 per million input tokens and $0.281 per million output tokens. In Tencent's blind test of 85 WorkBuddy users across 259 tasks, it scored close to Claude Haiku 5.5 in satisfaction. Tencent plans to release the weights.

Nvidia's Interim Chief Inherits $530 Billion in AI Financing Exposure

New developments: A Wall Street Journal profile of interim CEO Colette Kress, as summarized by Techstrong.ai, describes how Nvidia financed its own customers as investor, loan guarantor and buyer of excess capacity. Nvidia holds $279 billion in supplier purchase obligations, and total exposure including contingent loan and lease guarantees exceeds $530 billion. Analysts expect Kress to review existing guarantees more strictly and make fewer new investments.

OpenAI Opens a Science Blog With an Astra-Assisted Physics Result

OpenAI launched a science blog with a post by physicist Nathaniel Craig describing a candidate nonperturbative lattice definition of the Standard Model, proposed in an October preprint prepared with GPT-6 Astra. Craig writes that Astra took the lead in developing the detailed proof, which lattice field theorists Maarten Golterman, Andreas Kronfeld and Yigal Shamir looked over. He calls the method an answer in principle that still requires expert scrutiny.

FAA's AI Traffic System Draws Questions After Nationwide Ground Stop

The FAA's $875 million SMART system, designed by startup Air Space Intelligence, expanded nationwide on November 10, three days before Friday's ground stop. Government officials and the company say the two are unrelated. SMART centralizes 200 data streams and sends routing recommendations to human controllers. House Democrats asked the GAO in October to audit it, citing limited workforce input and the potential to hallucinate safety-critical information.
