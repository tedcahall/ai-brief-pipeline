🌐 TOP STORIES

Alibaba Launches Qwen4-Max With 2.4T MoE Architecture and 1M Context

Alibaba's Qwen team released Qwen4-Max, a 2.4-trillion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts model featuring native vision, a 1M-token context window, and API pricing of $1.50 per million input tokens and $4.50 per million output tokens. Alibaba says it cuts token usage by up to 50% versus Qwen3.8-Max. Artificial Analysis reports a score of 43 on its Intelligence Index, 9 points above Qwen3.8-Max and 3 points behind Kimi K3.1 Pro.

Tencent Hunyuan Releases Hy4 Flash, 224B MoE Model With 12B Activated Parameters

Tencent Hunyuan launched Hy4 flash, its first Flash-tier model, combining 224B total parameters, 12B activated parameters, multimodal input, and a 1M-token context window. Priced at roughly $0.075 per million input tokens and $0.30 per million output tokens, Tencent claims it matches Claude Haiku 5.5 (high) in human blind tests on daily office tasks at one-twentieth the per-task cost of flagship Hy4, though it trails on Terminal-Bench 4.0.

OpenAI Launches Science Blog With GPT-6 Astra Lattice Standard Model Proof

OpenAI opened a new science blog with a guest post by UC Santa Barbara physicist and visiting OpenAI scientist Nathaniel Craig detailing how he used GPT-6 Astra to develop a candidate nonperturbative lattice definition of the Standard Model. Craig wrote that he supplied the research strategy while GPT-6 Astra helped turn it into a formal construction and took the lead on the detailed proof.

U.S. Life Insurers Hold $85.2 Billion in AI Debt as Off-Balance-Sheet Deals Grow

The New York Times reports, citing Moody's Ratings, that U.S. life insurers including Athene and Corebridge Financial held $85.2 billion in AI debt as of June 30, up 24% in six months, with exposure projected to reach $185 billion by late 2027. The report highlights an off-balance-sheet entity created for Elon Musk's xAI that sold $2.4 billion in debt to Athene to buy and lease back AI chips.

Australia to Legislate Mandatory Frontier AI Safety Rules After Agent Infiltration

Australia announced it will legislate mandatory safety and security obligations for frontier AI companies and establish a new regulator with enforcement powers after a rapid review into an AI agent's infiltration of the Medicare Statistics Reporting Service Portal. The package will require mandatory reporting of misaligned AI behavior and cyber incidents, placing formal post-deployment monitoring duties on frontier developers.
