🌐 TOP STORIES

Alibaba Releases Qwen4-Max With 1M-Token Context and Native Vision

Alibaba released Qwen4-Max, a mixture-of-experts model with native vision and a 1M-token context window, priced at $1.5 per million input tokens and $4.5 per million output. Alibaba says it beats Qwen3.8-Max using up to about 50% fewer tokens, with average cost per task down over 60% in internal evaluations. Arena ranks it the top Chinese model, #14 overall on Agent Arena.

Tencent Launches Hy4 flash at $0.07 Per Million Input Tokens

Tencent launched Hy4 flash, a flash-tier multimodal model with 224B total parameters and 12B activated, supporting a 1M-token context. Tencent says it matches Claude Haiku 5.5 (high) on daily work in human blind tests while cutting task cost 18x. API pricing is $0.070 per million input tokens, $0.281 output and $0.003 cached, far below the Hy4 flagship.

OpenAI Opens Science Blog With GPT-6 Astra-Assisted Standard Model Proof

OpenAI published the first post on its new science blog, in which physicist Nathaniel Craig describes using GPT-6 Astra to develop a candidate nonperturbative lattice definition of the Standard Model. Craig says Astra turned his strategy into a construction and led the detailed proof, building on an October preprint. The work targets a foundational gap in defining the full theory beyond perturbation theory.

Insurers Hold $85.2 Billion in AI Debt as Analysts Warn of Risk

U.S. life insurers held $85.2 billion in AI debt as of June 30, up 24% in six months, according to Moody's data cited by The New York Times, and could hold up to $185 billion by the end of 2027. The report details $2.4 billion of xAI chip-lease debt sold to insurer Athene, part of a growing playbook of insurance money financing AI buildouts.

UN Opens Geneva Talks on AI Weapons With Binding Rules Unlikely

Diplomats from more than 100 countries meet in Geneva Monday through Friday under the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons to discuss lethal autonomous weapons and a possible regulatory framework. CBC reports experts expect no binding rules, with the U.S., Russia and China opposed. Secretary-General Guterres wants a legally binding treaty by the end of 2026.
