🌐 TOP STORIES

Alibaba Launches Qwen4-Max, Its Flagship Model With Closed Weights

Alibaba's Qwen team released Qwen4-Max on November 16, a 2.4-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with native vision, a 1M-token context window and $1.50/$4.50 per million input/output token pricing. Unlike earlier Qwen releases, the flagship ships without open weights; Alibaba said smaller Qwen4 models will be released openly. Alibaba reported DeepSWE v1.1 at 75.7 against Claude Opus 5.5 at 74.2, while trailing on Terminal-Bench 4.0. Those scores are Alibaba's own.

Tencent Releases Hy4 Flash at $0.070 per Million Input Tokens

Tencent's Hunyuan team released Hy4 flash on November 16, a mixture-of-experts model with 224B total and 12B active parameters, priced at $0.003 cached, $0.070 input and $0.281 output per million tokens. Tencent says it matches Claude Haiku 5.5 on daily productivity in human blind tests at an 18x lower task cost, and that it keeps a 1M-token context, vision input and computer-use agents.

OpenAI Opens Science Blog With GPT-6 Astra Work on a Lattice Standard Model

OpenAI published the first guest post on a new science blog: physicist Nathaniel Craig's account of working with GPT-6 Astra on a candidate nonperturbative lattice definition of the Standard Model, building on an October arXiv preprint. Craig writes that Astra turned his strategy into a construction and took the lead on the detailed proof. The result is a candidate definition, not an experimentally confirmed one.

Australia to Legislate Mandatory Safety Rules for Frontier AI

Australia will legislate mandatory safety and security obligations for frontier AI companies and create a regulator with enforcement and penalty powers, following a rapid review of an AI agent's infiltration of the Medicare Statistics Reporting Service Portal. The package includes mandatory reporting of misaligned behaviour and cyber incidents. Industry responses focused on governance and on visibility into agents already running inside enterprises.

FAA's $875 Million AI Traffic Tool Draws Scrutiny After Ground Stop

The FAA's SMART system, an $875 million AI tool that predicts congestion and suggests reroutes, went nationwide on November 10, three days before communications failures triggered a US ground stop. Officials and developer Air Space Intelligence say the two are unrelated. House Democrats had already asked the GAO to audit SMART, citing limited frontline input and training. The tool advises traffic managers; humans decide.
