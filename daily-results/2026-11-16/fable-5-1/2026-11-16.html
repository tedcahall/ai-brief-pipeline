🌐 TOP STORIES

Alibaba Launches Qwen4-Max With Native Vision and a 1M-Token Context Window

Alibaba's Qwen team launched Qwen4-Max, a new mixture-of-experts flagship with native vision and a 1M-token context window, priced at $1.50 per million input tokens and $4.50 per million output on QwenCloud. Alibaba says it outperforms Qwen3.8-Max with up to about 50% fewer tokens and over 60% lower cost per task in its internal evaluation, and that smaller Qwen4 models will be released with open weights.

Tencent Releases Hy4 flash, a Low-Cost Model at $0.07 per Million Input Tokens

Tencent released Hy4 flash, which it bills as its cheapest model for everyday productivity, with API list prices of $0.07 per million input tokens, $0.281 per million output and $0.003 for cached input. Tencent says the model matched Claude Haiku 5.5 in human blind tests at about 18 times lower task cost; TechNode reports it has 224 billion parameters, activates 12 billion per pass, and supports a one million token context.

OpenAI Launches a Science Blog With GPT-6 Astra Work on a Lattice Standard Model

OpenAI opened a science blog with a guest post by UC Santa Barbara physicist Nathaniel Craig, a visiting scientist at OpenAI, describing how GPT-6 Astra helped him propose a candidate lattice definition of the Standard Model. Craig writes that he supplied the strategy while Astra turned it into a construction and led the detailed proof, published as an October arXiv preprint, and he presents the result as a candidate definition rather than a settled one.

Google Opens Antigravity /teamwork Agents After a Machine-Checked Weil Conjectures Proof

Google Research said its Antigravity /teamwork multi-agent workflows, running Gemini 4 Argon, Carbon and Hydrogen, helped prove the 2013 Courtade-Kumar information theory conjecture with CUHK, USC and CMU researchers and autonomously produced a machine-checked Lean formalization of the Weil conjectures spanning about 1.8 million lines. Google is making the tools available in Antigravity and offering 10,000 academic researchers six months of free access to its highest usage tier.

Trump Administration Plans to Name Nvidia, OpenAI and Anthropic Among AI 'National Champions'

The Trump administration plans to designate more than two dozen US AI companies, including Nvidia, Amazon, Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic, as national champions to help them win business overseas against Chinese rivals, The Wall Street Journal reported Sunday, per The Next Web. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick could unveil the list within a week; the program would bundle US chips and models into export packages with government advocacy and faster license reviews.
