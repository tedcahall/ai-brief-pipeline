🌐 TOP STORIES

Alibaba Launches Qwen4-Max Flagship With 1M Context and Far Lower Task Costs

Alibaba's Qwen team launched Qwen4-Max, a new mixture-of-experts flagship with native vision and a 1M-token context window. The company says it beats Qwen3.8-Max with up to about 50% fewer tokens and over 60% lower average cost per task, at $1.50/$4.50 per 1M input/output tokens. Independent Artificial Analysis testing scores it 9 points over Qwen3.8 Max, and smaller open-weight Qwen4 models are promised.

GPT-6 Astra Takes the Lead on a Candidate Lattice Definition of the Standard Model

In the first guest post on OpenAI's new science blog, physicist Nathaniel Craig describes working with GPT-6 Astra on a candidate nonperturbative lattice definition of the Standard Model, a foundational gap in particle physics for over 50 years. Craig says Astra took the lead in developing the detailed proof, and outside lattice experts reviewed the construction; the result remains a candidate requiring scientific scrutiny.

Tencent Hunyuan Ships Hy4 Flash for Cheap Everyday AI Work

Tencent Hunyuan released Hy4 flash, positioned as its cheapest model for everyday productivity. The company says it matches Claude Haiku 5.5 (high) on daily work in human blind tests while cutting task cost 18x, with API pricing of $0.003 cached, $0.070 input and $0.281 output per 1M tokens. Third-party coverage describes the blind test as an 85-user internal Tencent evaluation, so the claim is the vendor's own.

US Life Insurers Hold $85.2 Billion in AI Debt as Analysts Warn

The New York Times reports that US life insurers, including Athene and Corebridge, held $85.2 billion in AI debt as of June 30, up 24% in six months, citing Moody's Ratings, with exposure projected to reach up to $185 billion by the end of next year. The article details a $2.4 billion Athene purchase of debt that financed an xAI chip-buying arrangement, and analyst concern over mounting risk.

OpenAI Codex Team Says It Fixed the "Model at Capacity" Errors

Tibo Sottiaux of OpenAI's Codex team posted that the team "might have actually fixed" the model-at-capacity errors that have repeatedly blocked developers since September. The claim is the team's own and explicitly qualified, and the errors are extensively documented in openai/codex GitHub issues. If it holds, it removes one of the most persistent reliability complaints around the Codex platform.
