🌐 TOP STORIES

Alibaba Launches Qwen4-Max With Vision and a Million-Token Context

Alibaba introduced Qwen4-Max with native vision, a one-million-token context window and a live QwenCloud API. Listed prices are $1.50 per million input tokens and $4.50 per million output tokens. Artificial Analysis reports an Intelligence Index score of 43, up nine points over Qwen3.8 Max. Smaller open-weight Qwen4 models are promised, not yet released in this announcement.

Tencent Introduces Hy4 Flash at $0.07 per Million Input Tokens

Tencent announced Hy4 flash for everyday productivity, listing API prices of $0.070 per million input tokens, $0.281 per million output tokens and $0.003 for cached input. Tencent says its human blind tests found daily-work performance comparable to Claude Haiku 5.5 at lower task cost. Those are vendor-run findings, not independent evidence of universal parity or the cheapest model across all workloads.

OpenAI Shares Astra-Assisted Candidate Standard Model Lattice Construction

Physicist Nathaniel Craig describes how GPT-6 Astra helped develop a candidate nonperturbative lattice definition of the Standard Model, including detailed proof work and review discussions with outside experts. The new science-blog account concerns an October preprint, not a newly completed physics result. Craig says scientific scrutiny remains necessary, practical simulations need further work and reflection positivity at finite lattice spacing remains a question.

US Life Insurers Held $85.2 Billion in AI Debt, Times Reports

US life insurers held $85.2 billion in AI debt at midyear, up 24% over six months, the Times reports, citing Moody’s. Its accessible article preview describes Athene buying $2.4 billion of debt connected to an entity that purchases chips and leases them to xAI. The report puts current AI debt at 2.1% of insurers’ total debt; larger future exposure is a projection, not realized investment.

Samsung’s Data-Center Push Extends Beyond Memory Into Cooling

The Korea Herald reports that Samsung is assembling data-center capabilities across construction, storage, financing and cooling. Reported talks to acquire Modine remain unconfirmed: Samsung declined comment, and no completed acquisition is established. The article adds that shareholders approved renaming the remaining Modine business on November 13, and places the talks alongside Samsung affiliates’ previously announced $1 billion Helix infrastructure investment.
