🌐 TOP STORIES

Google Launches Gemini 4 Neon at $3 per Million Input Tokens

Google began rolling out Neon through the Gemini API and to eligible Gemini and Antigravity subscribers. It sits between Carbon and Hydrogen, with a million-token context, four thinking levels and output pricing of $15 per million tokens. Artificial Analysis scored Neon 61 at its highest effort setting, one point above Carbon at high, at a lower cost per task against Carbon’s undiscounted price.

Anthropic Prices Its IPO at $83 a Share

New developments: Anthropic set a final price above its initial $70 to $78 range and increased the offering to 980 million shares. The base offering totals $81.34 billion before fees, including shares sold by existing stockholders whose proceeds Anthropic will not receive. The company expected trading under ANT on Nasdaq and had not yet reported a debut at the editorial cutoff.

Palo Alto Networks Agrees to Buy Applied Compute for $4.5 Billion

The all-stock agreement would give Palo Alto Networks a platform for adapting and serving open-weight models in public clouds or sovereign environments. Applied Compute co-founder Yash Patil is slated to become co-chief AI officer after closing. The $4.5 billion figure is the transaction value, not a funding round, and the deal still requires regulatory clearance.

OpenAI Reports Chip Design Gains With AMD and Its Own Silicon

OpenAI says its models found improvements of up to 14% against optimized baselines on parts of AMD’s flagship GPU design, and AMD included some changes in a design sent for manufacturing. OpenAI also says it verified area savings on its own Jalapeño chip in silicon. The figures refer to specific design blocks, not a 14% gain for an entire production chip.

California Proposes Independent AI Testing and Emergency Shutdown Powers

California’s recommendations call for baseline standards for AI developers, independent testing through training and deployment, expanded incident reporting, and verified containment plans. They would also give the state emergency shutdown authority and the power to limit certain work on recursive self-improvement. These are proposed oversight powers, separate from the state’s existing laws and from its lawsuit over earlier testing incidents.
