🌐 TOP STORIES

Google Releases Gemini 4 Neon

Google DeepMind released Gemini 4 Neon, a frontier model positioned between Gemini 4 Carbon and Hydrogen, rolling out in the Gemini API, AI Studio, Gemini for business, the Gemini app for AI Pro and Ultra subscribers, and Antigravity. Google prices it at $3 per million input tokens, $15 per million output tokens and $0.15 per million cached input tokens, with a 1 million token context window and four thinking levels.

Anthropic Prices Its IPO at $83 a Share

Anthropic priced its initial public offering of 980 million Class A shares at $83.00 per share, with 956,351,354 of those shares sold by the company. Shares are expected to begin trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market on November 19, 2026 under the symbol ANT, and the offering is expected to close on November 20. The New York Times reported a valuation of more than $1.5 trillion counting all promised shares.

Palo Alto Networks to Acquire Applied Compute for $4.5 Billion

Palo Alto Networks agreed to acquire Applied Compute, a platform for post-training and inference of open-weight models, in an all-stock transaction valued at $4.5 billion, subject to adjustments. The company expects the deal to close in its fiscal third quarter of 2027, and Applied Compute co-founder and CEO Yash Patil will become Palo Alto Networks Co-Chief AI Officer after the transaction closes.

OpenAI Reports How Far Coding Agents Have Accelerated Its Research

OpenAI published a second research acceleration report, finding that the median researcher now spends about $2,400 a day on agentic tools and the 90th percentile about $23,000, up 105 percent since September, while agent workdays per researcher rose from 0.5 to 5.7. It says agents completed research tasks without human intervention at rates of 69 percent for communication tasks and 43 percent for building, and that safety and alignment research time grew fastest of any area.

California Proposes Mandatory AI Safety Standards and Shutdown Authority

California released recommendations for its AI safety framework, including state-set baseline standards for all AI developers, mandatory independent safety testing through training, development and deployment, expanded incident reporting, verified shutdown protocols with emergency authority, and power to cap recursive self-improvement. The state built the report with an expert group and the California Office of Emergency Services and is asking Congress to adopt it as a national floor.
