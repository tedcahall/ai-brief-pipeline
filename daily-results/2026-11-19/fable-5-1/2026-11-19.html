🌐 TOP STORIES

Google Launches Gemini 4 Neon at $3/$15 per Million Tokens

New developments: Google released Gemini 4 Neon on November 18, positioned between Carbon and Hydrogen, priced at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens with a 1 million-token context window. It is rolling out in the Gemini API, Gemini for business, Antigravity and the Gemini app for paid subscribers. Artificial Analysis independently scores Neon 61 on its Intelligence Index, one point above Carbon, at about 17% lower cost per task.

Anthropic Prices IPO at $83 per Share; Nasdaq Trading Expected Today

New developments: Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83.00 per share on November 18, selling 980 million Class A shares. Trading is expected to begin on the Nasdaq Global Select Market today under the symbol ANT, with closing expected November 20. The same day, the Center for AI Safety and signatories including Steven Pinker, Thomas Wolf, Igor Babuschkin and Max Tegmark published a statement calling Anthropic "not a safe AI company."

OpenAI Says Its Models Delivered Up to 14% PPA Gains on AMD GPU Designs

OpenAI reported on November 18 that, working with AMD, its models identified power, performance and area improvements of up to 14% on AMD's flagship GPU IP in under five months by rewriting SystemVerilog RTL, and that AMD included some changes in a design sent for manufacturing. OpenAI also credits model-proposed changes with about 13% and 10% cell-area reductions in its Jalapeño chip's vector and matrix units, and is partnering with Synopsys on EDA tooling.

Palo Alto Networks to Acquire Applied Compute in $4.5 Billion All-Stock Deal

Palo Alto Networks announced on November 18 a definitive agreement to acquire Applied Compute, a post-training and inference platform for open-weight AI models, in an all-stock transaction valued at $4.5 billion, subject to adjustments. The company says the deal will let enterprises train, adapt and deploy open-weight models securely in public or sovereign clouds. Applied Compute CEO Yash Patil will become Co-Chief AI Officer; closing is expected in fiscal third quarter 2027.

California Proposes Mandatory AI Safety Standards and Emergency Shutdown Authority

Governor Gavin Newsom's office released recommendations on November 18 to strengthen California's AI safety framework. The report proposes that the state, not companies, set baseline standards all AI developers must follow, with independent safety testing across training and deployment, expanded incident reporting, verified risk-containment protocols, state authority to order an emergency shutdown, and power to cap recursive self-improvement. Gillian Hadfield, Alondra Nelson and Rob Reich were among the advisers.
