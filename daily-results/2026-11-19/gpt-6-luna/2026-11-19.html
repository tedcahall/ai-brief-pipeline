🌐 TOP STORIES

Google launches Gemini 4 Neon with one-million-token context

Google announced Gemini 4 Neon for its API, Google AI Studio, Gemini for business, the Gemini app and Antigravity. The company lists a one-million-token context window and prices of $3 per million input tokens, $0.15 per million cached input tokens and $15 per million output tokens. Google also reports benchmark rankings, including #3 on Artificial Analysis’ Intelligence Index, which should be read as reported evaluation results rather than a universal measure of model quality.

OpenAI reports faster coding-agent progress and urges public RSI metrics

OpenAI’s November 18 update describes internal measurements of coding agents and research workflows. It reports that estimated success on tasks rated as requiring two to four hours of human work rose from 23% in March to 46% in October, and says human involvement remains central. The company argues AI labs should track and publicly report indicators of recursive self-improvement; the figures are OpenAI’s own internal estimates.

Palo Alto Networks agrees to acquire Applied Compute for $4.5 billion

Palo Alto Networks announced a definitive agreement to acquire Applied Compute, a platform for post-training and inference of open-weight AI models. The stated $4.5 billion consideration is payable entirely in stock, according to the company’s investor announcement and earnings-call material. The transaction remains subject to closing conditions; it is a strategic acquisition, not a funding round.

California proposes mandatory AI safety standards and shutdown authority

A November 18 announcement from Governor Gavin Newsom outlines recommendations, not enacted new rules, for baseline AI standards, independent testing and verification, expanded incident reporting, risk-containment protocols and state emergency shutdown authority. The proposal also recommends authority to limit recursive self-improvement. The state’s announcement links to its accompanying report; implementation would require further government action.

Anthropic sets IPO price at $83 per share

Anthropic said on November 18 that it priced its initial public offering at $83 per share, with Nasdaq trading expected to begin the next day. The company’s announcement says the registration statement was declared effective by the SEC and that the offering is made by prospectus. The per-share offer price alone does not establish the company’s valuation or total proceeds.
