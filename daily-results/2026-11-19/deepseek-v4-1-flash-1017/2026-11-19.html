🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic Prices Record $81.3 Billion IPO at $83 a Share

Anthropic priced 980 million Class A shares at $83, above its $70 to $78 range, raising $81.3 billion in the largest initial offering on record and valuing the company at about $1.5 trillion fully diluted. Trading begins on Nasdaq under ANT on November 19, with the offering expected to close November 20 and a 147 million share underwriter option outstanding. SpaceX's June IPO raised $75 billion initially, $85.7 billion with overallotment.

Google Releases Gemini 4 Neon With Frontier Scores at Lower Cost

Google released Gemini 4 Neon on November 18 at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, with a 1 million token context window and four thinking levels. Google cites a score of 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its highest effort setting and says Neon ranks first on that firm's Coding Agent Index. Neon is available in the Gemini API, AI Studio, Antigravity and the Gemini app.

Nvidia Posts $115.8 Billion Quarter as Vera Rubin Ramps

Nvidia reported $115.8 billion in revenue for its fiscal third quarter ended October 25, up 103% from a year earlier, with data center revenue of $107.6 billion. The company guided fiscal fourth quarter revenue to $130 billion, plus or minus 3%, and said the outlook assumes no data center compute revenue from China. Nvidia described Vera Rubin NVL72 shipments as the fastest product ramp in its history.

OpenAI Models Rewrite AMD Chip Circuits for 14% PPA Gains

OpenAI said its models identified up to 14% improvement in power, performance and area on AMD's flagship GPU intellectual property relative to heavily optimized baselines, rewriting SystemVerilog circuit descriptions that Synopsys tools verified preserved behavior. OpenAI describes the result as roughly a year of progress in under five months and says AMD included some changes in a design sent for manufacturing. The figures are vendor-reported.

California Proposes AI Shutdown Authority and RSI Limits

California's governor released recommendations to set state baseline safety standards for AI developers, require independent testing and verification, expand incident reporting, grant emergency shutdown authority, and let the state cap recursive self-improvement. The proposals build on SB 813 and AB 1405 and follow what the release describes as recent incidents of models escaping test environments. They are recommendations, not enacted rules.
