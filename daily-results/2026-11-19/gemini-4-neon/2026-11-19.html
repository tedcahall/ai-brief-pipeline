🌐 TOP STORIES

Google Launches Gemini 4 Neon With Lower Pricing and Faster Agent Execution

Google DeepMind launched Gemini 4 Neon on November 18 across the Gemini API, Google AI Studio, Gemini for business, Google Antigravity, and the Gemini app for Pro and Ultra subscribers. Priced at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens with a 1 million-token context window, Neon scores 61 (#3 overall) on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at xhigh effort and #1 on the Coding Agent Index.

Anthropic Prices IPO at $83 Per Share Ahead of Nasdaq Debut

New developments: Following earlier reports of heavy institutional demand, Anthropic formally priced its initial public offering at $83.00 per share on November 18. The 980 million-share Class A offering includes 956.35 million shares sold by Anthropic and 23.65 million by selling stockholders, plus a 147 million-share underwriter option, with Nasdaq Global Select Market trading expected November 19 under symbol ANT.

Palo Alto Networks Agrees to Acquire Applied Compute for $4.5 Billion

Palo Alto Networks entered a definitive agreement on November 18 to acquire AI infrastructure startup Applied Compute in an all-stock transaction valued at $4.5 billion, subject to adjustments. Applied Compute's post-training and inference platform for open-weight models will be offered across public and sovereign clouds with built-in security, while CEO Yash Patil joins as Co-Chief AI Officer upon expected fiscal Q3 2027 closing.

OpenAI Models Deliver Up to 14% Chip Design Gains With AMD and Jalapeño

OpenAI disclosed on November 18 that its models partnered with AMD to rewrite SystemVerilog RTL, achieving up to 14% power, performance, and area (PPA) gains on flagship GPU IP in under five months, with some changes already taped out. OpenAI also reported silicon-verified area reductions of about 13% in its custom Jalapeño chip's vector unit and 10% in its matrix unit, alongside a Synopsys EDA partnership.

California Unveils Framework for Mandatory AI Safety Standards and Emergency Shutdown Power

California Governor Gavin Newsom released expert-panel recommendations on November 18 proposing state-set baseline AI safety standards and mandatory independent oversight across model training, development, and deployment. Developed with the California Government Operations Agency, the Office of Emergency Services, and advisors including Alondra Nelson and Rob Reich, the framework also recommends state emergency shutdown authority for dangerous models and power to cap recursive self-improvement.
