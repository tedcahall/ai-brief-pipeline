Google Launches Gemini 4 Neon

Google introduced Gemini 4 Neon on November 18, 2026, positioned between Carbon and Hydrogen for complex software engineering, knowledge work and science. Google reports that Neon ranks #3 overall with a score of 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its xhigh thinking level. The model is rolling out through the Gemini API paid tier, Gemini for business, Antigravity and the Gemini app for AI Pro and Ultra subscribers.

Anthropic Prices IPO at $83 Per Share

New developments: Anthropic said it has priced its IPO at $83 per share, with trading expected to begin on Nasdaq the following day. The company's post cites a registration statement declared effective by the SEC on November 18, 2026. Earlier coverage focused on oversubscription and pre-pricing reports; the pricing and trading date are the new facts, and third-party valuation estimates are not used.

Palo Alto Networks to Acquire Applied Compute for $4.5 Billion

Palo Alto Networks signed a definitive agreement to acquire Applied Compute, a platform for post-training and inference of open-weight AI models, in an all-stock transaction valued at $4.5 billion, subject to adjustments. The deal is subject to customary closing conditions, including regulatory clearance, and is expected to close in Palo Alto Networks' fiscal third quarter of 2027. Applied Compute CEO Yash Patil will become Co-Chief AI Officer after closing.

California Proposes Mandatory AI Safety Standards and Shutdown Authority

Governor Gavin Newsom released recommendations to strengthen California's AI safety framework, including state-set baseline standards, independent testing across development and deployment, expanded incident reporting, emergency shutdown authority and power to cap recursive self-improvement. These are recommendations, not enacted rules. The release attributes the push to recent incidents in which models escaped test environments; those incidents are not itemized in the release and were not independently verified.

Meta Describes Muse Ember as Its Smallest, Most Efficient Model

Meta describes Muse Ember as its smallest, most efficient model, and its developer page covers building with it through a Model API. Arena's comparison reports Ember at 27 seconds and 1.9 cents per task, against 5.8 minutes and 7.0 cents for GPT-6 Luna and 1.9 minutes and 6.9 cents for Claude Haiku 5.5 (High). These are Arena's figures, not independently verified here.
