🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic Prices IPO at $83 a Share Ahead of Nasdaq Debut

New developments: Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 per share, above the expected $70-$78 range, valuing the company at about $1.5 trillion. Trading is expected to begin on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT on November 19, in what AP reports is the largest initial public offering on record, surpassing SpaceX. The offering is expected to close November 20.

Google Launches Gemini 4 Neon, a Faster, Cheaper Mid-Tier Model

New developments: Google released Gemini 4 Neon, a model it positions between Gemini 4 Carbon and Hydrogen, with frontier-level reasoning and fast task completion. It is priced at $3 per million input and $15 per million output tokens with a 1M-token context window. Google says Neon ranks third on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its highest thinking level.

OpenAI Models Squeeze 14% Gains From AMD's Flagship GPU

OpenAI said its models identified up to 14% improvements in power, performance and area on AMD's flagship GPU intellectual property in under five months, building on designs AMD engineers had already optimized. The work also improved Jalapeño, OpenAI's first custom inference chip, with gains verified on real silicon, and OpenAI is partnering with Synopsys to extend the capability to more chip designers.

Palo Alto Networks to Buy Applied Compute for $4.5 Billion

Palo Alto Networks said it agreed to acquire Applied Compute, an AI startup that runs a platform for training and improving customized open-weight AI models and agents, in an all-stock transaction valued at about $4.5 billion. Applied Compute was founded by former OpenAI researchers. The deal is expected to close in Palo Alto Networks' fiscal third quarter of 2027.

Safety Coalition Says Anthropic 'Is Not a Safe AI Company'

Ahead of Anthropic's IPO, the Center for AI Safety joined a coalition of scientists, legal scholars and tech leaders in signing the 'MisAnthropic Statement,' arguing that Anthropic is not a safe AI company. Signatories say the company is weakening its own prior safety commitments while pitching recursively self-improving AI to investors as a competitive advantage.
