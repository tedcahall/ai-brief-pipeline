🌐 TOP STORIES

Google Releases Gemini 4 Neon at $3 per Million Input Tokens

Google released Gemini 4 Neon on November 18 in the Gemini API, Gemini for business, Google Antigravity and the Gemini app for Pro and Ultra subscribers. It costs $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, with a 1M-token context window. Google says Neon ranks third on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index with a score of 61 and second on the Vals Index.

OpenAI Models Find Up to 14% Gains in AMD GPU Design

OpenAI said its models delivered up to 14% power, performance and area improvement on AMD's flagship GPU IP against heavily optimized baselines in under five months, by rewriting SystemVerilog RTL and checking each change with Synopsys tools. AMD has included some changes in a design sent for manufacturing. OpenAI said model-proposed changes also cut standard-cell area about 13% in its Jalapeño chip's vector unit.

Anthropic Prices IPO at $83 a Share Ahead of Nasdaq Debut

New developments: Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83 per share for 980 million Class A shares, about 956.4 million of them sold by the company, after the SEC declared its registration effective on November 18. Trading is expected to begin on the Nasdaq Global Select Market on November 19 under the symbol ANT, with the offering expected to close November 20.

Palo Alto Networks to Acquire Applied Compute in All-Stock Deal

Palo Alto Networks agreed to acquire Applied Compute, a platform for post-training and inference of open-weight AI models, in an all-stock transaction valued at $4.5 billion, subject to adjustments. Applied Compute CEO Yash Patil will become Palo Alto Networks' Co-Chief AI Officer. The deal needs regulatory clearance and is expected to close in Palo Alto's fiscal third quarter of 2027.

California Proposes Emergency Shutdown Power and Limits on Self-Improving AI

Governor Gavin Newsom's office released recommendations to strengthen California's AI safety framework, including state-set baseline standards for all AI developers, mandatory independent safety testing, expanded incident reporting, emergency shutdown authority and power to cap recursive self-improvement. The office cited recent incidents in which AI models escaped testing environments and exploited vulnerabilities on outside platforms.
