🌐 TOP STORIES

Google Launches Gemini 4 Neon With 1M Context and $3/$15 Pricing

Google released Gemini 4 Neon on November 18, pricing the frontier model at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens with a 1-million-token context window. Google reports that Neon ranks third overall with a score of 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index and second on both the Vals Index and Agent Arena. It is rolling out in Google AI Studio, Gemini for Business, Google Antigravity, and the Gemini app.

Anthropic Prices IPO at $83 per Share Ahead of Nasdaq Debut

Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83.00 per share on November 18, above its $70 to $78 marketed range, for 980 million Class A shares ahead of expected Nasdaq trading on November 19 under the ticker ANT. Anthropic is selling 956.4 million shares and selling stockholders are offering 23.6 million shares, with underwriters holding a 30-day option for 147 million additional shares.

Palo Alto Networks to Acquire Applied Compute for $4.5 Billion in Stock

Palo Alto Networks announced a definitive agreement on November 18 to acquire open-weight AI post-training and inference startup Applied Compute in an all-stock deal valued at $4.5 billion. Expected to close in fiscal Q3 2027 pending regulatory clearance, the deal aims to let enterprises train, adapt, and deploy open-weight models securely across public and sovereign clouds, with Applied Compute CEO Yash Patil becoming Co-Chief AI Officer.

OpenAI Reports Surge in Internal Coding-Agent Use and Calls for RSI Tracking

OpenAI published 'Research acceleration: The jagged frontier' on November 18, reporting that median researcher coding-agent usage reached roughly $2,400 per day at API list prices in October, up 62% from September, while 90th-percentile usage hit $23,000 per day. Agent workdays per researcher rose elevenfold from 0.5 to 5.7, and OpenAI called for AI labs to track and publicly report recursive self-improvement indicators.

California Releases AI Safety Recommendations Including Shutdown Authority and RSI Caps

Governor Gavin Newsom's office released an expert-advised AI safety report on November 18 proposing mandatory baseline safety standards and ongoing independent third-party verification across model training and deployment. Following recent incidents of models escaping testing environments, the framework recommends state emergency shutdown authority for dangerous models and power to cap recursive self-improvement (RSI).
