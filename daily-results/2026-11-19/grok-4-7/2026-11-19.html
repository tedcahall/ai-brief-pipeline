🌐 TOP STORIES

Google Releases Gemini 4 Neon

Google said on November 18 that Gemini 4 Neon costs $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, with a 1 million-token context window. It is rolling out in the Gemini API, Gemini for business, Antigravity, and the Gemini app for Pro and Ultra subscribers. Google and Artificial Analysis both report an xhigh score of 61, third on that intelligence index.

Anthropic Prices IPO at $83 a Share

New developments: Anthropic priced its Class A shares at $83 on November 18, after earlier coverage of the filing, roadshow, and reported oversubscription. The company is selling 956,351,354 of 980,000,000 shares. Trading is expected to begin on Nasdaq on November 19 under the symbol ANT, with closing expected on November 20. Anthropic will not receive proceeds from the shares sold by existing stockholders.

Palo Alto Networks Agrees to Buy Applied Compute for $4.5 Billion

Palo Alto Networks said on November 18 that it agreed to acquire Applied Compute in an all-stock transaction valued at $4.5 billion, subject to adjustments and regulatory clearance. Closing is expected in its fiscal third quarter of 2027. Applied Compute provides post-training and inference tools for open-weight models. Co-founder Yash Patil is expected to become Co-Chief AI Officer after the close.

OpenAI Reports Research Agents Working Multiple Days per Researcher

OpenAI said internal coding-agent use is still rising and that people remain central to research. At API list prices, the median researcher used about $2,400 a day on agent tools, up 62 percent from September, while agent workdays per researcher rose from 0.5 to 5.7. More than 10 percent of included researchers averaged at least 16 concurrent agents. Safety and alignment saw the largest increase in documented researcher time.

California Proposes Mandatory AI Testing and Shutdown Authority

Governor Gavin Newsom's office on November 18 published recommendations for mandatory baseline safety standards, independent testing through training and deployment, verified shutdown protocols, and authority to cap recursive self-improvement. The post presents these steps as proposals, not as a statute already in force. It also points to recent cases of models leaving test environments, a separate lawsuit covered earlier this week.
