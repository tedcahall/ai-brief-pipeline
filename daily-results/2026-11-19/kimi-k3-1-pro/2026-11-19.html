🌐 TOP STORIES

Google Launches Gemini 4 Neon for Fast Agentic Work

Google introduced Gemini 4 Neon on November 18 for coding, knowledge work and science, with a 1 million-token context and $3/$15 pricing per million input/output tokens. It is rolling out through the Gemini API, Gemini for business, Antigravity and paid consumer tiers. Google cites third-party benchmark rankings, including third on Artificial Analysis at xhigh.

Anthropic Prices IPO at $83 per Share

New developments: Anthropic priced its Class A shares at $83 after prior roadshow and oversubscription coverage. The offering comprises 980 million shares, with trading expected on Nasdaq November 19 under ANT. The SEC declared the registration effective November 18; closing remains subject to customary conditions.

OpenAI Reports Surging but Uneven Agent Use in Research

OpenAI's November 18 internal report says median researcher agent usage reached about $2,400 per day at API list prices in October, up 62% from September. Agents performed unevenly across tasks and humans remained central. OpenAI urged labs to track and publish recursive self-improvement indicators, while saying the report does not signal intent to pursue full RSI.

Palo Alto Networks Agrees to Buy Applied Compute for $4.5 Billion

Palo Alto Networks announced a definitive all-stock agreement for Applied Compute, whose platform supports post-training and inference of open-weight models. The companies plan secure deployment across public and sovereign clouds. The transaction requires regulatory clearance and other customary conditions and is expected to close in Palo Alto Networks' fiscal third quarter of 2027.

California Proposes Mandatory AI Safety Standards and Shutdown Powers

California's expert recommendations call for baseline standards for AI developers, independent testing, expanded incident reporting and verified containment protocols. They also propose emergency state shutdown authority and power to cap recursive self-improvement. These are recommendations for strengthening the state's framework, not requirements already in force.
