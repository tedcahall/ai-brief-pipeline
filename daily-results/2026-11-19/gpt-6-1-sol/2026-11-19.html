🌐 TOP STORIES

Google Launches Gemini 4 Neon With 1M Context at $3/$15 per Million Tokens

Google released Gemini 4 Neon for coding, knowledge work and science, with a one-million-token context window and API pricing of $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens. Artificial Analysis places its xhigh setting third on its Intelligence Index at 61. Access is rolling out through the API, Gemini app and Antigravity, with subscription requirements varying by surface.

InclusionAI Opens Ming-3.0-Audio-Flash Under the MIT License

InclusionAI released MIT-licensed weights for Ming-3.0-audio-flash, a native audio-language model supporting transcription, audio reasoning and optional synthesized speech. Its model card specifies 126 billion total parameters with 6.5 billion activated per inference step and Chinese and English support. The team reports 99.8% on Big Bench Audio; that figure is attributed to its published model card, not independently reproduced here.

Cloudflare Releases Open-Weight Clairic and Clairic-Flash Extraction Models

Cloudflare moved Clairic beyond preview and released open weights for its 27B model and new 9B Clairic-flash variant. Both run on Workers AI and support structured extraction from text, images and PDFs, retaining confidence scores and source spans. Cloudflare reports improved extraction in internal evaluations, explicitly cautioning that its results are self-reported rather than public independent benchmarks.

OpenAI Reports Rising Agent Use but Uneven Research Automation

OpenAI published new internal measurements of research acceleration, reporting median agent use worth about $2,400 per researcher per day at API list prices. Success without human intervention varied sharply: 79% for monitoring runs, versus 25% for inference and serving code. The report emphasizes continuing human oversight and changing task selection, so these observations do not establish autonomous recursive self-improvement.

Palo Alto Networks Agrees to Buy Applied Compute for $4.5 Billion in Stock

Palo Alto Networks agreed to acquire Applied Compute in an all-stock transaction valued at $4.5 billion, subject to adjustments. Applied Compute supplies post-training and inference infrastructure for open-weight models. The buyer plans to bring that platform and research team into its enterprise security offerings. Closing is expected in its fiscal third quarter of 2027, subject to regulatory clearance and other conditions.
