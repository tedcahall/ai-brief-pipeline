📊 AI INDUSTRY PULSE

Google introduced Gemini 4 Neon, a new model aimed at coding and knowledge work, while DeepMind expanded access to its Genie 4 world generator. OpenAI described AI-assisted chip design work with AMD and published new internal measurements of research acceleration.

🌐 TOP STORIES

Google Launches Gemini 4 Neon

Google introduced Gemini 4 Neon for coding, analytical and scientific workflows. The company lists a one-million-token context window and API prices of $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens. Google says the model is rolling out through its API and selected paid Gemini products.

OpenAI Details AI-Assisted Chip Design With AMD

OpenAI says its models helped improve chip designs with AMD, reporting up to 14% improvement in power, performance and area against optimized GPU design baselines. AMD has included some changes in a design sent for manufacturing. OpenAI also reports silicon-proven area reductions in blocks of its own Jalapeño chip.

OpenAI Reports Uneven AI Research Acceleration

OpenAI published internal measures of how its researchers use coding agents and how those tools affect the research process. The company describes growing agent use alongside continued human involvement, while cautioning that its report presents only a partial picture. The results are internal observations, not an independent measure across the industry.

Genie 4 Reaches Project Genie Users

Google DeepMind says Genie 4 is now available in Project Genie, bringing native audio, real-time editing and an agent for creating interactive virtual worlds. Google CEO Sundar Pichai said eligible Google AI Ultra subscribers can use it. This is a new availability step following the earlier research preview.

California Proposes New AI Safety Oversight

California released recommendations for mandatory AI safety standards, independent testing and emergency shutdown authority. The proposal also calls for authority to cap certain recursive self-improvement activities. These are recommendations for future oversight, not a claim that the proposed powers are already law.
