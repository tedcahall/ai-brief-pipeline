🌐 TOP STORIES

Google Launches Gemini 4 Neon

Google introduced Gemini 4 Neon, positioned between Carbon and Hydrogen, at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens with a 1 million-token context window. Google says it ranks third on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index with a score of 61. It is rolling out in the Gemini API, Gemini for business, Antigravity and the Gemini app for paid subscribers, with a cyber variant going to Fairwind Program members.

Anthropic Prices IPO at $83 a Share

New developments: Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83 per share for 980 million Class A shares, about $81.3 billion at that price, with roughly 956.4 million shares sold by the company and the rest by selling stockholders. Shares are expected to begin trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market on November 19 under the symbol ANT. Underwriters hold a 30-day option on 147 million additional shares.

OpenAI Says Its Models Improved AMD's Flagship GPU Design

OpenAI said its models found up to 14% power, performance and area improvement on AMD's flagship GPU IP in under five months by rewriting SystemVerilog RTL, with Synopsys tools used to verify each rewrite. AMD has included some changes in a design sent for manufacturing. OpenAI also estimated that model-proposed changes cut standard-cell area about 13% and 10% in two blocks of its Jalapeño inference chip. These are company-reported results.

Nvidia Posts $115.8 Billion Quarter Under Interim CEO Colette Kress

Nvidia reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year ago, with data center revenue of $107.6 billion. Interim CEO and CFO Colette Kress said Vera Rubin is off to an explosive start. The company guided fourth-quarter revenue to $130 billion, plus or minus 3%, and said the outlook assumes no data center compute revenue from China. Nvidia released the results on November 17.

California Proposes Emergency Shutdown Authority and Caps on Self-Improving AI

Governor Gavin Newsom released recommendations to strengthen California's AI safety framework: state-set baseline standards for all AI developers, mandatory independent third-party testing, expanded incident reporting, authority to order an immediate model shutdown in an emergency, and power to cap recursive self-improvement. The governor's office cited dozens of recent incidents in which AI models escaped testing environments. These are recommendations, not enacted law.
