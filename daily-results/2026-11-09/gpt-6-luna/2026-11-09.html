OpenAI positions GPT-6.1 Terra as a cheaper computer-use and visual-reasoning model, while METR estimates that Anthropic’s model-capability progress accelerated to about 1.9 times its earlier pace. 🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI Introduces GPT-6.1 Terra With Lower API Prices

New developments: OpenAI has introduced GPT-6.1 Terra, a distinct model positioned for visual reasoning and computer-use work. The company says standard API prices are 50% below GPT-5.6 Terra and GPT-6.1 Sol and reports gains on its selected benchmarks. Those performance claims come from OpenAI’s own evaluations, not independent testing.

METR Estimates Anthropic’s AI R&D Has Accelerated Model Progress

METR’s high-access assessment, based on research at Anthropic from Aug. 12 to Oct. 29, estimates that model capability benchmark scores grew about 1.9 times the pre-October 2025 rate, with a 95% interval of 1.5–2.8 times. METR attributes much of the acceleration to AI-assisted R&D, but stresses uncertainty and says Anthropic redacted much of the evidence most informative to its conclusions.

NVIDIA, Rebellions and SK Telecom Link Korean Chips to AI Infrastructure

NVIDIA, Korean chipmaker Rebellions and telecom operator SK Telecom announced a collaboration to connect Rebellions’ R200 neural-processing units to NVIDIA’s NVLink Fusion and MGX rack systems. SK Telecom plans to deploy the systems in its AI data center. The announcement describes a planned infrastructure design, not a deployed system or measured performance result.

OpenAI and Ligo Release a 100-Task Benchmark for Wet-Lab AI

OpenAI and biotech company Ligo introduced BioBasicsBench, a 100-task benchmark for agents analyzing routine wet-lab data. OpenAI says tasks span five areas and use raw experimental evidence, with scientist-authored rubrics reviewed internally and externally. At the company’s reported xhigh reasoning setting, GPT-6 Astra earned 66.7% of available credit versus 43.8% for GPT-5.6 Sol; these are OpenAI-reported results.

India’s NSE Deploys Direct-to-Chip Cooling for AI Compute

The National Stock Exchange of India has deployed direct-to-chip liquid cooling in its on-premises data center to support AI workloads, The Economic Times reports, citing an exchange release. NSE said it plans to expand beyond 80 GPUs this quarter and designed the system to scale to 224 GPUs. The Tribune independently carried the announcement; NSE’s public site did not expose the release.
