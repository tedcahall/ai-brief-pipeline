🌐 TOP STORIES

Nvidia Board Opens Independent Investigation Into Foreign-Intelligence Reporting on CEO Jensen Huang

Nvidia said a committee of independent directors, assisted by outside counsel, will investigate recent reporting that CEO Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. The board says it has no reason to believe national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised, and that Huang is cooperating. The Associated Press reported China's foreign ministry denied the allegation on Monday and that Nvidia shares fell about 5% in pre-market trading.

Artificial Analysis Launches Decision Index v2 With 45% Private Datasets

Artificial Analysis released Decision Index v2, raising the private-dataset share from 25% to 45% and adding a Decision Score that rewards correct answers and calibrated confidence. Drex Max v1.1 from Nace AI leads decision models at 73, within one point of GLiDE v1.2 and Mammoth 27B. Claude Haiku 5.5 (Max) scores 77 and GPT-6 Luna (Max) 76, with the latter costing $1,530 per million tasks versus $102 for Drex Max.

llama-modchip Claims DeepSeek-V4-Flash Runs on a 64GB CPU-Only Machine

The Apache-2.0 llama-modchip project says it runs DeepSeek-V4-Flash, a 284B-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 13.17B active parameters, at 256K context on a 64GB CPU-only machine using a 1.71 bits-per-weight format. It reports 44.4 tokens per second prefill and 9.1 to 14.7 decode on a 16-core Zen 5 chip. AI Weekly notes all figures come from the author and have not been independently reproduced.

Musk Promises Grok Bot Fixes as Subscribers Report Usage Limits Draining Quickly

Elon Musk said fixes for Grok Bot token efficiency and behavior were underway after a subscriber reported a $200 plan lasting 14 hours that previously lasted seven days; that usage figure is the subscriber's own and unverified. Musk later said fixes were being implemented overnight. He had said on November 1 that serving costs were unsustainably high, and no higher weekly allowance has been announced, Tesla North reports.

Asahi Linux Moves to GPL, Citing AI-Assisted Code Laundering

Asahi Linux announced in its Linux 7.3 progress report that all repositories are moving from the MIT license to GPLv2 or later. The project cited attempts to exploit permissive licensing, including lifting unreleased code from contributors' personal forks and rewriting code with AI to hide its origin. It's FOSS notes the report names no one and that Asahi bans AI-generated contributions.
