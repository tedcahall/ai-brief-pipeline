🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI Fixes GPT-6.2 Billing Mistake, So Users Hit Limits Up to 40% Faster

OpenAI said a billing mistake at GPT-6.2's launch incorrectly discounted Astra and Sol usage for most users. Now fixed, the same work uses more of a plan's allowance: users reach limits 40% faster on GPT-6.2 Astra and 25% faster on Sol, though OpenAI says both still go further than 6 Astra and 6.1 Sol. It is adding one banked reset for Go, Plus, Pro and Business users and will not charge for past usage.

Anthropic Researchers Shrink the Riemann Hypothesis Lean Proof by More Than 99%

A new grh-lean repository proves the same five theorems of the November 20 Riemann Hypothesis proof in about 76,000 lines of Lean, down from 17.4 million, a 99.56% reduction. Heise cites an independent analysis counting about 113,000 lines including comments and blanks, still more than 99% smaller, a step toward making the giant proof practical to review.

Sand AI's 480B-Parameter MAGI-2 Takes the Image-to-Video Crown at a New Price Frontier

Sand AI's MAGI-2, released November 24, is a 480B-parameter (about 28B active) Multi-Head Latent MoE video model generating up to 30 seconds of 1080p 25 fps audio-video with omni-reference across up to 20 media items. Artificial Analysis ranks it #1 in Image to Video, statistically tied with Dreamina Seedance 2.5, at $4.80 per minute, about 7x cheaper. It is a proprietary hosted API at launch, with stated intent to open-source.

Moonshot AI Opens Kimi for Science With Free API Credits for Researchers Worldwide

Moonshot AI opened applications on November 27 for Kimi for Science, giving selected teams free Kimi API credits, access to its latest models and long-term collaboration opportunities. The first round, closing December 8, covers theoretical and experimental research in fundamental science and engineering; third-party reports put credits at up to $20,000, roughly 7 billion tokens, per team.

Canada Expands Its AI Compute Access Fund to $1 Billion With Sector-Based Rounds

Ottawa is adding $700 million to the AI Compute Access Fund, bringing the program to $1 billion to subsidize compute for small and medium-sized Canadian firms. The money will flow through sector-based application rounds starting with health, with more than $55 million already committed to 45 companies and all funding calls planned to launch by the end of 2027.
