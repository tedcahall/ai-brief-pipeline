🌐 TOP STORIES

Ant Ling Releases Open-Weight Ming-Image-0.2-Design

Ant Ling has released Ming-Image-0.2-Design, a 6B-parameter visual-design model aimed at product imagery, interface drafts and spatial renderings. Its model card describes improvements to composition, lighting and text. The company says it ranks first among open-weight models on Artificial Analysis’s text-to-image leaderboard; the separate upgraded layer-decomposition model is planned for next week, not released alongside it.

Tencent Open-Sources DFly2 for Faster Long-Context Inference

Tencent released DFly2 weights, training code and a technical report through AngelSpec. The drafter reuses the target model’s sparse cache and selected positions rather than maintaining its own cache. The researchers report 62% and 81% higher throughput on two 64K-context tests of DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash at matched per-user decode speed. These are reported benchmark results, not universal serving guarantees.

Kimi Offers API Credits for Scientific Research

Moonshot’s Kimi team announced a worldwide scientific-research program offering selected teams free API credits, access to its latest models and opportunities for longer-term collaboration. The invitation covers theoretical and experimental work in fundamental science and engineering. The announcement does not specify a guaranteed credit allocation for every applicant; access is awarded through selection.

SemiAnalysis Reports Trainium3 Price-Performance Result

SemiAnalysis says Amazon’s Trainium3 UltraServer submission delivered 2.28 times better performance per dollar than Nvidia’s GB300 NVL72 on its agentic inference benchmark. The result is attributed to the benchmark operator and its tested configuration, not a claim about every workload. SemiAnalysis also said it expects to benchmark Trainium4; that future comparison is not yet a reported result.

Canada Adds C$700 Million to AI Compute Access Fund

Canada announced an additional C$700 million for its AI Compute Access Fund, bringing the program to C$1 billion. The expansion opens a health and life sciences call and supports access to computing resources for developing and commercializing AI products. This is public compute-access support, not a private startup funding round or a US-dollar investment figure.
