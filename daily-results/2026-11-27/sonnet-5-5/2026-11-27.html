🌐 TOP STORIES

SemiAnalysis Says Trainium and TPU Beat NVIDIA on Performance per Dollar in InferenceX

SemiAnalysis said Google's TPU and Amazon's Trainium are now on its InferenceX and AgentX benchmarks, reporting 1.2x to 2.5x the performance per dollar of NVIDIA GPUs. Amazon's first Trainium3 UltraServer submission, running Z.ai's 744-billion-parameter GLM model, showed up to 2.28x better performance per dollar than GB300 NVL72. SemiAnalysis labels the Trainium3 figures an official preview that may change.

Ant Group Open-Sources Ming-Image-0.2-Design, Top Open-Weight Image Model on Artificial Analysis

Ant Group's Ant Ling team released Ming-Image-0.2-Design, a 6-billion-parameter text-to-image model with MIT-licensed weights on Hugging Face. It scores 1,095 Elo on the Artificial Analysis text-to-image leaderboard, first among open-weight models and 24th overall. i10x notes the score is statistically tied with Alibaba's paid Qwen-Image-3.0-Pro and that the model needs an 80 GB GPU; the Layer editing variant is due next week.

Tencent Open-Sources DFly2, a Speculative Decoding Drafter for Hy4 Flash

Tencent open-sourced DFly2, a speculative-decoding drafter for its Hy4 flash model that shares the target model's KV cache and top-k token selection, so drafting cost stays flat across context lengths. Tencent claims up to 4.2x speedup on long-context and agentic workloads and about 30% faster agentic decoding than the same-recipe DSpark. These figures are Tencent's own and were not independently verified.

Canada Adds $700 Million to AI Compute Access Fund, Raising It to $1 Billion

Canada's AI minister Evan Solomon announced a $700 million expansion of the AI Compute Access Fund on November 26, bringing its total to $1 billion after an initial $300 million. The government program offsets cloud-compute costs for Canadian organizations and opens a health and life sciences call. Unite.AI reports it covers up to 80% of costs from sovereign providers and 50% from Canadian-based providers.

Japan Weighs Safety Duties and Penalties for Major AI Providers Under Revised AI Act

Japan is preparing revisions to its AI Act that would require large AI providers to put safety measures in place, the Yomiuri Shimbun reported, as relayed by Reuters and Yonhap. Likely covered firms include OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Alibaba and DeepSeek. Noncompliance would bring a recommendation, a corrective order and then penalties, whose details are undecided. The government aims to submit a bill next year.
