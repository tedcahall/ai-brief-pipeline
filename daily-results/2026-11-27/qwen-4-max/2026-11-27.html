🌐 TOP STORIES

SemiAnalysis's GDPx Benchmark Puts OpenAI's Astra on Top

SemiAnalysis's GDPx benchmark, which scores models on 206 economically valuable tasks across 11 domains, ranks OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra first at 19.3%, ahead of Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2 at 16.4%. Google's Gemini 4 Neon, popular on social media, places sixth at 10.1%. OpenAI dominates the cost-performance frontier, and Alibaba's Qwen 4 Max leads Chinese models at 7.4%.

Sand AI's MAGI-2 Video Model Ties Seedance at a Fraction of the Cost

Sand AI released MAGI-2, a 480-billion-parameter video model with about 28 billion active parameters that generates up to 30 seconds of 1080p audio-video. Artificial Analysis ranks it first in image-to-video, statistically tied with ByteDance's Seedance 2.5, at $0.08 per second versus $34.12 per minute for Seedance, roughly seven times cheaper. Sand AI says open-source releases will follow.

Anthropic Researcher Uses Claude to Shrink the Riemann Hypothesis Proof

Anthropic researcher Luke Sernau used Claude to compress Levent Alpöge's Claude-assisted Riemann hypothesis proof from 17.4 million lines of Lean to about 76,000, a 99.56% reduction. A readable edition runs about 300,000 lines. The proof has passed machine checks only; no mathematician has read it through, and the Clay Institute still lists the problem as open.

Ant's Ling Team Open-Sources the Ming-Image-0.2-Design Image Model

Ant Group's Ling team open-sourced Ming-Image-0.2-Design, a 6-billion-parameter design-focused image model it says ranks first among open-weight models on Artificial Analysis's text-to-image leaderboard with 1,095 Elo. It improves composition, lighting and text rendering for e-commerce, UI and spatial design, and pairs with Ling-3.2-flash to build interactive pages. A layer-decomposition variant is planned for next week.

Canada Expands Its Sovereign AI Compute Fund and Opens a Health-Science Call

Canada's government expanded its AI Compute Access Fund by C$700 million to C$1 billion, opening a first sector call for health and life sciences. The program offsets cloud-compute costs for Canadian organizations and favors sovereign providers, and announced more than C$52 million for 45 companies. It supports Ottawa's goal of bringing 500 megawatts of sovereign AI compute online.
