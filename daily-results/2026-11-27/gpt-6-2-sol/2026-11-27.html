🌐 TOP STORIES

KRAFTON Releases Raon-1 Mini With Open Weights and a 256K Context Window

South Korean game maker KRAFTON released the full version of its Apache 2.0 language model on November 25. Raon-1 mini uses 4.3 billion of its 28.8 billion parameters per token and offers six reasoning settings. KRAFTON says the NVFP4 format can handle the full 256K context in about 20 GiB of GPU memory for a single request. A separate DFlash2 draft model is available to speed up generation.

Ant’s InclusionAI Releases Open Weights for Ming-Image-0.2-Design

InclusionAI released an MIT-licensed, 6-billion-parameter image model for tasks such as drafting user interfaces and creating product graphics. Its team says the update improves composition, lighting and text rendering, and reports a first-place result among open-weight models on Artificial Analysis’s text-to-image benchmark. The separate model that breaks images into editable layers was announced but is not part of this release.

Microsoft Finds Larger Coding-Agent Patches Aren’t Necessarily More Wasteful

Microsoft researchers examined 7,660 successful coding-agent runs across two benchmarks, reverting pieces of each patch and rerunning tests to see what was needed. On SWE-bench Verified, 61% of extra edits added tests and 13% added documentation; on DeepSWE, most extra edits were code. Agent code was at least as necessary on average as developer code under this method, but some unnecessary edits still changed behavior.

Canada Adds C$700 Million to Its AI Compute Access Fund

Canada opened a new phase of its AI Compute Access Fund on November 26, increasing the program from C$300 million to C$1 billion. The first call targets health and life sciences projects, and the expanded program prioritizes Canadian computing capacity. Separately, the government announced more than C$52 million in support for 45 companies under the initial phase.

Bank of England Warns AI Financing Could Spread Market Stress

The Bank of England estimated that major cloud operators have just over US$4 trillion in potential exposure, including debt and off-balance-sheet contractual commitments. Its November financial stability report says a drop in expected AI revenues could put those arrangements under strain, with financing stress at one company affecting similar projects. The figure measures potential exposure, not debt alone or forecast losses.
