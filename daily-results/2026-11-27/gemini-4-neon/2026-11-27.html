🌐 TOP STORIES

Tencent Hunyuan Open-Sources DFly2 Speculative Decoding Drafter for Long-Context Agents

Tencent Hunyuan open-sourced DFly2, a block-parallel speculative decoding drafter designed for natively sparse models such as Hy4 flash, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, and GLM-5.3-Flash. According to the authors' arXiv preprint and release notes, DFly2 reuses the target model's KV cache and sparse attention index with zero extra KV memory, delivering up to 4.2x speedup on long-context and agentic workloads and 10.0% to 31.5% more accepted tokens per step than DFlash and DSpark baselines.

Bank of England Warns Hyperscalers' $4 Trillion AI Exposure Amplifies Global Market Risks

The Bank of England warned in its November 26 Financial Stability Report that hyperscalers' financial exposure to AI infrastructure has surpassed $4 trillion, deepening interconnections across global markets. The central bank described the rapid expansion of AI financing as increasingly complex, circular, and opaque, noting that hyperscalers have raised over $300 billion in bond debt this year alongside off-balance-sheet deals, while private credit funds grow more selective over credit quality.

Kimi Launches Scientific Research Program Offering Up to $20,000 in Free API Credits

Kimi launched its Kimi for Science program on November 27, offering selected research teams worldwide up to $20,000 in free Kimi API credits, roughly 7 billion tokens for model usage rather than cash funding. Open to natural sciences, engineering, and interdisciplinary teams at all career stages, the initiative also provides early access to new models, technical support, and long-term collaboration opportunities, with first-round applications due December 8.

Canada Expands AI Compute Access Fund by C$700 Million to C$1 Billion

Artificial Intelligence and Digital Innovation Minister Evan Solomon announced a C$700 million expansion of Canada's AI Compute Access Fund on November 26, lifting total funding to C$1 billion alongside more than C$52 million in initial-phase awards for 45 companies. The renewed program opens its first sector call for health and life sciences and reimburses up to 80% of compute costs from Canadian-owned sovereign providers, supporting Canada's 500-megawatt sovereign compute target.

Japan Weighs Mandatory AI Safety Duties and Penalties for Global Providers

Japan is drafting revisions to its AI Act that would impose mandatory safety rules and penalties on major AI providers operating at scale in the country, including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Alibaba, and DeepSeek, according to Yomiuri Shimbun reporting cited by Reuters-Yonhap. Proposals include blocking cyberattack and harmful-substance instructions, reporting exploitable flaws, verifying users of high-capability cyber models, and issuing corrective orders before submitting a bill to parliament next year.
