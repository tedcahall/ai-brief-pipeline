🌐 TOP STORIES

Ant Ling Open-Sources Ming-Image-0.2-Design

Ant Ling released Ming-Image-0.2-Design, a 6B image-generation model with MIT-licensed weights and improvements aimed at text, lighting and complex layouts. Its model card documents product imagery, website drafts and interior renderings. The lab announced the release of Design now, while the upgraded Design-Layer decomposition model remains planned for later, rather than already available.

Tencent Releases DFly2 for Faster Hy4 Flash Decoding

Tencent announced open-source DFly2, a speculative-decoding drafter that reuses Hy4 flash’s key-value cache and sparse token selection instead of maintaining an extra draft cache. The company reports up to 4.2 times faster decoding on selected workloads. That peak is not an end-to-end latency guarantee: prompt processing and deployment conditions still affect the speed users experience.

Trainium3 Posts a New Agentic Inference Price-Performance Result

SemiAnalysis says Amazon’s Trainium3 UltraServer submission achieved 2.28 times the performance per dollar of NVIDIA’s GB300 NVL72 in its agentic inference benchmark. This is the benchmark operator’s reported comparison, not evidence of a universal hardware advantage. Teams choosing accelerators still need to check model, concurrency, latency target and cost assumptions against their own workloads.

Kimi Opens API-Credit Support for Scientific Research

Moonshot’s Kimi for Science program is accepting researchers in natural sciences, engineering and related fields. Its official page offers selected projects up to $20,000 in API credits, with awards depending on research needs and review. Support can include technical help and early model access. These are model-usage credits, not cash grants or a new model release.

Canada Adds C$700 Million to Its AI Compute Access Fund

Canada announced a C$700 million expansion of its AI Compute Access Fund, bringing the program’s total to C$1 billion. The next phase opens a health and life sciences call and encourages domestic, sovereign compute capacity. Separately, the government announced more than C$52 million for 45 companies under the initial phase; those awards should not be confused with the new expansion.
