🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic Researchers Use Claude to Shrink Riemann Hypothesis Proof to About 76,000 Lines

New developments: Anthropic researcher Luke Sernau published grh-lean, a Lean 4 proof of the Riemann Hypothesis and the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions, produced with Anthropic colleagues and Claude. Its compact edition proves the same five theorems as Levent Alpöge's 17.4-million-line November 20 release in about 76,000 lines, beside a 300,000-line readable edition. Heise reports MIT's Andrew Sutherland and Waterloo's Michael Rubinstein independently verified both versions.

GPT-6.2 Astra Tops SemiAnalysis's GDPx Benchmark at 19.3%, Ahead of Claude Fable 5.2

New developments: a month after GPT-6.2 launched, SemiAnalysis updated GDPx, its 206-task benchmark across 11 frontier domains built with 14 data partners. OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra leads the main leaderboard at 19.3%, ahead of Claude Fable 5.2 at 16.4%; Gemini 4 Carbon ranks fifth at 10.9% and Gemini 4 Neon sixth at 10.1%. SemiAnalysis says safety classifiers depress Astra and Fable scores in cyber, biology and alignment, and that Astra remains weak at software engineering.

Tencent Open-Sources DFly2, the Speculative Decoding Drafter Behind Hy4 Flash

Tencent's Hunyuan AI Infra team open-sourced DFly2, the speculative decoding drafter that ships with Hy4 flash, releasing weights, training code and a technical report. DFly2 reads the target model's sparse KV cache at the positions its indexer already selected, so it keeps no cache of its own and drafting cost stays flat at any context length. Tencent reports speedups of up to 4.2x on long-context and agentic workloads, with new drafters for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and GLM-5.3-Flash.

Moonshot Opens Kimi for Science With Free API Credits for Researchers Worldwide

Moonshot AI launched Kimi for Science, a program offering free Kimi API credits to researchers in the natural sciences, engineering and related interdisciplinary fields. Selected projects receive up to $20,000 in credits, roughly 7 billion tokens, plus technical support, early access to new models and collaboration opportunities; the credits cover model usage, not cash funding. Researchers at all career stages may apply, and the first-round deadline is December 8.

Japan Weighs AI Act Revisions Imposing Safety Duties and Penalties on Frontier Providers

Japan's government is preparing revisions to its AI Act that would require large AI providers, including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Alibaba and DeepSeek, to adopt safety measures, Seoul Economic Daily reported citing the Yomiuri Shimbun. Measures under review include blocking answers about cyberattack methods and weapons, verifying users of highly capable systems and mandatory reporting of exploitable flaws, enforced through recommendations, corrective orders and penalties. The bill is slated for next year's ordinary Diet session.
