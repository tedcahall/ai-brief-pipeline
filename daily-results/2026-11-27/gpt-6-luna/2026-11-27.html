🌐 TOP STORIES

Canada Adds C$700 Million to AI Compute Access Fund, Bringing It to C$1 Billion

On November 26, Canada announced an additional C$700 million for the AI Compute Access Fund, bringing its total to C$1 billion after an initial C$300 million allocation. The expanded program opens a targeted call for health and life sciences proposals and encourages use of Canadian-owned or operated compute. Separately, the government announced more than C$52 million for 45 companies under the first phase, bringing commitments to 89 firms.

Qwen Expands Audio Services to 59 ASR, 65 TTS and 53 Realtime Languages

Alibaba’s Qwen team announced on November 26 that three hosted Qwen-Audio-3.1 services now cover more languages: ASR in 59, text-to-speech in 65, and realtime conversation in 53. The post also gives counts of supported Chinese dialects for each service. This is an availability and language-coverage update; Qwen did not provide new benchmarks or claim an architectural change in the announcement.

NVIDIA Researchers Test Letting Models Manage Their Own Context

In a November 25 preprint, NVIDIA researchers compare harness-triggered context compaction with a context API that lets a model choose what and when to compact. On OfficeQA Pro v2 at a 32K-token budget, GPT 6.1 scores 56% with model-driven context management versus 21% in the comparison setup; Kimi uses up to 41% fewer input tokens. The authors also report up to 15% lower prefill compute from KV-cache reuse, while noting that models vary in how they use the API.

OS-Omni Benchmark Finds Large Cross-Platform Gaps in Computer-Using Agents

The OS-Omni preprint evaluates computer-using agents on 300 workflows across Windows, Ubuntu, macOS, iOS, Android and the web. Its reported best overall model reaches 68.5% success against a 77.3% human reference, but performance varies sharply by platform, including stronger results on Linux and weaker results on mobile systems. The authors emphasize long workflows averaging about 68 interaction steps and identify grounding, state tracking, persistence, and platform timing as error sources.

Shanghai AI Lab’s Sage-Med Reports 58.26% Across 31 Public Medical Benchmarks

A preprint introduces Sage-Med-Preview, a medical multimodal model with 35 billion total parameters and 3 billion activated parameters. The authors report an average score of 58.26% across 31 public benchmarks and describe comparisons with other open models on that set; they characterize results against a proprietary model as approaching it on selected tasks. The paper also says complex medical reasoning and multi-step clinical workflows remain substantial challenges.
