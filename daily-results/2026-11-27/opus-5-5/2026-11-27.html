🌐 TOP STORIES

Sber Releases Open-Weight GigaChat 4 With a 1M-Token Context Window

Russia's state-controlled Sber released two open-weight GigaChat 4 models on November 26: a 708B-parameter flagship with 41B active parameters and a 37B version, both supporting 1,048,576-token context. The flagship mixes Gated DeltaNet linear attention with DeepSeek Sparse Attention. Sber's own table shows it trailing DeepSeek V4.1 Flash on DeepSWE, 35.4% to 74.3%, and independent benchmark results are not yet available.

Switzerland Confirms Its First Cyberattacks Run by AI Agents

Switzerland's Federal Office for Cybersecurity (BACS) confirmed five recent cyberattacks that used AI agents, the country's first such cases, director Florian Schütz told broadcaster SRF. Four of the attacks broke into their targets, including two of the three aimed at critical infrastructure operators; no major disruptions were reported and the attackers are unidentified. The agency says freely reachable AI attack tools have nearly doubled since October.

Bank of England Puts Hyperscalers' AI Exposure Above $4 Trillion

In its November Financial Stability Report, the Bank of England said hyperscalers' AI-related financial exposure has grown to more than $4 trillion and that AI financing is "increasingly complex, circular and opaque," deepening links across the global financial system. It warned that debt and off-balance-sheet commitments could come under strain if expectations for future AI revenue were revised down.

Tencent Open-Sources DFly2 Drafter for Faster Long-Context Inference

Tencent's Hunyuan AI Infra team open-sourced DFly2, a speculative decoding drafter that reuses the target model's sparse KV cache instead of keeping its own, so drafting memory does not grow with context. Tencent says it delivers up to 4.2x speedups for Hy4 flash on long-context and agentic workloads, and reports up to 81% more throughput for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash at 64K context. It works with vLLM and SGLang.

Japan Weighs Mandatory Safety Duties and Penalties for AI Providers

Japan's government is reviewing AI Act revisions that would require large AI providers, likely including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Alibaba and DeepSeek, to block answers on cyberattack methods and weapons, verify certain users of strongly cyber-capable models and report exploitable flaws, the Yomiuri Shimbun reported. Companies that ignore government orders could face penalties. A bill is targeted for next year's ordinary parliamentary session.
