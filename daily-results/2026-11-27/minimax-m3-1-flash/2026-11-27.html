🌐 TOP STORIES

Trainium and TPU Beat Nvidia GPUs on Performance per Dollar in SemiAnalysis AgentX Results

SemiAnalysis said Amazon's Trainium and Google's TPU are now on its InferenceX and AgentX inference benchmarks, with performance per dollar of 1.2x to 2.5x Nvidia GPUs. A Trainium3 UltraServer reached 2.28x on the GB300 NVL72, B300 and AMD MI355X at roughly 250 tokens per second per user. The firm estimated Trainium3 at $1.05 per chip-hour against $2.31 for GB300.

Volvo Cars Starts Training Hexagon Robotics Humanoids at Its Ghent Plant

Volvo Cars has begun training Hexagon Robotics' AEON humanoid robots at its Ghent, Belgium plant, the first Volvo factory to test humanoids, according to a company release dated November 26. Staff are teaching the wheeled, 60 kg robots to place parts into two fixtures. A larger pilot is planned for the first half of 2027, with production use from mid-2027.

Canada Expands AI Compute Access Fund and Favors Sovereign Compute Providers

Canada's government put an additional C$700 million into its AI Compute Access Fund on November 26, bringing the total to C$1 billion, Artificial Intelligence and Digital Innovation Minister Evan Solomon announced in Toronto. The updated program covers up to 80% of eligible cloud compute costs from sovereign providers and up to 50% from Canadian-based providers, and opens a health and life sciences call.

Congressional AI Regulation Push Stalls Over Committee Jurisdiction Fights

The New York Times reports that key Republicans and Democrats in Congress are pressing for action on AI risks, but months of hearings have produced no consensus bill as committee jurisdiction fights slow the work. Proposals on the table include a Senate select committee with subpoena power, a House FRONTIER Act outline requiring third-party evaluators for large frontier developers, and preserving stronger state laws.

OpenAI Case Study Shows Codex and Astra Surfacing Exoplanet Candidates in Public Data

OpenAI published a case study on November 24 about two amateur astronomers who used Codex and Astra to search public Kepler and TESS data for exoplanet candidates. Bond's Codex pipeline flagged more than 200 signals from about 100,000 stars, and a SETI Institute astronomer independently found two of them. One of Byrd's 49 first-pass candidates is with TESS researchers.
