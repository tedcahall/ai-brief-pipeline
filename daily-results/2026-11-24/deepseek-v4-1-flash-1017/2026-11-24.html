🌐 TOP STORIES

Z.ai Launches GLM-5.4, Its First Loop Transformer Model

Z.ai released GLM-5.4 on Nov. 23, calling it its first flagship on a Loop Transformer: 28 layers of the GLM-5 base model run for two iterations, adding depth without new parameters at roughly 750 billion total. It adds vision and a one-million-token context, is live for GLM Coding Plan, ZCode and Z.ai Chat, and Z.ai says the API opens after a safety evaluation. No GLM-5.4 benchmark scores have been published.

NVIDIA Board Reinstates Jensen Huang, Adding Nearly $1 Trillion in Market Value

NVIDIA's board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, CEO and director effective immediately, saying input from senior U.S. government officials gave it confidence that his return poses no national security risk. The independent investigation into a reported foreign intelligence effort continues, and Huang said he fully supports it. Fortune reported shares rose more than 31%, lifting market value to about $4 trillion from roughly $3 trillion.

OpenAI and Cisco Open-Source Shirudo to Shield Linux Systems Without Downtime

OpenAI and Cisco released Shirudo, a BSD 2-Clause open-source Linux runtime that loads cryptographically signed eBPF shields into a running kernel so defenders can block an exploit before a patch ships. The kernel verifier checks each program and a TPM records what was loaded. In a controlled test, a Codex-generated shield blocked a Java4Shell attempt against a running Elasticsearch service without a restart.

Baidu Publishes ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview Weights Under Apache 2.0

Three public Hugging Face repositories carrying ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview appeared on Nov. 23 at 09:00 UTC with no accompanying announcement, two days ahead of Baidu's own target. The model has 280B total parameters, 14B active and a one-million-token context, under plain Apache 2.0, which is more permissive than the base checkpoint's territory limits and $50 million revenue threshold. Baidu's benchmark table has no independent replication yet.

UN Chief Calls for Global AI Risk Framework Within a Year

Closing the Bridgetown Special Summit on AI Safety, Secretary-General Antonio Guterres asked member states to begin negotiating a multilateral AI Risk Management Framework within about twelve months, built on shared safety measures, common testing, transparency and independent oversight of advanced models. He warned that power is shifting from states to a few companies and called autonomous weapons unacceptable. Barbados' prime minister raised contractual kill switches.
