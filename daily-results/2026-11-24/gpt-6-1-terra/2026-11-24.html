📊 AI INDUSTRY PULSE

Baidu has released ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview weights, while InclusionAI introduced its open-weight Ling-3.2-air multimodal model. Both announcements add new options for developers deploying Chinese AI models.

Microsoft detailed its Rocket reinforcement-learning infrastructure, and Anthropic opened its zero-data-retention-compatible enterprise safeguards to public beta. Separately, SGLang and Miles researchers described an approach to matching training and rollout calculations exactly.

🌐 TOP STORIES

Baidu releases ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview weights

Baidu says the weights for ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview are available under Apache 2.0. The company describes a mixture-of-experts model with 280 billion total parameters, 14 billion active parameters and a one-million-token context window. Its original preview announcement predates the weight release, and Baidu says post-training is ongoing.

InclusionAI releases Ling-3.2-air open weights

Ant Group’s InclusionAI introduced Ling-3.2-air, an open-weight multimodal model with 124 billion total parameters and 5.5 billion activated per token. Its model card reports improvements over its predecessor on workflow automation and professional-document evaluations. Those benchmark figures are reported by the model maker and should not be treated as independent verification.

Microsoft details Rocket reinforcement-learning infrastructure

Microsoft AI described Rocket, its internal reinforcement-learning framework for long training runs, and says it is using the system to post-train MAI Base 2 without distilling third-party models. The company details strategies for handling stale rollouts, numerical consistency and hardware failures across extended runs on large GPU fleets.

Anthropic opens zero-retention enterprise safeguards beta

Anthropic has opened Enterprise Frontier Safeguards to eligible businesses in public beta. The company says the system detects sophisticated misuse across requests while remaining compatible with zero data retention, and that it developed the offering with more than 100 customers. The public beta follows an earlier private testing period.

SGLang and Miles report bit-exact reinforcement learning

The SGLang and Miles teams describe a reinforcement-learning approach designed to make rollout and trainer probabilities match bit for bit at equal weights. Their experiments cover DeepSeek, GLM and Qwen families. The authors report whole-step throughput of 90% and 93% of their standard baseline in two 64-H200 tests, respectively.
