🌐 TOP STORIES

NVIDIA Board Reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO

NVIDIA's board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as president and chief executive, effective immediately, roughly two weeks after he resigned. The chipmaker's shares surged following the announcement, which closes a leadership vacuum that had rattled the AI hardware industry. Huang returns as President, CEO and a board member.

Z.ai Launches GLM-5.4, Its First Loop-Transformer Model

Z.ai launched GLM-5.4, its first flagship model built on a Loop Transformer that reuses 28 layers of the GLM-5 base across two iterations, which the lab says reaches intelligence comparable to 2T-3T parameter models. The model extends "Generalized Coding" beyond code to video, games, 3D shaders and CAD. API access opens after a safety evaluation; weights will be released responsibly.

OpenAI and Cisco Open-Source Shirudo, an eBPF Runtime for Self-Defending Linux

OpenAI and Cisco introduced Shirudo, an open-source Linux runtime that lets agents write dynamic eBPF controls to shield running systems from exploits while a permanent patch is tested and rolled out. The companies describe it as a first step toward self-defending infrastructure, with defenders working alongside AI agents to find affected systems and deploy shields without downtime.

Microsoft AI Details Rocket RL Infrastructure for Post-Training MAI Base 2

Microsoft AI detailed Rocket, its in-house reinforcement-learning framework, in a technical post on post-training MAI Base 2, its 3-trillion-parameter base model. Rocket is built for RL runs spanning weeks to months across thousands of GPUs, addressing staleness, numerical consistency and fault tolerance, and climbs from the base model without distilling third-party models.

UN Chief Calls for Global AI Risk Framework Within a Year

UN Secretary-General António Guterres, closing the Bridgetown Special Summit on AI Safety in Barbados, called on member states to begin discussions on a multilateral AI Risk Management Framework. He warned that protections against AI risks remain fragmented and that governments "are playing catch-up," urging a common foundation for AI safety.
