🌐 TOP STORIES

Z.ai Introduces GLM-5.4 With a Loop Transformer

Z.ai announced GLM-5.4 as its first Loop Transformer, reusing layers from the GLM-5 base model to deepen reasoning while adding vision for coding tasks. The company says the model extends beyond conventional coding into video, games, 3D shaders and CAD. Z.ai said API access would follow safety evaluation and that model weights would be released responsibly.

Google DeepMind Uses Lean-Verified Agents to Optimize Quantum Circuits

Google DeepMind reports that teams of Gemini 4 agents found lower-cost quantum circuits for problems involving fusion, chemistry and drug metabolism, with each result checked using the Lean theorem prover. The company reports an 11.8-fold reduction in a fusion calculation’s cost metric, an eightfold reduction for one molecular simulation, and larger gains on two algorithmic building blocks. These are research results, not evidence that current quantum hardware can run the simulations.

Microsoft Details Rocket, Its Long-Run Reinforcement-Learning System

Microsoft AI described Rocket, its in-house framework for reinforcement-learning runs intended to continue for weeks or months across thousands of GPUs. The system addresses stale agent rollouts, numerical consistency between samplers and learners, and recovery from hardware failures without restarting a complete run. Microsoft says Rocket is being used to post-train its MAI Base 2 model without distillation from third-party models.

Ant Group Opens Two LingSante Medical Models and Announces a Healthcare AI Family

Ant Group’s Afu health app announced LingSante, a medical-model family spanning multimodal understanding, clinical reasoning, health-risk prediction and safety. Its announcement says two models, LingSante-3.1-air-VL and LingSante-3.1-flash, are being released with open weights; the latter has 560 billion total parameters and a context window up to one million tokens. These are developer-provided specifications and intended uses.

OpenAI Launches OpenWorks for Hands-On Small-Business AI Support

OpenAI introduced OpenWorks, a program that funds and equips local partners to work with small businesses on practical AI projects and training. OpenAI says participating businesses pay nothing and receive three months of ChatGPT Business, while partners may receive a monthly stipend of up to $8,000. The company says the initial pilot is live in selected U.S. communities and is recruiting its first partners.
