🌐 TOP STORIES

Jensen Huang Returns as Nvidia CEO After Nearly Two Weeks of Turmoil

Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as President, CEO and board director, according to the company's newsroom post shared on X. Fortune reports that investors and Nvidia partners had worried that the company's financing commitments could slow under interim CEO Colette Kress. Fortune also reports Nvidia had invested nearly $50 billion in AI model makers including OpenAI, Anthropic and xAI as of the end of July.

Z.ai Introduces GLM-5.4, Its First Loop Transformer

Z.ai introduced GLM-5.4, which loops existing layers in the GLM-5 base model for deeper reasoning. The company says the model extends coding to video, games, 3D shaders and CAD modeling and adds vision capabilities. Z.ai states that API access will open after safety evaluation and that model weights will be released responsibly.

Amazon Confirms Decart Technology Deal Reported at $7.5 Billion

New developments: Amazon's blog post confirms it will acquire the technology behind Decart Optimization Stack along with its founding team and researchers. The roughly $7.5 billion price is reported by Bloomberg, which TechCrunch cites. Decart's software helps AI models run efficiently on Nvidia, Amazon and Google hardware, and Amazon is preparing to sell Trainium chips to customers.

OpenAI Says GPT-6 Cyber Helped Validate 948 Linux Kernel Bugs

OpenAI said that in one run GPT-6 Cyber helped validate 948 Linux kernel bugs that could grant local root access, and that it generated patches for review. Researchers @clintgibler and @ky1ebot published findings on working with maintainers to get fixes upstream. These are OpenAI's own claims and have not been independently verified in this brief.

UN Chief Urges Multilateral AI Risk Framework Within a Year

At the Bridgetown Special Summit on AI Safety in Barbados on Monday, Guterres asked member states to develop a multilateral AI risk framework within the next year, backed by independent and credible oversight. Euronews reports he suggested shared safety measures, a testing regime and basic requirements for independent oversight as starting points. Euronews also notes that the US and China are competing for research leads and that US regulators are unlikely to intervene.
