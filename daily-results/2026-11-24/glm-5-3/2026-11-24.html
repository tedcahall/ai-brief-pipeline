🌐 TOP STORIES

NVIDIA Board Reinstates Jensen Huang as President and CEO

NVIDIA's board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as President, CEO and board director, effective immediately, after discussions that included input from senior levels of the US government. The independent investigation into a reported foreign intelligence effort targeting Huang continues, and NVIDIA formed a National Security and Export Control Committee. Colette Kress had served as interim CEO.

Z.ai Introduces GLM-5.4 With Its First Loop Transformer

Z.ai announced GLM-5.4, a generalized coding model built with its first loop transformer, which loops existing layers of the GLM-5 base model for deeper reasoning. The company says it extends beyond traditional coding into video, games, 3D shaders and CAD modeling, and adds vision capabilities for visual coding. API access opens after safety evaluation, and weights will be released responsibly.

Baidu Open-Sources ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview Under Apache 2.0

Baidu released ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview weights under Apache 2.0, a 280B total-parameter mixture with 14B active parameters and a 1M-token context. Baidu reports across-the-board gains in coding, agentic tasks, knowledge work and deep research. Post-training is ongoing, weights are on Hugging Face, and free API access is available on OpenRouter.

Microsoft AI Details Rocket, Its In-House RL Infrastructure

Microsoft AI published technical details of Rocket, the reinforcement learning framework it uses to post-train its new three-trillion-parameter MAI Base 2 model through long RL climbs without distilling from third-party models. The post covers staleness control, sampler and learner numerical consistency, GRPO group handling and an MAI-maintained SGLang fork.

UN Chief Urges Global Talks on an AI Safety Framework

UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres called on Member States to begin discussions on a multilateral framework for managing AI risks, warning the technology is driving the biggest transfer of power in the history of humankind. The UN also delayed rulemaking on autonomous weapons this week, leaving governance gaps as national AI safety debates continue.
