🌐 TOP STORIES

Tencent Releases Hy4 Open Weights With Vision and Computer Use

Tencent released weights for Hy4, a multimodal mixture-of-experts model with 770 billion backbone parameters, 49 billion active per token and a one-million-token context. Its repository documents image and video understanding, computer-use workflows, base and FP8 weights, and vLLM/SGLang deployment. The release uses Tencent's Hy License; the published capability comparisons are Tencent's own evaluations, not independent confirmation.

OpenAI Brings GPT-Live-1.1 to the API With Lower-Cost Standby

OpenAI made GPT-Live-1.1 available to API developers after deploying it in ChatGPT voice in October. The company says it improves multilingual accents, transcription and task completion. A new standby configuration pauses inference while keeping WebRTC calls connected, charging $0.02 per minute instead of the $0.05 active rate. Backend models and tool usage remain separately billed.

Apple Releases Depth Pro 2 for Images and Video

Apple released Depth Pro 2 code and weights for estimating metric depth at native resolution from photos, videos and live feeds. Four model sizes range from 24 million to 872 million parameters, with a separate shared focal-length estimator. Apple's paper reports improvements in boundary sharpness and temporal consistency. The weights are restricted to non-commercial scientific research and academic development.

Nous Brings Hermes Enterprise to Microsoft Teams and Azure

Nous Research announced Hermes Enterprise integration with Microsoft Teams, a Windows desktop app in the Microsoft Store and execution on Azure Container Apps Sandboxes. Its Collective Wisdom feature lets employees propose reusable task skills for team sharing after review. Organizations can run the product in their own Azure tenant. These are announced product capabilities, not independently tested reliability or security guarantees.

US Lawmakers Outline Federal Safety Requirements for Frontier AI

Representatives Don Beyer, Sara Jacobs and Celeste Maloy released an outline for the forthcoming bipartisan SAFE AI Act. The proposal would require frontier-lab safety frameworks, incident reporting and transparency, with government oversight and due process. The announcement also describes targeted emergency powers and room for stronger state protections. This is a legislative blueprint seeking feedback, not enacted law or final legislative text.
