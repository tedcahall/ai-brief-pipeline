🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI announces three AI-produced scientific discoveries in math, computer science and physics

OpenAI announced three new discoveries in mathematics (harmonic analysis), computer science (complexity theory) and physics (quantum field theory) produced by its internal models, which it describes as raw research data. In a new release process, external experts act as shepherds for academic collaborations, with the AI output public for the math and computer science results and release decisions deferred to collaborators for physics.

Google DeepMind announces Genie 4, a real-time interactive world model with native audio

Google DeepMind announced Genie 4, a natively multimodal world model that generates interactive simulations whose music, ambient noise and dialogue are produced simultaneously with the world, using Gemini models for realism and reasoning. It is rolling out as a research preview to trusted testers, followed by Google AI Ultra subscribers and early access partners.

Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5 finds open weights models falling further behind

Artificial Analysis launched Intelligence Index v5, its biggest upgrade since January, with 60% of the Index built on private datasets and new benchmarks including Cognition's FrontierCode 2.0 and Terminal-Bench-Science. Claude Opus 5.5 (max) leads at 58 ahead of GPT-6 Astra (max) at 57, while the open weights gap widened from 3 to 12 points, with DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 and GLM-5.3 highest among released weights at 32.

Anthropic reports early progress using Claude in drug development

Anthropic said its scientists have engaged in deep research partnerships with six organizations developing new medicines, including BigHat Biosciences, Bristol Myers Squibb and Generate Biomedicines, and have begun drug discovery efforts of their own. The post shares early results and how Claude supports the many stages of drug development within Anthropic's wider life sciences ambitions.

Cognition launches FrontierCode 2.0, a merge-judgment coding benchmark topped at 45%

Cognition introduced FrontierCode 2.0, a private benchmark of 132 tasks in real open source repositories graded on whether a maintainer would merge the agent's change, now using deliberately underspecified prompts to test engineering judgment. The benchmark is far from saturated, with the top model scoring 45%, and Cognition says it ranks leading models differently from prior coding evals.
