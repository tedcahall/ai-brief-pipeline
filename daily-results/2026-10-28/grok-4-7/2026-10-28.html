📊 AI INDUSTRY PULSE

🌐 TOP STORIES

Google DeepMind announces Genie 4 world model

Google DeepMind said on October 27 that Genie 4 generates a scene and its audio together, including ambient sound and spoken dialogue, and that users can steer it in real time with text prompts. The model is first a research preview for trusted testers. Google AI Ultra subscribers in Project Genie, and early-access Gemini API and AI Studio partners, are planned next. DeepMind says longer sessions can still drift.

Liquid AI releases open-weight LFM3 model family

Liquid AI released LFM3, a family of open-weight models around 2 billion parameters on a shared backbone, with text, vision, audio, omni, and decision variants. The company says LFM3-1.7B has a 256,000-token context window and was post-trained for agent harnesses including Hermes Agent and OpenClaw. Weights are on Hugging Face, with a playground and API also listed. Vendor scores are not an independent audit.

Oracle and Google to offer TPUs on Oracle Cloud

Oracle and Google said on October 27 that Google TPUs will be available on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, which they called the first hyperscale cloud outside Google to offer them. The note names eighth-generation TPU 8t for training and TPU 8i for inference, and says TPU 8t delivers nearly three times the prior generation's compute per pod. Availability is calendar year 2027, so this is a stated plan rather than chips customers can use now.

OpenAI publishes model results for outside verification

OpenAI said its models produced results on local smoothing and the Kakeya conjectures, a Lean-checked refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis, and a quantum-field-theory claim. It is releasing the math output publicly, publishing the complexity proof while an outside group explains it, and letting a physics group hold those artifacts. Impagliazzo, Paturi, and Williams are named to shepherd the complexity work. Verification is still open.

Anthropic reports early Claude drug-development results

Anthropic said its scientists have worked with BigHat Biosciences, Bristol Myers Squibb, Divergence Labs, Enveda, Generate Biomedicines, and The Column Group, and have started internal discovery programs, including rare childhood muscular dystrophy. The post describes partner-reported lab results, such as a shorter CAR-T design that made about ten times as many receptors in tests, and about 35,000 antibody designs at Divergence. Anthropic presents these as early case studies, not approved drugs.
