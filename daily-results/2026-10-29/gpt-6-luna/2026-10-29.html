🌐 TOP STORIES

Google unveils Gemini 4 Carbon for cybersecurity and long-horizon work

New developments: Gemini 4 Carbon is distinct from yesterday's Genie 4 world-model announcement. Google calls Carbon its next frontier model for cybersecurity and long-horizon capabilities, and is initially sharing it with a selected group of cyber defenders before broader access. Artificial Analysis independently placed Carbon first on its v5 Intelligence Index with a score of 59; that is a benchmark result, not a general guarantee of model superiority.

Microsoft opens its Copilot harness to multi-model agent builders

Microsoft has made its Copilot harness available in Foundry as a managed service for building agents that reason, use tools and handle long-running tasks. The company says the same multi-model orchestration layer powers Copilot Code, Copilot Studio, GitHub Copilot, CLI, VS Code and more, letting customers use different models without maintaining the harness themselves. The capabilities and performance claims are Microsoft’s.

Anthropic study finds analysts followed an AI agent’s assigned lean 86% of the time

In a randomized task with 50 professional causal-inference analysts using Claude Code, participants followed the agent’s coin-flip-assigned preference on four analytic choices 86% of the time; 77% reused its arguments, while 15% credited the agent. The study used deliberately strengthened preferences in a fictional cooperative scenario. Anthropic cautions that real-world effects could be smaller.

Broadcom arranges $63 billion financing structure for chips leased to Anthropic

Bloomberg reported Oct. 26 that Broadcom arranged $63 billion in committed financing from Bank of America, Blackstone, Citi and Morgan Stanley to purchase Broadcom AI chips and lease them to Anthropic through a special-purpose vehicle; Broadcom backstops part of the residual value. This is a financing and equipment-lease structure, not $63 billion of equity raised by Anthropic.

New York enacts chatbot safeguards for minors, effective January 2027

New York signed Senate Bill 9051B and Assembly Bill 10379C on Oct. 28, barring chatbot features for minors across 12 categories, including impersonating a human, encouraging self-harm and lewd conversations. The law takes effect Jan. 1, 2027 and requires multiple age-assurance methods, at least one not based on government identification; age data must be deleted after verification. Violations can draw penalties up to $25,000.
