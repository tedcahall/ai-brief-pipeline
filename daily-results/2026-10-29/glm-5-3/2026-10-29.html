🌐 TOP STORIES

Google launches Gemini 4 Carbon with state-of-the-art cybersecurity claims

Google DeepMind introduced Gemini 4 Carbon on October 28, describing it as its strongest model, state of the art across cybersecurity and long-horizon agentic tasks, with initial access for a group of cyber defenders. Artificial Analysis scored Carbon 59 on its Intelligence Index v5, the highest of any model measured, and reported a cost of $7.76 per task, about 23% below Claude Opus 5.5 (max).

Four Wall Street firms finance $63 billion in Broadcom AI chips for Anthropic

Bank of America, Blackstone, Citi and Morgan Stanley announced a $63 billion financing structure, in partnership with Broadcom, to enable AI compute capacity for Anthropic, one of the largest debt arrangements tied to the AI buildout. The October 26 announcement extends the emerging pattern of suppliers and lenders financing customer AI hardware purchases directly.

Microsoft Cloud revenue grows 30% to $63.9 billion as agent platform scales

Microsoft said Microsoft Cloud revenue reached $63.9 billion in its fiscal 2026 first quarter, up 30% (31% in constant currency), with Azure growth of 48% to $33.1 billion reported by Forbes. Foundry revenue more than tripled year over year, commercial remaining performance obligation rose 73% to $677 billion, and Agent 365 passed 50 million agents, up 30% quarter over quarter.

OpenAI gives ChatGPT Pro and Business subscribers up to $500 monthly in API credits

OpenAI announced that eligible ChatGPT Pro and Business subscribers can claim up to $500 per month in API credits to build apps and agents, claimed through ChatGPT billing. Pro plans receive $100, $200 or $500 per month by tier, and Business plans receive $20 per paid Standard seat or $100 per paid Premium seat, pooled up to $500 per workspace.

New York bans unsafe AI chatbot features for minors

New York Governor Kathy Hochul signed Senate Bill 9051B / Assembly Bill 10379C, prohibiting companies from offering minors AI chatbot features that pretend to be human, build emotional dependency, encourage self-harm or engage in sexually explicit conversation. The law builds on the state's Stop Addictive Feeds Exploitation for Kids Act and is among the first state chatbot design mandates.
