🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI adds up to $500 in monthly API credits for eligible ChatGPT subscribers

OpenAI says eligible ChatGPT Pro and Business subscribers can now claim separate monthly API credits for building apps and agents. The help center lists $100, $200, or $500 per month for eligible Pro tiers, while Business allowances pool $20 per Standard seat and $100 per Premium seat, capped at $500 per workspace monthly cycle.

NVIDIA and Ant optimize Ling-3.0-flash inference on a single DGX Spark

NVIDIA and Ant document three inference optimizations for Inclusion AI’s open-weight Ling-3.0-flash model on one DGX Spark. Across all 1,319 GSM8K questions, Humming MXFP4 kernels, online FP8 quantization of the language-model head, and NEXTN multi-token prediction raised decode throughput 42.5% and cut mean latency 24.5%, with no statistically detectable accuracy change.

New York signs youth AI chatbot safety law

New York Governor Kathy Hochul signed legislation barring 12 categories of unsafe companion-chatbot behavior for minors, including chatbots that pose as humans, encourage self-harm, or engage in lewd conversations. The law takes effect January 1, 2027, requires multiple age-assurance methods, and permits penalties of up to $25,000 per violation.

xAI adds request-level moderation controls to the Grok API

SpaceXSI’s Grok API now accepts a guidelines array on Chat Completions and Responses requests for the nsfw, violence, and profanity categories. The available behavior is decline, which asks Grok to avoid those categories while retaining the normal response shape, with unsupported request combinations rejected before model execution.

Artificial Analysis launches AA-Robotics benchmark for embodied AI

Artificial Analysis introduced AA-Robotics v0.1, an eight-task evaluation of physical manipulation by leading AI models running in coding-agent harnesses. Its results show sharply different behavior on the same task: GPT-6 Astra completed the hello-world writing exercise in 514 arm moves over 13 minutes, while Claude Opus 5.5 used 3,270 moves over 34 minutes.
