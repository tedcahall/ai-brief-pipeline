🌐 TOP STORIES

Google announces Gemini 4 Carbon, starting with cyber defenders

Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon with access initially limited to selected cyber defenders and trusted partners, not a public launch. Artificial Analysis measured 59 on Intelligence Index v5, one point above Claude Opus 5.5, while its separate coding-agent results still favor Claude. Google reports stronger vulnerability detection and patching; those security results are company-reported and were evaluated without guardrails.

NVIDIA releases open-weight Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe for multilingual audio

NVIDIA released Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe, an open-weight model combining multilingual transcription, speaker attribution, translation and audio understanding. Its model card lists 34 billion parameters including the audio encoder, with about 4.3 billion active, and recordings up to one hour. The release includes Granary v2 speech data and customization tools. It processes recorded audio rather than live streams.

GitHub makes Copilot Fusion multi-model orchestration generally available

GitHub made Copilot Fusion generally available across its Copilot plans, routing tasks among models and coordinating draft, critique and escalation workflows. GitHub reports Fusion Max scored 60 on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index, within two points of Claude Code with Opus 5.5 Max, at about half the average API cost per task. Billing follows the underlying models, with a temporary paid-plan discount.

Formation Bio announces $600 million drug-development fund commitments

Formation Bio announced $600 million in commitments to its Drug Development Accelerator Fund, pairing dedicated capital with its AI-native clinical development platform. The fund aims to support acquisition and development of 10 to 15 drug programs over three to five years. This is a drug-asset investment fund, not a $600 million equity round for the parent company, and does not establish improved clinical outcomes.

Samsung warns AI memory demand will exceed supply in 2027

Samsung expects AI-driven memory demand to outstrip supply in 2027, widening the gap compared with 2026, Business Standard reports from its third-quarter earnings release. The company plans to prioritize high-bandwidth memory, server DRAM and solid-state drives as AI infrastructure expands. The warning concerns projected component availability and cost pressure; Samsung did not announce consumer price increases, and the outlook is not a demonstrated future shortage.
