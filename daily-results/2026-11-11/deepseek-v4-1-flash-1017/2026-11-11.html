🌐 TOP STORIES

Google Ships Gemini 4 Carbon to All Paying Developers at Argon's Price

Google made Gemini 4 Carbon available to developers, enterprises, scientists and consumers on November 10, two weeks after a defenders-only release. Google says Carbon ranks first on CursorBench 4.0, Perplexity's WANDR and Zapier's AutomationBench at more than half the cost per task of the next best external model, and it previewed a /swarm multi-agent mode in Antigravity. Introductory pricing matches Argon at $2 and $10 per million tokens.

Alibaba Opens Qwen4-Max Preview With a One-Million-Token Window

Alibaba opened a preview of Qwen4-Max on November 11, the first model from its next flagship Qwen4 family, with image and video inputs and a one-million-token context window. Access runs through the Token Plan subscription plus QwenWork, Qoder, the Qwen app and QwenStudio. Alibaba published no model card, benchmarks, parameter counts or open-weight commitment, so capability claims remain unverified.

Anthropic Launches IPO Roadshow at $70 to $78 a Share

New developments: Anthropic launched the roadshow for its proposed IPO on November 10, offering 825 million Class A shares at $70 to $78 each, a range worth roughly $57.8 billion to $64.4 billion, and applied to list on Nasdaq as ANT. The registration statement is filed but not yet effective. Existing stockholders are selling 23.6 million of those shares, and Anthropic receives no proceeds from that portion.

NVIDIA Opens GR00T 2, a World Action Model for Robot Bodies

NVIDIA introduced GR00T 2 at the Conference on Robot Learning, an open world action model built on Cosmos and trained on 100,000 hours of its open human egocentric video. NVIDIA says it completed 40 of 50 evaluation tasks without task-specific demonstrations and reached 95 percent average success with five demonstrations per task. Weights are slated for December, and the announcement named no license.

SGLang Flaw Exposes NEAR AI Users Behind Confidential Computing

NEAR AI disclosed that an attacker used a remote code execution flaw in SGLang, the open-source inference engine on its servers, to reach internal systems and download account details for about 100 users plus chat history, settings and logs from 18 agents on a legacy deployment. Two Cloud customers' API keys were exposed and revoked. NEAR AI said requests processed on November 8, including confidential-mode ones, may have been visible.
