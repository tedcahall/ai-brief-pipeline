🌐 TOP STORIES

MIT researchers use GPT-6 Astra to rewrite Qwen weights, lifting MATH-500 to 48.1%

MIT CSAIL researchers reported early weight-space programming results in which GPT-6 Astra reads and edits neural-network weights without gradient training. In one Qwen3.5-0.8B experiment, Astra changed 0.016% of weights and raised MATH-500 from 7.9% to 48.1% with sampling settings held fixed. The authors also reported gains in instruction following, while emphasizing that these are early experiments across small networks and selected tasks.

OpenAI introduces LifeVal benchmark for everyday personal-assistant tasks

OpenAI introduced LifeVal, an evaluation of personal-assistant work including caregiving, health, education, finances, shopping and government services. It built the task set from two time-use studies and a demographically representative survey of 3,995 respondents. OpenAI says it has used LifeVal over the past year to guide improvements in browser use, computer use and connector tasks, with results varying by individual workflow.

OpenAI’s Pando Git backend cuts push latency for agent-scale development

OpenAI detailed Pando, a Git-compatible backend now powering all development in its monorepo as Codex increases concurrent engineering work. Pando coordinates branches independently and reuses packfiles to scale reads and writes. OpenAI reports that median push latency fell nearly 5x, or about 80%, while the system serves nearly a petabyte of Git data daily without changing engineers’ or agents’ Git workflows.

Oracle files stock-and-bond offering to finance AI buildout

Oracle filed with the SEC to sell $10 billion in common stock and $10 billion in senior notes to finance its AI buildout. CNBC reports that the proceeds are designated for general corporate purposes, potentially including capital expenditures, debt repayment and investments. The fundraising was previewed at Oracle’s investor day, but Monday’s filings formalized the plan as shares fell 8.8%.

RoboArchitect evolves robot-agent harnesses without changing model weights

RoboArchitect uses a coding model to diagnose robot rollouts and iteratively rewrite the control harness around a frozen policy. The paper reports average success rising from 9.7% to 43.3% on unseen scenes and from 12.7% to 40.2% on unseen tasks across multiple benchmarks and policies. The harness also develops persistent memory, retaining details such as effective grasp depths for later decisions.
