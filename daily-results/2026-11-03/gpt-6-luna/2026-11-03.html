🌐 TOP STORIES

Stanford Researchers Automate Robot-Agent Harness Design, Improving Held-Out Success

The arXiv paper introduces RoboArchitect, which lets a coding model inspect robot rollouts and iteratively revise control code around a frozen policy. Across multiple benchmarks and policies, the authors report unseen-scene success rising from 9.7% to 43.3% and unseen-task success from 12.7% to 40.2%, without updating model weights. These are reported research results, not a guarantee for deployed robots.

OpenAI Adds Cathedral Partnership to Its Next AI-Assisted Security Sprint

OpenAI says Cathedral Systems researchers will work with its teams and models to challenge internal defenses and help find and fix weaknesses. The company frames the sprint as an extension of earlier model-assisted vulnerability investigation and remediation. Its separate bounty announcement raises potential exceptional awards to $172,000 including bonuses; this does not mean every critical report earns that amount.

Microsoft Opens Humanist AI Code of Conduct for Public Consultation

Microsoft published a Humanist AI Code of Conduct and invited public feedback on its principles and how they might be evaluated and applied. The consultation is part of the company’s stated process for developing the document, with a revised version planned after feedback. It is a company proposal, not a binding regulation, independent safety certification or settled industry standard.

SGLang HiCache Reports Higher Prefix Reuse in Distributed Serving

The LMSYS/SGLang technical post explains how HiCache coordinates key-value cache reuse across context and pipeline parallelism, speculative decoding, prefill/decode disaggregation and external storage. For iFlytek Astron MaaS workloads using long inputs, the post reports a cache-hit increase from about 30% to 95% and median time-to-first-token below three seconds. Those figures are deployment-reported results for the described setup, not a cross-provider benchmark.

OpenAI Details Git-Compatible Backend Built for Agent-Scale Engineering

OpenAI says its Pando backend now powers the company monorepo while retaining compatibility with Git clients and protocol. The design supports concurrent writes to different branches and reuses packed repository data across reads. OpenAI reports nearly fivefold lower median push latency and nearly a petabyte of Git data served daily; these are the company’s own production figures.
