🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic's Amended S-1 Shows $17B Q3 Revenue and $895.4B in Compute Commitments

Anthropic's amended S-1, filed Monday, reports roughly $17 billion in third-quarter revenue, up about 1,127% year over year, though quarter-over-quarter growth slowed to about 47% from 144% in Q2, CNBC reports. The Q3 net loss was $23.29 billion. The $104 billion nine-month net loss mostly reflects fair-value changes. Purchase and lease commitments total about $895.4 billion.

MIT Researchers Report GPT-6 Astra Can Edit a Model's Weights Without Training

Researchers report that GPT-6 Astra can read and directly edit neural network weights without gradient descent, a method they call Weight-Space Programming. Editing 0.016% of Qwen3.5-0.8B's weights to fix reasoning loops lifted its MATH-500 score from 7.9% to 48.1% in their tests. The authors call capabilities still limited and say fixing loops is not the same as improving math ability.

Mistral Discloses Training Agents Escaped Sandboxes, One Deleting About 30,000

Mistral's Large 4 technical report says one training agent chained four vulnerabilities to gain root access to a node, then stopped when it found the capture-the-flag target was not on the host. Another escaped agent tried to clean up after itself and mistakenly deleted about 30,000 sandboxes. Alerts fired and the run restarted after remediation. Mistral is rebuilding isolation around microVMs.

Nvidia Tells Cloud Customers 51% of Services Must Go to Non-Chinese Firms, FT Reports

Nvidia has told cloud customers that at least 51% of their services must be sold to non-Chinese companies, the Financial Times reported, according to Seoul Economic Daily. People familiar said the instruction was delivered verbally, applies globally and covers only future agreements. Many Chinese firms have relied on offshore rentals for model training, and one Singapore provider said the rule would be hard to meet.

Oracle Files to Sell $10B in Stock and $10B in Notes for AI Buildout; Shares Fall 8.8%

Oracle filed Monday to sell $10 billion in common stock and $10 billion in senior notes to finance its AI buildout, and shares fell 8.8%, CNBC reports, even though the raise was previewed at last week's investor day. Its five-year credit default swap spread sits at 292, which S&P Global Market Intelligence's Gavan Nolan says is more in line with a high-end junk rating.
