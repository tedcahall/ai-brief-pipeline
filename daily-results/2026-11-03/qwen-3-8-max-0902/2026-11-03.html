🌐 TOP STORIES

Oracle Files to Sell $20 Billion in Stock and Bonds to Fund AI Buildout

Oracle filed with the SEC on Monday to sell $10 billion of common stock and $10 billion of senior notes to finance its AI infrastructure expansion, drawing down the full $20 billion raise cap it had announced through fiscal 2030. CNBC reported the filing, and Yahoo Finance noted shares fell about 7% on the day as investors weighed dilution against the company's data center commitments.

OpenAI Details Pando, a Custom Git Backend Serving Nearly a Petabyte a Day

OpenAI published how it built Pando, a new Git backend that now powers all development in its monorepo after agent-driven commit volume surged. The company says Pando cut push latency by about 80% and serves nearly a petabyte of Git data a day while leaving standard Git workflows unchanged. Greg Brockman wrote separately that OpenAI is considering providing Pando to others.

Abu Dhabi Signs Four OpenAI Deals in Push Toward AI-Native Government

OpenAI signed four agreements with Abu Dhabi entities spanning government services, energy and investment as Sam Altman met the emirate's crown prince, CNBC reported. The package includes a pilot that turns government regulations into machine-readable form, already running on food imports per TNW, and ADNOC's deployment of OpenAI's Frontier platform with G42, which The National called the first by an energy company.

China Accelerates Comprehensive AI Legislation With Risk Reporting and Sandboxes

China's National Development and Reform Commission is advancing comprehensive AI legislation that would require developers and operators to disclose safety risks and report incidents and vulnerabilities, TokenPost reported. The planned framework sets lifecycle safety and ethics standards across data, compute, algorithms and applications, adds regulatory sandboxes with transition periods, and covers job displacement, deepfakes and emotional dependence on AI.

AWS CEO Reaffirms $184 Billion Run Rate and $578 Billion Committed Backlog

AWS chief Matt Garman pointed customers to ample capacity for AI growth, citing a $184 billion run rate, 39.3% growth, a $578 billion committed backlog and 40%+ growth ahead in a post thanking Bloomberg's Ed Ludlow. The figures match Amazon's October 30 earnings, where GeekWire reported AWS growth hit 39%, its fastest pace in nearly five years.
