🌐 TOP STORIES

Ant Releases Open-Weight LingSante-3.1 Medical Models

Ant Ling released LingSante-3.1-flash, a medical Mixture-of-Experts model with 560 billion total parameters, 25 billion active per token and a one-million-token context window. The release also includes the smaller multimodal air-VL model for medical images and tool use. Its reported benchmark advantages are developer evaluations, not evidence of clinical effectiveness; the model card restricts use to research and evaluation.

OpenAI Proposes Shared Development Limits Tied to Perplexity

Chief scientist Jakub Pachocki published internal pretraining measurements and proposed common development limits linked to perplexity and other metrics, with explicit monitoring milestones required before crossing them. Microsoft backed the initiative and disclosed a MAI-2 Base comparison. This is a proposal, not an adopted industry standard; Pachocki acknowledges that datasets differ and perplexity misses gains from reasoning and other post-training techniques.

vLLM Tests NVMe as an Alternative to DRAM for Agent Caches

vLLM reports that a 1.8 TB NVMe cache pool matched an equally sized DRAM pool within 1% throughput on Kimi-K3 across three concurrency levels, using 16 AMD MI355X GPUs. A separate 5.1-times gain compared different pool capacities, not equivalent storage. These are project measurements on specified workloads; write endurance, buffering and workload shape remain important deployment constraints.

Tesla Describes the Neural World Model Behind Its In-Car Visualizer

Elon Musk described a roughly one-billion-parameter diffusion model that generates the car's visualization from Tesla Universal Vision Encoder features. He says it produces eight-image video chunks at 36 frames per second, with latency constrained by available compute. This is a company technical account of a visualizer, not independent validation of autonomous-driving safety, consciousness or general superintelligence.

Governors Seek Complementary Federal and State AI Rules

The National Governors Association issued bipartisan AI governance principles calling for national coordination while preserving appropriate state authority over deployment. The recommendations pair worker, privacy and public-safety protections with evidence-based safeguards, less duplication and competition that does not exclude smaller companies. They are policy recommendations for a federal framework, not newly enacted requirements or a finalized national AI law.
