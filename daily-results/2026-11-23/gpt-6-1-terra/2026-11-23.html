📊 AI INDUSTRY PULSE

Ant Ling released open-weight medical models, while OpenAI’s chief scientist proposed a shared measure and limits for frontier AI development. Both developments carry important qualifications: medical benchmarks are not clinical validation, and the proposed safety standard is not an industry agreement.

Separately, researchers reported AI-assisted industrial cyberattack exercises, Apple is recruiting for a computer-use agent, and a Tracxn report found Indian AI funding rose even as the number of deals fell.

🌐 TOP STORIES

Ant Ling Releases Open-Weight Medical AI Models

Ant Ling released LingSante-3.1-flash, a medical model with 560 billion total parameters and 25 billion active per token, alongside the multimodal air-VL model. Ant reports its own benchmark results, which are not independent clinical validation. The model card warns that it must not independently diagnose, prescribe or make treatment decisions.

OpenAI Scientist Proposes Shared AI Safety Limits

OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki proposes using perplexity, a measure of next-token prediction, to inform shared safety standards and possible development limits across labs. He disclosed OpenAI pretraining measurements while noting difficulties comparing datasets and the metric’s inability to capture all capability gains. The proposal is not an agreed industry standard.

AI-Assisted Tests Show Industrial Cyberattack Risks

Forescout researchers used Claude to adapt a known industrial-controller exploit, while a separate Booz Allen exercise tested AI models inside an industrial-control network. The reported work describes exercises, not an attack on an operating plant. Forescout’s final porting stage took more than eight hours and used $535.74 in API tokens.

Apple Is Recruiting for a Computer-Use AI Agent

A recent Apple job listing describes an agent intended to browse the web, work across apps, run commands and write or debug code, Dataconomy reports. Apple has not announced a launch date. Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman expects a release no earlier than late 2027, according to separate reporting by Digital Trends.

India AI Funding Rises as Deal Count Falls

Indian AI companies raised $1.6 billion in equity funding in the first nine months of 2026, up from $822 million a year earlier, according to a Tracxn report cited by Fortune India. Deal count fell from 149 to 87. The total covers multiple investments, not a single funding round.
