🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI Proposes Shared Pretraining Metrics and AI Safety Limits

OpenAI published pretraining perplexity measurements and proposed common evaluation standards, red-team safety cases and possible development limits tied to monitoring milestones. The proposal is not an adopted industry standard. OpenAI notes that different training datasets complicate comparisons across labs and discusses how shared measurement could be improved.

Amazon Reports AI Agents Exploited a Zero-Day in Medical-Imaging Software

Amazon Threat Intelligence said a threat actor used autonomous agents on the Cairn framework to find and exploit a path-traversal zero-day in MONAI Label, an open-source medical-imaging application. The agents reached root access after about 11 minutes from mapping the application API to exploitation. Amazon described Chinese-nexus attribution as moderate confidence; the activity occurred in September and maintainers issued a fix on November 18.

Ant Group Releases Open-Weight LingSante-3.1-Flash Medical Model

The open-weight Mixture-of-Experts model has 560 billion total parameters, activates 25 billion per token and supports a one-million-token context window, according to its InclusionAI model card. The card describes medical reasoning and long-horizon workflow capabilities, and says the model is intended for research and evaluation, not independent diagnosis or treatment. Benchmark comparisons are developer-reported.

India's AI Funding Nearly Doubles to $1.6 Billion, but a Few Deals Dominate

Citing Tracxn, Fortune India reports that Indian AI companies raised $1.6 billion in equity between January and September 2026, 96% more than a year earlier. The 87 funding rounds were down 42%, while the ten largest deals accounted for 90% of the total, indicating that the rise was concentrated rather than broad-based.

vLLM Reports NVMe KV Cache Matching DRAM on a Kimi Workload

In a vLLM-reported test using Kimi-K3, 16 AMD MI355X GPUs and AgentX traces, 1.8 TB of NVMe matched 1.8 TB of DRAM within 1%. On a 40-day WEKA trace at 70 sessions, the authors report that 4.6 TB NVMe delivered 5.1 times the throughput of 1 TB DRAM. These are workload-specific results, not a general claim that NVMe outperforms DRAM.
