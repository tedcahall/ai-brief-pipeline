🌐 TOP STORIES

Ant Group Releases Weights for Two Medical AI Models

Ant Group’s Afu team released weights for two LingSante models. LingSante-3.1-flash has 560 billion total parameters, activates 25 billion per token and is designed for complex reasoning and multi-step medical workflows. The 124-billion-parameter air-VL handles text and images for tasks such as medical report generation. The model cards describe both as research models, not medical devices or substitutes for professional judgment.

OpenAI Proposes Shared Limits on Frontier Model Development

Chief scientist Jakub Pachocki published internal pretraining measurements and proposed that labs use perplexity alongside other metrics to set thresholds they would not cross until monitoring improved. Microsoft AI chief Mustafa Suleyman supported the disclosure and reported a log-perplexity of 0.819 for MAI-2 Base on OpenAI’s evaluation set. That is a step toward comparing measurements, not an agreed industry limit or a direct measure of safety.

vLLM Matches DRAM Cache Throughput With SSDs in Kimi Tests

The vLLM team reported that a 1.8 TB NVMe cache pool matched an equally sized DRAM pool within 1% when serving Kimi-K3 on AMD MI355X GPUs. A separate trace with longer prompts showed 5.1 times the throughput for a 4.6 TB SSD pool versus 1 TB of DRAM at 70 sessions. That comparison reflects extra cache capacity under load, not inherently faster SSDs.

OpenRouter Drops Three Providers After Data-Handling Findings

OpenRouter said it removed three inference providers and took 125 endpoints out of its zero-data-retention program after Dragos researchers reported data-handling problems at some providers. The endpoint total includes precautionary removals, not 125 confirmed incidents. OpenRouter said it notified potentially affected customers and that the findings did not show a breach of OpenRouter itself.

U.S. Governors Propose a Federal-State Split on AI Oversight

The National Governors Association urged Congress to address national and international AI risks while preserving states’ authority over AI use in areas they already oversee. The recommendations call for safeguards proportionate to documented risks and less duplication across jurisdictions. They outline a negotiating position for the incoming Congress, not legislation or a binding division of authority.
