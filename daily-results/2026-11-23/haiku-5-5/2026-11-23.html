📊 AI INDUSTRY PULSE

OpenAI proposed perplexity as a shared metric for pacing frontier AI, publishing internal pretraining curves and urging labs to agree on common limits. Microsoft's Mustafa Suleyman publicly backed the transparency step, while skeptics questioned whether the metric's target had shifted over time.

Security and open weights dominated the agent and model news. Amazon reported that China-linked hackers used autonomous agents to find and exploit a zero-day in MONAI Label, a medical imaging project, while Ant Group released LingSante-3.1-flash under an MIT license with company-reported medical benchmark wins.

On capital, Palo Alto Networks agreed to buy Applied Compute for $4.5 billion in stock, and Databricks is reportedly in early talks at a $225 billion valuation.

🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI Proposes Perplexity as a Shared Pacing Metric

OpenAI's post proposes perplexity, the average next-token prediction loss, as a metric for comparing model developers and informing shared safety standards. It publishes internal perplexity measurements from GPT-3.5 onward and says the company hopes to set shared pacing measures across the industry. Sam Altman called it the most important thing OpenAI has published in a while.

Amazon Reports China-Linked AI Agents Exploited a MONAI Label Zero-Day

Amazon Threat Intelligence reported that a China-linked actor used Cairn, an open-source penetration-testing framework, with autonomous AI agents given the single goal of obtaining shell access. The agents found and exploited a zero-day in MONAI Label, an open-source medical imaging application. Its maintainers released a fix and a security advisory.

Ant Group Opens LingSante-3.1-flash, a 560B Medical Model

Ant Group's Ant Ling team released LingSante-3.1-flash, an open-weight medical model with 560B total parameters and 25B active per token. Weights are on Hugging Face under an MIT license. The company reports leading results on HealthBench Professional, HLE with tools and BrowseComp with context, ahead of GPT-6 Astra, Claude Opus 5.5 and Gemini 4 Argon; those results are company-reported and not independently verified.

Palo Alto Networks Agrees to Buy Applied Compute for $4.5 Billion

Palo Alto Networks said November 18 that it agreed to acquire Applied Compute, an AI infrastructure startup, in an all-stock deal valued at $4.5 billion, subject to adjustments. Applied Compute's platform helps companies adapt open-weight models to their own data and run them in production. CEO Nikesh Arora said the bet could be bigger than the company's cybersecurity business if it works.

Databricks Is in Early Talks at a $225 Billion Valuation

Secondary coverage relays a Wall Street Journal report that Databricks is in early talks to raise several billion dollars at a valuation of about $225 billion, roughly 18% above its August round. The talks are early, and no round has closed or set final terms.
