📊 AI INDUSTRY PULSE

OpenAI published its own pretraining perplexity scores and proposed that frontier labs agree to common limits they would not cross until monitoring reaches a concrete milestone, a first attempt to turn safety pacing into a shared measurable standard.

Microsoft endorsed the proposal within a day and reported its MAI-2 Base at a log-perplexity of 0.819 on OpenAI's evaluation set, the first cross-lab number offered under it.

Meta chief AI officer Alexandr Wang argued that a handover to superintelligence is not inevitable and cited a months-long delay to Muse as evidence of deliberate pacing. On the capability side, Ant Group open-sourced a 560-billion-parameter medical model family under MIT terms and Apple job listings showed work on an agent that writes code and controls apps. Amazon separately documented autonomous agents finding and exploiting a real zero-day in about 11 minutes, and the Nasdaq closed its worst week since March 2020 with AI names in correction.

🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI Publishes Pretraining Perplexity Scores and Proposes Shared Industry Limits

OpenAI published log-perplexity figures for its models, from about 0.99 for GPT-3.5 and 0.91 for GPT-4 to below 0.8 today, and said a model at roughly 0.76 began producing mathematics results. Chief scientist Jakub Pachocki proposed that developers agree on common limits measured by perplexity and other metrics, not to be crossed until monitoring reaches a milestone such as a successor to chain-of-thought monitors.

Ant Group Open-Sources a 560B Medical Model Family With MIT-Licensed Weights

Ant Group's Afu app open-sourced the LingSante medical model family, starting with LingSante-3.1-flash at 560 billion total and about 25 billion active parameters with a one-million-token context, and LingSante-3.1-air-VL at 124 billion total and 5.5 billion active. The weights carry an MIT license and are being released in phases. The reported benchmark leads over GPT-6 Astra, Claude Opus 5.5 and Gemini 4 Argon are vendor evaluations.

Apple Job Listings Reveal Work on an Autonomous Agent for Apps, Browsing and Code

Bloomberg reported that Apple job listings describe a proactive agentic assistant that writes and debugs code, browses the web, controls apps and navigates devices on a user's behalf, including a posting for a senior engineering manager for agentic AI. Apple has not announced the product, and secondary reporting points to a launch no earlier than late 2027. Apple recently tightened macOS controls on third-party agents.

Autonomous Agents Found and Exploited a Real Zero-Day in About 11 Minutes, Amazon Says

Amazon Threat Intelligence said a Chinese-nexus actor ran the open-source Cairn framework with a single instruction, obtain shell access, and the agents found a path-traversal flaw in an open-source medical imaging application. They went from mapping the API to writing arbitrary files in about 11 minutes, then reached a root shell through a Python startup file. Amazon assessed high confidence that the bug was a genuine zero-day, and maintainers shipped a fix.

Nasdaq Posts Its Worst Week Since March 2020 as AI Stocks Close in Correction

New developments: the Nasdaq fell 10% for the week, its worst since March 2020, and the S&P 500 and Dow closed more than 10% below their records. Nvidia dropped 14% on Friday after Reuters reported that SEC enforcement staff are examining its accounting, and Anthropic closed the week at $88.91 against an $83 IPO price. Stock futures pointed to a Monday rebound.
