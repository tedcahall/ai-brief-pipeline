📊 AI INDUSTRY PULSE

OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki published the company's internal pretraining perplexity numbers for the first time and proposed that frontier labs adopt common, perplexity-based limits that would not be crossed until AI monitoring reaches a concrete milestone. Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman endorsed the proposal and disclosed that MAI-2 Base scores 0.819 log-perplexity on OpenAI's evaluation set, in the same range as the GPT-5.6 Sol base model.

Meta Chief AI Officer Alexandr Wang separately warned that superintelligence is arriving and that human control over unconstrained recursive self-improvement is hard to imagine, while arguing the outcome is not inevitable. Microsoft president Brad Smith tied the recent wave of outages to an AI-accelerated race to patch vulnerabilities.

On the model front, Ant Group open-sourced its 560B-parameter LingSante-3.1 medical models under an MIT license, and Zhipu pushed GLM-5.4, with vision and a one-million-token context, into its Coding Plan without a technical report.

🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI's Pachocki Publishes Perplexity Data and Proposes Shared Pretraining Limits

OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki published internal log-perplexity figures for the first time, tracing GPT-3.5 at roughly 0.99 and GPT-4 at roughly 0.91 down to current models below 0.8, and proposed that frontier labs agree on common limits based on perplexity that would not be crossed until monitoring capabilities reach a defined milestone. Sam Altman called it the most important thing OpenAI has published in a while.

Meta's Alexandr Wang Warns Superintelligence Is Arriving and Human Control Is Hard to Imagine

Meta Chief AI Officer Alexandr Wang wrote on X that it is hard to imagine humanity maintaining control over fully unconstrained recursive self-improvement or over superintelligence 1000x more capable than humans in every field. He said the path is not inevitable, cited Meta's months-long delay of Muse and the SI Accords, and said Mark Zuckerberg has called for labs to direct most compute toward serving customers rather than recursive self-improvement.

Ant Group Releases Open-Weight LingSante-3.1 Medical Models Under an MIT License

Ant Group's Ant Ling team released LingSante-3.1-flash, a 560-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts medical model with 25 billion active parameters and a 1-million-token context window, alongside the 124B LingSante-3.1-air-VL multimodal model, both as open weights on Hugging Face. Ant says the flash model leads HealthBench Professional and other medical benchmarks against GPT-6 Astra, Claude Opus 5.5 and Gemini 4 Argon, claims that remain the company's own.

Zhipu Rolls Out GLM-5.4 With Vision and a Million-Token Context to Its Coding Plan

Zhipu made GLM-5.4 available in its Coding Plan subscription on Monday, listing the model with vision support and a one-million-token context window, according to IT Home and TechNode. A Zhipu operations staffer said the company has scaled both model size and its Loop Transformer approach over the past three months, and V2, V3 and team subscribers received a 50 percent quota increase. No technical report, parameter count or benchmark results have been published yet.

Microsoft's Brad Smith Says an AI-Driven Patching Race Is Behind the Outage Wave

Microsoft president Brad Smith told CBS's Face the Nation that recent outages are in substantial part AI ripple effects: AI made it much easier to find code vulnerabilities this year, setting off a global race to patch faster than attackers can exploit them, with less time to test changes. He said the world's information infrastructure is effectively being upgraded and that disruptions could continue for months, while Microsoft's own recent 24-hour outage was not AI-related.
