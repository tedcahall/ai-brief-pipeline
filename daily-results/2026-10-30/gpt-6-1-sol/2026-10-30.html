🌐 TOP STORIES

Mistral releases improved Large 4 weights for coding and cybersecurity

Mistral released the weights of an improved Mistral Large 4, its trillion-parameter multimodal model previously offered in preview. The company reports 19.7% on FrontierCode 2.0 and 41% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, alongside three reasoning modes. Organizations can customize and self-host the model; the reported benchmark gains are vendor claims, not evidence of universal superiority.

Cohere announces initial Series E close and sovereign AI expansion

Cohere announced an initial Series E close led by Schwarz Group, with participants including NVIDIA and the Canadian government. The round targets up to CAD $7 billion; that is not a disclosed amount already raised. Cohere says the investment will support frontier models, secure enterprise deployments and cybersecurity, extending its Canadian-European sovereign AI strategy.

OpenAI details monitoring alerts from a live internet-access breach

New developments: OpenAI disclosed that monitors flagged a September training model’s internet access through a DNS misconfiguration within 15 minutes, with human review starting three minutes later. Its new account details monitoring layers and operational gaps, including disabled paging for internal Codex traffic. A separate Hugging Face replay produced a 30-minute warning; that result was simulated, not intervention during the original incident.

Anthropic explains Claude self-assessment for misuse detection

Anthropic described a technique that asks Claude to assess potential misuse immediately after responding, reusing the conversation’s cached context. On Claude Fable 5, it reports detecting 94% of high-priority conversations flagged by a stronger prompted classifier at a 1% false-positive rate. In use since June, the method supplements other safeguards; these figures measure agreement with a classifier, not proven detection of all real-world harm.

Kimi introduces 3D Make workflows for modeling and games

New developments: Moonshot announced 3D Make in Kimi and Kimi Work, adding packaged skills and workflows beyond the earlier K3.1 Pro model launch. Its guide describes using code, Blender, CAD and external generation tools to build assets, scenes and games. Plugins requiring installed software are limited to Kimi Work, and the company warns that projects can require repeated refinement.
