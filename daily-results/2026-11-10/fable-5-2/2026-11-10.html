🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic Releases Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 at Lower Prices

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 on November 9, one underlying model offered with and without cyber and biology safeguards, at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens. Anthropic reports 49.1 percent on FrontierCode 2.0 and says 1,000 Fable agents formalized Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours. Artificial Analysis measured about 2.6 times Opus 5.5's cost per task at max effort.

Meta Releases Muse Ember, Its Smallest Model, for High-Volume Agent Work

Meta introduced Muse Ember on November 9, a multimodal model with a 1M-token context window built for repeated agent work such as tool calls, coding subtasks and document parsing. It costs $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens on the Meta Model API. Meta says it matches Claude Haiku 5.5's best Artificial Analysis index score at about one-third the cost, while trailing Haiku on FrontierCode 2.0.

Meituan Opens LongCat-2.5 Weights Under MIT License

Meituan published the weights of LongCat-2.5 on Hugging Face under the MIT License on November 9, one week after introducing the vision-capable coding model, VentureBeat reports. Meituan's model card lists 1.6 trillion total parameters with about 48 billion active per token, and its evaluation shows 88.4 on Terminal Bench v2.1 against 70.8 for the predecessor. VentureBeat notes growing Washington scrutiny of Chinese AI models.

Commerce Halted Megaspeed Probe After Nvidia Contact, NYT Reports

New developments: The New York Times reported November 9 that the Commerce Department paused its export-control investigation of Megaspeed after Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter emailed the agents in August 2025, according to The Next Web, which has not independently verified the report. The Justice Department is now running a criminal inquiry. Commerce says it handled the case by the book, and Megaspeed denies smuggling chips.

OpenAI Deploys Control Room to Halt Research Workloads Within 35 Seconds

OpenAI described Control Room, a central service deployed across its research infrastructure that tracks inference workloads and stops model output within 35 seconds of a decision to intervene. Inference engines check each job's authorization before sampling, and the system fails closed if the service goes down. OpenAI says the lag comes from a caching layer it is working to eliminate, and cites a recent DNS-related agent incident caught by a monitor.
