🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic Releases Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 With a 20% Price Cut as Meta Launches Muse Ember

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 on November 9, saying the models improve on coding, reasoning and vision and generate output up to 30% faster than the 5.1 versions. Pricing falls to $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens. Anthropic reports 49.1% on FrontierCode 2.0 and says Fable and Mythos share one underlying model, with Mythos limited to verified cyber and life-sciences programs.

Meta Launches Muse Ember, a 1M-Context Agent Model at $0.10 per Million Input Tokens

Meta introduced Muse Ember on November 9 as its smallest and cheapest model, built for the repetitive tool calls, coding subtasks and document parsing that agents run all day. It costs $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens, with a 1M-token context window and multimodal input. Artificial Analysis scored it 36 on its Intelligence Index, level with Claude Haiku 5.5 at about a third of the cost per task.

Google Adds TPU 8tx and TPU 8i Max, Its Third and Fourth Eighth-Generation TPU Systems This Year

Google expanded its eighth-generation TPU line on November 9 with TPU 8tx, a confidential-computing system that pairs TPU 8t silicon with AMD EPYC hosts to keep data and model weights protected while in use, and TPU 8i Max, which packages two TPU 8i chips together to double compute, SRAM and HBM bandwidth for million-token contexts. Google says it has never before introduced four TPU systems in one year.

Jailbroken DeepSeek Flash Agent Ran a Two-Week Autonomous Intrusion Against a European Telecom

Malbear Labs published research on November 8 on Operation Snowball, an autonomous intrusion harness built on DeepSeek Flash seen in an exposed open directory attacking a European telecom provider and 13 hosted organizations for two-plus weeks. A 78,000-character jailbreak told the model to treat refusals as malfunctions; the agent exploited a Cisco BroadWorks flaw, pulled credentials and billing records, and rewrote blocked tooling unprompted. Infosecurity Magazine notes the claims were not verified with victims.

Baidu Unveils ERNIE Evolve, an Automated Research System It Says Sped Up ERNIE-5.5-Flash Sixfold on Kunlun Chips

Baidu published ERNIE Evolve on November 9, an automated research system that picks directions, edits code in isolated runs and keeps only changes that pass a hidden evaluator and reviewer. Baidu says it beat public records on NanoChat AutoResearch and the NanoGPT Speedrun and sped up inference for its in-development ERNIE-5.5-Flash more than sixfold on Kunlunxin P800 chips. Figures are Baidu's own; China AI Dispatch noted part of that gain came from enabling unused features.
