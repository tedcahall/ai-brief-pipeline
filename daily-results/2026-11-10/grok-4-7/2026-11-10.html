🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic Releases Claude Fable and Mythos for Harder Technical Work

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 on November 9 as the same underlying model with different safeguards. Fable is on paid plans and major clouds at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, down from $10 and $50. Mythos stays limited to approved life-sciences and cyber programs. Anthropic says the pair beat Opus 5.5 on most of its own evaluations. Those scores are company-reported, not independent replications.

Meta Introduces Muse Ember for Repeated Agent Work

Meta released Muse Ember on November 9, a small multimodal model for repeated agent tasks. It is available as muse-ember-1.0 at $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens, with a 1 million token context window. Meta says it matches Claude Haiku 5.5's best Artificial Analysis score at about a third of the cost. That comparison is Meta's citation of Artificial Analysis, not proof of broad superiority.

ByteDance Introduces Seedream for Stable Multi-Turn Image Editing

ByteDance's Seed team introduced Seedream 5.5 for professional image production. The official post says it preserves unedited regions across 100 successive edits, generates native images with transparent backgrounds, and renders small text more accurately than the previous version. ByteDance says it leads other major models on text structure and text-to-image realism. That ranking is the company's own evaluation, not an independent test.

Google Adds Confidential and High-Context TPU Systems

Google announced TPU 8tx and TPU 8i Max on November 9. TPU 8tx pairs TPU 8t silicon with AMD EPYC hosts for confidential inference, keeping data and weights protected while in use. TPU 8i Max packages two TPU 8i chips and doubles compute, SRAM, and HBM bandwidth versus TPU 8i. Google says this is its first year with four TPU systems in one generation. The post includes no independent benchmarks.

OpenAI Deploys Control Room to Halt Research Workloads

OpenAI described Control Room, already deployed on its research infrastructure, to track inference jobs and stop them when safety or security concerns arise. Engines must check with Control Room before producing tokens. OpenAI says further output stops within 35 seconds of a halt decision because of an authorization cache, and frontier research jobs fail closed if Control Room is down. The 35-second figure is OpenAI's stated maximum, not an independent measurement.
