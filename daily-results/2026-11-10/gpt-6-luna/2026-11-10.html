🌐 TOP STORIES

Anthropic Releases Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 for paid users and limits the same underlying Mythos 5.2 model to verified cybersecurity and biomedical organizations. The company reports improved coding, reasoning and vision, and says the models generate output up to 30% faster than version 5.1 at 20% lower prices, or $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens. Its published benchmark and safety results are company-reported.

Meta Introduces Muse Ember, Its Smallest Agent-Focused Model

Meta introduced Muse Ember for repeated agent tasks such as tool calls, coding subtasks, routing and document extraction. The company says it supports text, images, audio and video, a one-million-token context window, and workflows that call tools across apps. Muse Ember is available through Meta Model API at $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens; its performance comparisons are Meta-reported.

Google Adds TPU 8tx and TPU 8i Max Systems for Confidential and High-Context AI

Google added TPU 8tx, aimed at confidential inference, and TPU 8i Max, aimed at workloads with very large context windows, to its eighth-generation TPU systems. Google says 8tx pairs TPU 8t silicon with fifth-generation AMD EPYC hosts and protects data and model weights during use; 8i Max combines two 8i chips, doubling compute, SRAM and HBM bandwidth per package. These are system configurations, not newly announced chip designs.

OpenAI Describes Control Room for Stopping Research Model Workloads

OpenAI says Control Room tracks research workloads, links them to inference engines and denies further model output after a job is stopped. It is deployed across the company’s research infrastructure and covers inference for training, evaluation and data processing. OpenAI reports a maximum 35-second delay between receiving a stop instruction and halting output, caused by cached inference authorizations, and says the system is designed to stop output even if Control Room itself fails.

TSMC October Revenue Rises 59.7% Year over Year

TSMC reported October revenue of NT$586.89 billion, or about US$17.88 billion, up 59.7% from a year earlier and 14.7% from September, according to CNBC. The report attributes demand to advanced semiconductors bought amid technology companies’ AI investment, while TSMC has said capital spending will be significantly higher from 2026 to 2028. The revenue figure is a company report; the demand explanation is CNBC’s characterization.
