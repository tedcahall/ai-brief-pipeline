NOV 13, 2026

📊 AI INDUSTRY PULSE

Google introduced Gemini 4 Hydrogen for faster coding and agentic workflows, while Thinking Machines made Inkling 2 Small weights downloadable. OpenAI announced two security-agent products, and Anthropic began rolling out a Claude Meetings beta for teams.

Separately, nonprofit groups asked a California court to halt development or deployment of new models by five AI companies; the filing is a lawsuit, not a ruling.

🌐 TOP STORIES

Google introduces Gemini 4 Hydrogen

Google introduced Gemini 4 Hydrogen for coding and agentic workflows. The company says it completes tasks 2.5 times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash at its default high setting. API pricing is $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens, with availability across Google AI Studio and other Google products.

Thinking Machines releases Inkling 2 Small weights

New developments: Thinking Machines says Inkling 2 Small weights are now downloadable on Hugging Face under Apache 2.0. The model was announced earlier; this is the subsequent availability of its weights, rather than a new model launch. The company announced the download on its official account.

OpenAI introduces Daybreak security agents

OpenAI announced Daybreak Red Team and Defense Factory at its November 12 security event. SecurityWeek reports that Red Team investigates and validates web-application vulnerabilities, while Defense Factory proposes patches for engineers to review. Red Team is accepting early-access requests; patches are not deployed automatically.

Claude Meetings enters beta for teams

Anthropic is rolling out Claude Meetings in beta for Team and Enterprise plans. Claude can join Google Meet as a visible participant, answer questions and build documents or whiteboards during a call. The company says transcripts and notes are saved, and access for outside guests is controlled by administrators.

Advocacy groups ask court to halt new AI models

Four nonprofit advocacy groups sued Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI and SpaceXSI in San Francisco Superior Court, seeking to stop development or deployment of new models. The Associated Press reports that the complaint alleges public-health and property harms. These are plaintiffs’ allegations, not a court finding or an existing injunction.
