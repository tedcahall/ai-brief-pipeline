🌐 TOP STORIES

Google Launches Gemini 4 Hydrogen for Faster Coding and Agent Workflows

Google introduced Gemini 4 Hydrogen across developer, enterprise and consumer products, with four reasoning levels and a one-million-token context window. Pricing is $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens. Google reports tasks finish 2.5 times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash; its speed and benchmark comparisons depend on the stated settings and workloads.

Thinking Machines Releases Inkling 2 Small Weights Under Apache 2.0

New developments: Thinking Machines has now released Inkling 2 Small’s weights under Apache 2.0, following the earlier announcement that downloads would arrive after safety testing. The multimodal model accepts text and images and produces text, with 594 billion total parameters and 17 billion active. Its model card lists local inference support and at least 500GB of aggregate GPU memory for the native NVFP4 checkpoint.

Claude Meetings Enters Beta for Team and Enterprise

Claude Meetings is starting a beta rollout on Team and Enterprise plans, initially supporting Google Meet. Claude appears as a visible participant, answers questions, and creates documents and whiteboards during calls. Connected tools require permission. Anthropic says it saves transcripts, summaries and screen descriptions rather than audio; organizations using certain retention, healthcare and encryption configurations are excluded during the beta.

OpenAI Introduces Daybreak Tools for Vulnerability Fixes and Red Teaming

OpenAI announced Daybreak Defense Factory and Daybreak Red Team as products for applying its cybersecurity capabilities. Defense Factory investigates vulnerability reports and prepares patches for engineers to review and test, with Codex available for collaboration. Red Team, labeled early access, coordinates agents to test web applications, validate vulnerabilities and check fixes within controlled assessment scope. This is product tooling, not a newly announced foundation model.

Nonprofits Ask California Court to Halt New Models at Five AI Developers

Four advocacy groups filed a lawsuit in San Francisco Superior Court seeking to stop Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI and SpaceXSI from developing or deploying new AI models. The complaint alleges violations of California unfair-competition law and risks to public health and computer systems. The requested pause is a legal demand, not a court order reported as granted; the companies did not immediately respond to AP.
