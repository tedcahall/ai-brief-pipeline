🌐 TOP STORIES

Google Releases Gemini 4 Hydrogen for Faster Agentic Work

New developments: Google has launched Hydrogen, distinct from the Carbon rollout and Decisions API covered earlier this week. It offers four thinking levels and a one-million-token context window at $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens. Google reports tasks finish 2.5 times faster than with Gemini 3.8 Flash; rollout spans its app, API, Search and developer tools.

Thinking Machines Releases Inkling 2 Small Weights Under Apache 2.0

New developments: The weights promised at the earlier Inkling 2 Small launch are now available under Apache 2.0, according to Thinking Machines. Its model card describes text-and-image inputs, text output and a one-million-token context window. The native NVFP4 checkpoint needs at least 500GB of aggregated GPU memory, so open availability does not mean it fits on ordinary consumer hardware.

Nvidia Names Colette Kress Interim CEO After Jensen Huang Resigns

New developments: Nvidia says Jensen Huang has resigned as president, CEO and director, effective immediately, moving beyond the board investigation previously reported. CFO Colette Kress becomes interim CEO while retaining her finance role; the board will consider internal and external successors. Harvey C. Jones becomes board chair. The board says it expects operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap to continue without interruption.

OpenAI Adds Daybreak Agents for Patching and Web-App Security Tests

OpenAI announced Daybreak Defense Factory and Daybreak Red Team. Defense Factory investigates security findings and prepares patches for engineers to review and test before merging. Red Team, offered through early access, uses coordinated agents to test web applications, validate vulnerabilities and check fixes. OpenAI describes isolated environments, assessment-scope controls and Guardian review; these are product capabilities, not evidence that autonomous defense is infallible.

Nonprofits Sue Five AI Developers Seeking an Advanced-Model Freeze

Four nonprofits filed suit in San Francisco Superior Court seeking to freeze advanced AI development at Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI and SpaceXSI. They invoke California's Unfair Competition Law and allege threats to public health, safety and property. The filing and requested relief are confirmed by the plaintiffs' announcement and news reporting; the allegations are not court findings, and a freeze has not been established by these sources.
