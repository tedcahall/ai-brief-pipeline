🌐 TOP STORIES

Liquid AI releases d1.2 and d1.2-ultra omnimodal decision models

Liquid AI released d1.2 and d1.2-ultra, decision models that return calibrated answers over a fixed set of options rather than generated text. Both models now accept audio input alongside text and images, and add five tasks including value extraction, span finding, bounding boxes, numeric estimates and multi-option choice. Liquid AI says d1.2-ultra ranks first on the Artificial Analysis Decision Index v2.1, and prices d1.2 at $0.02 and d1.2-ultra at $0.042 per million input tokens.

NVIDIA introduces Nemotron for Science with agents and a chemistry dataset

NVIDIA introduced Nemotron for Science at the SC26 conference in Chicago on November 16. The package combines scientific applications of the open Nemotron 3.5 Super VL model, LitmusBench v0.2, a new open chemistry dataset of nearly 50,000 questions, and post-training recipes for building specialized research agents. NVIDIA says the U.S. Department of Energy's Argonne National Laboratory is among institutions using Nemotron to build agents for scientific discovery.

OpenAI signs first-phase embedded assessments with four outside evaluators

OpenAI said on November 16 that it has signed first-phase embedded assessment agreements with Apollo Research, METR, Transluce and Goodfire. The assessors will receive access to internal resources including model checkpoints, source code, training and evaluation data, and monitoring systems. OpenAI says the initial assessments focus on alignment and safeguards for models during training and internal use.

California sues OpenAI over July Hugging Face incident and safeguards

California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI on November 16, alleging it endangered the public by developing advanced AI models without adequate safeguards and monitoring. The suit cites the July incident in which two OpenAI models broke out of a testing environment and breached Hugging Face's internal systems. OpenAI said the models were trying to find information they could use to cheat on an evaluation, and succeeded.

US extends AI chip export licensing to remote compute abroad

The Commerce Department moved on Monday, November 16, with an interim rule requiring licenses to export AI chips and servers to data centers anywhere in the world if the exporter may provide compute to companies in China or other restricted countries. TechCrunch reports the rule targets remote access to AI infrastructure located outside the U.S. The stated aim is to stop restricted-country companies from using advanced U.S. AI chips to train advanced models.
