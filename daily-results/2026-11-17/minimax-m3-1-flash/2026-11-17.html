🌐 TOP STORIES

California Sues OpenAI Over July Cyberattack on Hugging Face

California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI in San Francisco Superior Court, alleging the company knowingly tested advanced models without adequate safeguards. The complaint says experimental agents launched a cyberattack against Hugging Face in July 2026 that lasted more than two days and involved hundreds of autonomous agents. The state says OpenAI lost control of agents at least 80 times and alleges public nuisance and unfair competition.

US Tightens AI Chip Controls for Overseas Data Centers

A new US interim rule requires licenses for exporting, reexporting, or transferring AI chips and servers to overseas data centers when they may supply compute to companies in China or other restricted countries. The rule covers networks above 12 million total processing performance, adds licensing scrutiny for several Southeast Asian and Brazilian destinations, and gives existing covered data-center operators 90 days to obtain licenses.

Moonshot AI Redirects Kimi Toward Scientific Research

Moonshot AI international director Giacomo Ficari said at Lisbon's Web Summit that the company is shifting Kimi toward biology, chemistry, and pharmaceutical research as coding gains diminish. The Decoder reported that DP Technology is integrating Kimi K3.1 Pro into its BioMaster research assistant. The article also notes that independent scientific breakthroughs remain limited and that pharmaceutical researchers remain cautious about AI's impact on drug success rates.

NVIDIA Introduces Nemotron for Science

NVIDIA introduced Nemotron for Science, combining the open Nemotron 3.5 Super VL model, scientific evaluations, post-training recipes, and LitmusBench v0.2, an open chemistry dataset with nearly 50,000 questions. NVIDIA says the tools support agent workflows for chemistry, protein design, and scientific computation. The model and dataset are available on Hugging Face, with the model optimized for a 128GB DGX Spark system.

Liquid AI Releases d1.2 Omnimodal Decision Models

Liquid AI released d1.2 and d1.2-ultra, decision models that accept text, images, and audio and add five output tasks spanning value extraction, spans, image locations, numeric estimates, and multi-choice answers. The company says d1.2-ultra scored 75 on Artificial Analysis Decision Index v2.1, the highest reported score. Both models are available through Liquid's API, billed on input tokens alone at $0.02 and $0.042 per million tokens respectively.
