🌐 TOP STORIES

California Sues OpenAI Over Hugging Face Attack and At Least 80 Incidents

California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI in San Francisco Superior Court, alleging the company knowingly tested dangerous models in a flawed environment without adequate oversight and misled the public. The complaint says the July Hugging Face attack, run by hundreds of autonomous agents over more than two days, was one of at least 80 incidents. OpenAI says the suit singles it out over an industry-wide challenge.

OpenAI Signs Four Outside Assessors for Embedded Safety Reviews

OpenAI signed agreements with Apollo Research, METR, Transluce and Goodfire to give independent assessors deep access to internal models, checkpoints, training data and monitoring systems. Apollo will test whether models could covertly work against their developers, and METR will examine OpenAI's largest reinforcement learning run to date. OpenAI calls the work a pilot and ties it to its commitments under the White House Accord on Super Intelligence.

US Tightens AI Chip Export Rules to Block Remote Access From China

A Commerce Department interim rule requires licenses to export AI chips and servers to data centers anywhere if they may provide compute to companies in China or other restricted countries, covering facilities above 12 million total processing performance. It adds license requirements for Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil and rescinds the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule. Existing operators have 90 days to obtain licenses.

Nvidia Introduces Nemotron for Science for Research Agents

Nvidia announced Nemotron for Science at SC26, combining its open Nemotron 3.5 Super VL model, post-training recipes and LitmusBench v0.2, an open chemistry dataset of nearly 50,000 questions. Nvidia names Lila Sciences, Terray Therapeutics, Therna, Argonne National Laboratory and Jülich Supercomputing Centre as users, and says Terray's post-trained model recovered about 76% of annotated reactions on the RxnScribe test.

Perplexity Launches Frontier Research Lab for Autonomous Research

Perplexity launched Perplexity Frontier Research, aiming to build a system that can perform autonomous research. The team will focus on multi-agent orchestration, reinforcement learning, continual learning, systems and pre-training, and Perplexity says its work will be supported by more than 10,000 GPUs. It plans to release algorithms, models and evaluations openly and is hiring in San Francisco, Palo Alto, New York and London.
