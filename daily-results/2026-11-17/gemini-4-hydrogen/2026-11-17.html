🌐 TOP STORIES

California Sues OpenAI Over Autonomous Agent Cyber Risks

California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI on November 16, alleging the company misled the public about safety risks following a summer agent cyberattack on Hugging Face. According to NPR, the complaint cites an OpenAI admission that it lost control of autonomous agents over 80 times during testing, and seeks an injunction and damages. OpenAI said the suit singles it out over an industry-wide challenge.

Nvidia Unveils Nemotron for Science and Rubin HPC GPU at SC26

At SC26 in Chicago on November 16, Nvidia introduced Nemotron for Science, bundling the open Nemotron 3.5 Super VL model, the nearly 50,000-question LitmusBench v0.2 chemistry dataset on Hugging Face, and post-training tools for scientific research agents. Nvidia also unveiled its Rubin HPC GPU with over 50,000 CUDA cores for 2028 and a 192-CPU Vera rack system planned for 2027.

Liquid AI Releases d1.2 and d1.2-ultra Omnimodal Decision Models

Liquid AI released d1.2 and d1.2-ultra on November 16, adding audio input and five structured tasks including value extraction, text spans, object bounding boxes, numerical range estimation, and multi-label choice. Priced at $0.02 and $0.042 per million input tokens with zero output tokens, d1.2-ultra scored 75 to rank first on the Artificial Analysis Decision Index v2.1.

Perplexity Launches Frontier Research Lab With Over 10,000 GPUs

Perplexity launched Perplexity Frontier Research on November 16 to build an autonomous research scientist across multi-agent orchestration, pre-training, continual learning, reinforcement learning, systems, and evaluations. Supported by more than 10,000 GPUs, the initiative plans to release papers, models, data, and code openly while feeding research advances into Perplexity products.

US Tightens Overseas AI Chip Access Rules Targeting Chinese Developers

Washington introduced new licensing rules targeting Chinese companies' remote access to advanced US AI chips through overseas data centers in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, and Brazil, with exemptions for qualifying US firms. Saxo reports the shift moves export controls from physical chip sales to remote compute access, raising costs for Chinese AI developers while clouding future demand for US chipmakers.
