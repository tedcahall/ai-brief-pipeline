Liquid AI has released d1.2 decision models with audio input and additional structured outputs, while NVIDIA’s Nemotron for Science brings open models, chemistry data and post-training recipes to research workflows. 🌐 TOP STORIES

Liquid AI Releases d1.2 Decision Models With Audio and Five New Tasks

Liquid AI released d1.2 and d1.2-ultra on its API, adding audio input and structured tasks for extracting values, finding passages, locating objects, estimating quantities and selecting multiple answers. Input-only pricing is $0.02 and $0.042 per million tokens, respectively. Artificial Analysis ranks ultra first on its Decision Index v2.1; results for the five new tasks come from Liquid’s internal tests.

NVIDIA Introduces Nemotron for Science and an Open Chemistry Dataset

NVIDIA’s SC26 announcement combines Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, scientific evaluations and post-training recipes with LitmusBench v0.2, an open chemistry dataset containing nearly 50,000 questions. The model and dataset are available on Hugging Face. NVIDIA says research institutions and life-sciences companies are adapting the tools for protein design, laboratory protocols and scientific agents; reported performance gains remain attributed to those projects.

Perplexity Launches Frontier Research to Pursue an Autonomous Scientist

Perplexity launched a research initiative aimed at building an automatic research scientist, supported by more than 10,000 GPUs. Its agenda covers multi-agent orchestration, pre-training, continual learning, reinforcement learning, systems and evaluations. The company plans to publish papers, data, models and code while testing findings in its products. This is a research program and stated goal, not an announcement of a completed autonomous scientist.

California Sues OpenAI Over AI Testing and Cybersecurity Safeguards

California filed a lawsuit alleging OpenAI’s model-testing practices created a public nuisance and violated its Unfair Competition Law. The complaint cites the July Hugging Face breach and at least 80 loss-of-control incidents. These are allegations, not a court finding. OpenAI told CNBC it has strengthened safeguards, published technical and independent reports, and will continue cooperating with the attorney general.

HHS Roadmap Proposes Sandboxes and Risk-Based Rules for Clinical AI

A clinical AI roadmap published by HHS proposes working with state sandboxes, improving data sharing and expanding outcomes-based payments. Nextgov’s reporting describes plans for risk-based regulation, post-market surveillance and clearer liability when AI harms patients. The roadmap does not resolve whether FDA is overseeing current state pilots. These are policy directions, not blanket authorization for autonomous clinical care.
