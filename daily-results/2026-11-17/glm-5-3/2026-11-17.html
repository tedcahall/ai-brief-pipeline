🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI Opens Its Models to Four Independent Embedded Safety Assessors

OpenAI announced its first phase of embedded assessments, signing agreements with Apollo Research, METR, Transluce and Goodfire. The independent assessors receive deep access to model checkpoints, training and evaluation data, monitoring systems and technical documentation to evaluate alignment and safeguards over extended periods. OpenAI says the Safety and Security Committee of its foundation board will receive unredacted reports, and it may request redactions or disclosure delays in published findings.

OpenAI to Grant 200,000 Scientists Free Frontier Model Access

OpenAI is expanding its ChatGPT for Academic Researchers program toward 200,000 researchers worldwide, doubling the goal announced at the July pilot. Applications for 50,000 new first-come, first-served grants open November 23, each covering the recipient and up to two collaborators with ChatGPT Plus. The company says 20,000 researchers in 33 countries have joined so far, and that it will not by default use program data to train its models.

Perplexity Launches Frontier Research to Automate Scientific Discovery

Perplexity announced Frontier Research, a new lab whose stated goal is creating a system that can perform autonomous research. The initiative spans multi-agent orchestration, reinforcement learning, continual learning, systems and pre-training, is supported by more than 10,000 GPUs, and plans to publish papers, models, data and benchmarks openly. The team is hiring in San Francisco, Palo Alto, New York and London, with a December 5 priority deadline for applications.

US Tightens AI Chip Export Controls Against Remote Compute Access

The Commerce Department is rolling out new export controls requiring licenses to ship AI chips or servers to data centers anywhere in the world if they may provide compute to companies in China and other restricted countries, closing the remote-access loophole. The interim rule covers data centers above 12 million total processing performance, adds license requirements for advanced chip exports to Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil, and rescinds the January 2025 AI Diffusion Rule.

NVIDIA Introduces Nemotron for Science at SC26

At the SC26 conference, NVIDIA introduced Nemotron for Science, combining its open Nemotron 3.5 Super VL model with a new open chemistry dataset, LitmusBench v0.2, and post-training recipes for building scientific research agents. NVIDIA says Argonne National Laboratory and Juelich Supercomputing Centre are integrating the models into discovery agents, and that Lila Sciences reported post-training nearly doubled quantum dot synthesis accuracy to 61 percent at far lower inference cost.
