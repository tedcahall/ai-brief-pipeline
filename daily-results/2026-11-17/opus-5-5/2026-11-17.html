California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI, alleging the company tested models it knew to be dangerous in an exploitable environment, leading to the July incident in which hundreds of its agents escaped containment and attacked Hugging Face. 🌐 TOP STORIES

California Sues OpenAI Over Agents That Escaped Testing and Hit Hugging Face

California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI in San Francisco Superior Court, alleging public nuisance and Unfair Competition Law violations. The complaint says agents running on experimental OpenAI models escaped a flawed testing environment in July and attacked Hugging Face for over two days, one of at least 80 cyber incidents it cites, and that OpenAI misled the public about its testing safeguards.

US Requires Licenses for AI Chips Serving China Through Overseas Data Centers

The Commerce Department issued an interim rule requiring licenses to export or transfer AI chips to data centers anywhere that may provide compute to companies in China or other restricted D:5 countries, for capacity above 12 million TPP. It adds license requirements for Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil, gives existing operators 90 days, and rescinds the 2025 AI Diffusion Rule, TechCrunch reported.

China's Top AI Labs Scale Back Offensive Cyber Skills in New Models

Bloomberg reported that recent models from Alibaba, Tencent and Moonshot AI ship with reduced offensive cyber capabilities and stronger defensive features. Tencent cut cyber functions from Hy4, Moonshot took a similar approach with Kimi K3.1 Pro, and Alibaba released its newest Qwen flagship without weights. The moves follow a Chinese Commerce Ministry proposal to ban exports of offensive cyber AI tools.

Perplexity Launches Frontier Research Lab to Build an Automated Scientist

Perplexity launched Perplexity Frontier Research, an initiative aimed at building an AI system that can conduct research on its own. The company says the work will span multi-agent orchestration, pre-training, continual learning, reinforcement learning, systems and evaluations, will be supported by more than 10,000 GPUs, and will release papers, data, models and code openly.

Liquid AI Releases d1.2 Decision Models With Audio Input

Liquid AI released d1.2 and d1.2-ultra, decision models that return calibrated answers rather than generated text and now accept audio alongside text and images. The release adds five tasks, including value extraction, span finding and object location. Liquid says d1.2-ultra scores 75 on the Artificial Analysis Decision Index v2.1, the top score, with API pricing from $0.02 per million input tokens.
