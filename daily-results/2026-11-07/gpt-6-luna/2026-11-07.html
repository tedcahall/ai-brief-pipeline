🌐 TOP STORIES

Nous Research releases Arete-27B, a post-trained Qwen3.8 model

Nous Research released Arete-27B, a post-trained version of Alibaba’s Qwen3.8-27B focused on changing model behavior across political and social questions. Nous says its 803-prompt evaluation measured a reduction in responses it classified as propaganda from 22% to about 1%, with no meaningful capability regression on its reported Hermes Bench comparison. The weights, evaluation prompts and training data are linked by the lab; these are developer-reported results.

Meta rolls out real-time voice and expressive avatars for Muse

Meta says voice conversations and expressive avatars are rolling out in the Muse app in the United States, with availability on mobile, web and Mac. Users can choose among hundreds of voices, interrupt the assistant, and select voice-only or avatar presentation. Meta says the feature uses Muse Realtime Voice and Muse Realtime Avatar technology; expansion to AI Glasses and Muse Charm is planned.

Democratic leaders press for restrictions on AI data-center expansion

Following the November 2026 midterm elections, several Democratic lawmakers and elected officials called for tighter limits on data-center development and AI expansion. CNBC reports Sen. Bernie Sanders urged a national moratorium, while Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa had pledged to pause new data-center approvals pending legislation. These are political proposals, not enacted national restrictions; the debate could affect permitting and infrastructure plans.

MIT researchers develop a robot system to correct unreliable object memories

MIT researchers described UQ-DAAAM, a method that flags uncertain object descriptions in a robot’s stored memory, gathers additional views and updates the description. The team says this improved answers about objects, locations and when they were seen compared with baseline methods, with a streamlined process for continual updates on mobile robots. The work is to be presented at NeurIPS; broader household and industrial uses remain prospective.

AI demand pushes advanced chip packaging toward a capacity bottleneck

A Reuters report describes advanced packaging as a growing constraint on AI chip supply, as demand for accelerators outpaces available capacity. It says Nvidia, AMD and other chip designers are expanding production or seeking additional suppliers, while research firm Yole forecasts capital spending on advanced packaging near $28 billion in 2026. The article is based on interviews with industry executives and analysts; the spending figure is a forecast.
