🌐 TOP STORIES

Thinking Machines launches Inkling 2 Small, with open weights planned

Thinking Machines introduced a 594B-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 17B active parameters, image inputs and an Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index score of 31. Fine-tuning and inference are available through Tinker. The company plans Apache 2.0 weights after external safety testing; the launch is not yet an open-weight release.

Meta brings Muse Wonder-trained models to Muse and Meta AI

Meta is rolling out specialized versions of existing models trained using Muse Wonder. Active Memory targets relevant information across conversations, while Active Focus targets recovery when tasks encounter obstacles. Meta reports reduced forgetting and improved task completion in its own evaluations; those figures are company results, not independent evidence of general reliability.

Reflection details Beam’s training and million-token context

Reflection published its Beam technical report, describing a 501.3B-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 23.4B active parameters and a one-million-token context window. The report documents pretraining on 23.8T tokens and an asynchronous reinforcement-learning run generating over 100 million rollouts. These are the lab’s disclosures; the new development is the report, not a newly verified launch date.

METR estimates faster AI research progress at Anthropic, with major caveats

METR estimates Anthropic’s benchmark capability gains accelerated to 1.9 times their earlier rate, with a 95% confidence interval of 1.5–2.8 times. It judges AI assistance likely explains most current acceleration, but emphasizes uncertainty. Anthropic redacted much of the evidence METR found most informative, limiting public scrutiny; the findings do not demonstrate fully autonomous recursive improvement.

Frontier Model Forum introduces a cyber-jailbreak risk framework

The Frontier Model Forum released a preliminary framework for comparing successful cyber jailbreaks using capability gain, capability scope, ease of deployment and ease of discovery. Member companies contributed to the work. Version 1.0 is intended to help prioritize safeguards and remains subject to testing and public feedback; it is not evidence that a particular model escaped containment.
