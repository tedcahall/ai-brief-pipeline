🌐 TOP STORIES

Thinking Machines Launches Inkling 2 Small, With Apache 2.0 Weights Planned

Thinking Machines Lab introduced Inkling 2 Small, its second model generation: a mixture-of-experts model with 594B total and 17B active parameters, image input and variable effort. It scores 31 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index. The model launches with closed weights on Tinker; the lab says it intends to release full weights under Apache 2.0 after external safety testing and red-teaming.

Google DeepMind Agents Uncover Two Weaknesses in Legacy RSA-1024

Google DeepMind said AI agents working under human supervision found two weaknesses in legacy 1024-bit RSA. Padding adds almost no forgery protection once an attacker controls about half the signed block, and a new algorithm theoretically improves the best attack about 1,600x even with a 32-byte digest. A second result cuts per-key batch factoring cost about 30x. Modern 2048-bit keys are unaffected.

Meta Brings Muse Wonder-Trained Specialized Models to Muse and Meta AI

Meta is rolling out specialized versions of its existing models, trained using its Muse Wonder frontier series, to people using Muse and Meta AI. The models carry Muse Wonder's Active Memory and Active Focus features. Meta reports a 2.4x reduction in forgetting goal-critical information and a 67% improvement on everyday tasks where unexpected issues arise, both measured in Muse.

Nous Research Releases Arete-27B to Strip State-Aligned Behavior From Qwen3.8

Nous Research post-trained Alibaba's Qwen3.8-27B into Arete-27B to counter Chinese state-aligned behavior in open weights. On its own eval across seven value categories, Nous reports answers containing propaganda fell from 22% to 0.9% and its fact score rose from 2.32 to 2.98 out of 5, without meaningful regression on the capability benchmarks it ran. Weights, eval and training data are public.

Justice Department Launches Super Intelligence Task Force

The US Justice Department formed a Super Intelligence Task Force led by Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward and Associate Deputy Attorney General Stephen Weiss. It targets misuse of advanced AI by foreign state actors, transnational criminal groups and fraudsters. Weiss said the department also aims to harden reporting on AI risk and ensure companies exercise internal controls so the technology behaves as intended.
