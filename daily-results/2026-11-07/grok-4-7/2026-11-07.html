🌐 TOP STORIES

Thinking Machines launches Inkling 2 Small, a 594B model with weights planned next week

Thinking Machines introduced Inkling 2 Small on November 5, a mixture-of-experts model with 594 billion total parameters and 17 billion active. It accepts image inputs and is available now for inference and fine-tuning on Tinker with closed weights. The lab says it scores 31 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index and intends to release Apache 2.0 weights next week after external safety testing and red-teaming.

Meta brings Muse Wonder memory and focus models to Muse and Meta AI

Meta said on November 6 that it is rolling specialized models trained with Muse Wonder into Muse and Meta AI, adding Active Memory and Active Focus. Meta reports a 2.4x reduction in forgetting information it calls critical to a user's goals, and a 67% improvement at completing everyday tasks when unexpected issues arise. Those figures are Meta's own measurements, not an independent evaluation.

Anthropic details how Claude dreaming reorganizes agent memory

Anthropic's November 6 engineering post describes dreaming, an offline pass that reorganizes Claude's notes between sessions. In an internal document test, a dreamed wiki alone scored 48%, while search plus the wiki rose from 54% to 61% as the wiki grew. The post is an account of memory design, not a new model release.

OpenAI and Ligo publish BioBasicsBench for wet-lab agent analysis

OpenAI and enzyme-design company Ligo released BioBasicsBench on November 6, a 100-task test of whether agents can read raw wet-lab evidence, catch errors, and stop when the data do not support a conclusion. At the highest reported reasoning setting, OpenAI says GPT-6 Astra scored 66.7% of available credit versus 43.8% for GPT-5.6 Sol. Those scores are OpenAI's own evaluation, with five attempts on each task.

Nous Research releases Arete-27B, a Qwen values fine-tune

Nous Research released Arete-27B on November 6, a post-train of Alibaba's Qwen3.8-27B, with weights, eval prompts, and training pairs posted publicly. Nous reports that on its 803-prompt eval, graded by Grok 4.7, the base model gave propaganda-style answers on 22% of prompts, versus 0.9% for Arete-27B with thinking off at temperature 0. The result is Nous's measurement, not an independent audit.
