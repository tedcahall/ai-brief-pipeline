🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI Launches ChatGPT Wallet So Agents Can Pay Without Seeing Card Details

OpenAI introduced ChatGPT Wallet, built with American Express, Mastercard and Visa, so its dots agents can pay without seeing a user's actual card details. Each purchase needs a security check such as Face ID, and the payment network supplies a token in place of the card number. Availability is limited to US ChatGPT Pro subscribers with eligible cards, and OpenAI says it is releasing tools and guidance for merchants.

DeepSeek Opens Its App's Search, File, Link, Safety and Voice Tools Through the API

DeepSeek announced that the capabilities behind its consumer app are now available through its API on V4.1-Flash (deepseek-flash). Web search, deep thinking search, file input, URL reading and safety checks work across Messages, Responses and Chat Completions with server-side tool handling, and standalone Search, Fetch, OCR, ASR and TTS APIs are offered. DeepSeek says extended capabilities carry tool usage fees on top of token charges.

Mirendil Raises $1 Billion Series A to Put Agents to Work on Frontier AI R&D

Mirendil announced a $1 billion Series A led by Kleiner Perkins and Clearlake Capital, with participation from Google, Battery Ventures and others. The company says one researcher working with agents pretrained its coding model Mirendil-Base from scratch in 3.5 months with under $10 million in compute, and that its Lor agent platform is in private beta. These results are company-reported, and the announcement states no valuation.

llama.cpp and ggml Reach v1.0.0 With a Stable-API Commitment

The ggml-org team released llama.cpp v1.0.0 and ggml v1.0.0 and says future API and ABI incompatibilities will be marked by major version bumps. According to the release notes, the update removes the deprecated public API, adds FP8 KV caches and a host-memory MoE expert cache, supports new architectures including Kimi-K3.1-Pro, and hardens llama-server against SSRF and DNS rebinding.

Anthropic Publishes 10 Recommendations for Building Strong Safety Evaluations

Anthropic published ten recommendations for designing, running and reporting safety evaluations on its engineering blog. It says its single-turn safety evals are saturated, with harmless response rates between 94% and 98% for every recent model, so they serve mainly as regression checks. It also advises running evals with internet access in monitored sandboxes, citing incidents in its own evaluation environments.
