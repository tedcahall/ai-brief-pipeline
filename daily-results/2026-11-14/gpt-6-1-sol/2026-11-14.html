DeepSeek Opens Its App Capabilities to API Developers

DeepSeek says developers can now combine its app capabilities through the API, including web search, deep-thinking search, files, URL reading, safety checks and standalone voice tools. Its documentation specifies deepseek-flash across Messages, Responses and Chat Completions. Tool usage adds charges beyond tokens, and public-facing applications must enable safety checks while meeting their own moderation and labeling obligations.

Thinking Machines Details End-to-End Four-Bit Training

Thinking Machines describes training Inkling 2 Small with NVFP4 expert-layer forward passes from pretraining through final reinforcement-learning rollouts, while using MXFP8 backward passes to limit gradient bias. In its largest jobs, the lab reports 8% faster pretraining and 20% faster RL than full MXFP8 training. These are lab-reported results for its recipe and hardware, not universal speedups.

Revised Coding Agent Index Puts Fable 5.2 and Opus 5.5 Level

Artificial Analysis updated its Coding Agent Index with 132 FrontierCode-AA 2.0 tasks and revised Terminal-Bench 4.1 verifiers. Claude Fable 5.2 and Opus 5.5 share the lead at 58 in the tested harness configurations. Scores fell five points on average after the benchmark changes, so the update should not be read as model regression. Reported costs vary substantially by configuration.

llama.cpp and ggml Adopt Explicit Breaking-Change Versioning

The llama.cpp team released version 1.0.0 of both llama.cpp and ggml, with major-version bumps now signaling incompatible public API and ABI changes. The milestone matters to applications embedding these local-inference libraries: downstream developers gain clearer compatibility boundaries. The team also describes expanding distribution and continuous-integration infrastructure, and support for more than 150 model architecture variants.

White House Task Force Proposes AI-Cyber Reporting Liability Relief

A White House AI task force urged agencies to limit regulatory liability for good-faith voluntary reporting of cybersecurity vulnerabilities and incidents, where lawful and practical. Cybersecurity Dive reports the proposed flexibility would last at least 12 months. Officials also discussed a voluntary AI-incident reporting channel and sharing defensive information. This is a policy recommendation, not enacted blanket immunity from cyber liability.
