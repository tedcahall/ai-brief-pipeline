🌐 TOP STORIES

Mirendil Raises $1 Billion to Expand Agent-Led AI Research and Development

Mirendil announced a $1 billion Series A led by Kleiner Perkins and Clearlake Capital, with Google and other investors participating. The company says one researcher working with agents pretrained its Mirendil-Base coding model from scratch in 3.5 months using less than $10 million in compute, then post-trained it into Fern. These are company-reported results; the announcement did not state a valuation.

Google DeepMind Opens WeatherNext 3’s PARDIG and SatWeave Data

Google DeepMind released historical archives and live feeds for PARDIG precipitation estimates and SatWeave satellite imagery, datasets used in its WeatherNext 3 model. SatWeave combines observations from geostationary satellites into a global mosaic and updates hourly; PARDIG estimates precipitation globally. The company says it is making the data available to researchers and developers for weather, hydrology and climate applications.

DeepSeek Adds Search, File, URL and Safety Tools to V4.1-Flash APIs

DeepSeek says its V4.1-Flash API now supports server-side web and deep-thinking search, file parsing, URL reading and safety checks across Messages, Responses and Chat Completions. It also offers Search, Fetch, OCR, speech recognition and speech synthesis as standalone APIs. DeepSeek says the expanded tools carry additional usage fees and requires public-facing applications to enable safety checks.

White House AI Task Force Urges 12-Month Cyber Incident Liability Relief

After its second meeting, the White House AI task force recommended that federal agencies limit regulatory liability for good-faith voluntary reporting of cybersecurity vulnerabilities and incidents, where lawful and consistent with public interests. It said the flexibility should last at least 12 months, with possible extension. This is a task-force recommendation, not an enacted change; the group also called for clear, practical incident-reporting requirements.

DeepStress Tests Whether Search Agents Trust Degraded Evidence

A November 12 revision of the DeepStress preprint introduces a controlled test environment that varies the trustworthiness, relevance and factuality of documents returned to search agents. In tests of 12 systems on HotpotQA and BrowseComp-Plus, the authors report that several open-weight agents answered confidently despite obvious credibility warnings, while many HotpotQA answers relied on model knowledge even when retrieved documents were false. The work is a preprint, not peer-reviewed publication.
