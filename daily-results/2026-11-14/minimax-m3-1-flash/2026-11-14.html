🌐 TOP STORIES

Thinking Machines Trains Inkling 2 Small End to End With a Native 4-Bit Forward Pass

New developments: Thinking Machines Lab says Inkling 2 Small is the largest model it knows of trained end to end with a native 4-bit forward pass, using NVFP4 for forward passes and MXFP8 with stochastic rounding for backward passes. The company reports an 8 percent wall-clock speedup in pretraining and a 20 percent speedup in reinforcement learning over full MXFP8 training, and says the recipe removes the need to calibrate a quantized checkpoint when training ends.

DeepSeek Opens the Full Experience of Its App to Developers Through the API

V4.1-Flash now supports web search with citations, deep thinking search, file input for PDFs and Office documents, URL reading and safety checks across the Messages, Responses and Chat Completions APIs, with tool calls and context assembly handled server side. DeepSeek also offers search, fetch, OCR, ASR and TTS as standalone APIs, and says extended capabilities add tool usage fees on top of standard token charges.

vLLM Serves DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash at 58 Times GB300 Throughput on Vera Rubin

The project reports that one NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 rack serves DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash at 1.7 million tokens per second per GPU and 387 tokens per second per user on the SemiAnalysis AgentX workload, which it describes as 58 times the GB300 NVL72 result. It also cites 2.52 million tokens per second per GPU at peak throughput and 1,810 tokens per second for a single session, with the work led by the Inferact team.

LMSYS Org Launches the SGLang Consortium With NVIDIA, AMD, Intel, Google and Meta

The nonprofit behind the open-source SGLang inference engine launched the consortium in the opening keynote of the inaugural SGLang Summit in San Francisco. Founding members join the maintainers in a standing forum with two roadmap meetings a year and working groups on frontier model support, reasoning and multimodal workloads, distributed inference and hardware optimization, while code acceptance stays with the maintainers.

Anthropic Publishes Ten Recommendations for Building Strong Safety Evaluations

The guidance covers staffing evals with subject-matter and methods experts, running them in sandboxes with real-time monitoring, matching observational or causal design to the question, measuring construct validity beyond refusal rates, generalizing with multi-turn simulations, controlling for contamination and reward hacking, and calibrating graders. Anthropic notes that single-turn aggregate harmless-response rates now run between 94 and 98 percent for every recent model.
