🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI Launches ChatGPT Wallet So Its Dots Agents Can Pay for Purchases

OpenAI introduced ChatGPT Wallet, built with American Express, Mastercard and Visa, letting its dots agents complete purchases using network payment tokens instead of users' actual card details. Each purchase requires the user to review it and pass a security check such as Face ID, and cardholders keep existing rewards and dispute protections. It launches first for ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the US, alongside a Commerce Partner Playbook for businesses.

Mirendil Raises $1 Billion Series A to Bring Frontier AI R&D to Every Scientist

Mirendil announced a $1 billion Series A led by Kleiner Perkins and Clearlake Capital, with Google, Battery Ventures and others participating. The company says one researcher working with agents pretrained its Mirendil-Base coding model from scratch in 3.5 months on under $10 million of compute, and that its Lor platform, now in private beta, runs hundreds of agents writing code and running experiments for days at a time.

DeepSeek Opens Its App's Search, File, Voice and Safety Tools Through the API

DeepSeek made the capabilities behind its consumer app available through its API. V4.1-Flash now supports server-side web search, deep thinking search, URL reading and file input across the Messages, Responses and Chat Completions interfaces, and OCR, speech recognition and text-to-speech are offered as standalone APIs. DeepSeek says these tools carry usage fees on top of standard token charges and released an open-source frontend demo.

FAA Rules Out Its New SMART AI Tool as Cause of Nationwide Ground Stop

The FAA lifted a nationwide ground stop on Friday evening after a telecommunications outage halted departures for more than four hours. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the agency's recently introduced SMART AI system only makes recommendations to controllers and is not connected to its STARS or ERAM systems. Officials blamed overloaded switches and routers in aging infrastructure and said they found no evidence of a cyberattack or AI interference, though the investigation continues.

White House AI Task Force Urges a Year of Liability Relief for Good-Faith Cyber Reporting

The Trump administration's AI task force said earlier standards for regulatory action after cyber incidents may be outdated as AI raises attack risk, and urged agencies to limit liability for good-faith voluntary reporting of vulnerabilities and incidents for at least 12 months. Officials also discussed a voluntary process for companies to report AI-related incidents and said the NSA and NIST have been testing frontier models with major labs.
