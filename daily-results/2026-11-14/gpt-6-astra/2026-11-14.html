📊 AI INDUSTRY PULSE

DeepSeek is opening its app capabilities to developers through expanded APIs, while Thinking Machines has disclosed how native four-bit forward passes accelerated Inkling 2 Small training. The latter reports measured gains on its own training jobs, not a universal speed advantage.

Agent deployment is the other focus: OpenAI is adding security investigation and patching tools, and Cognition is putting private internal applications directly on Devin’s cloud computers. Cohere’s planned Japanese entity and in-region inference expansion address a different deployment requirement: keeping enterprise AI workloads under local control.

🌐 TOP STORIES

DeepSeek Brings Search, Files and Voice to Its API

DeepSeek says developers can now recreate its app experience through APIs for search, file input, URL reading, safety checks and voice. V4.1-Flash handles built-in tool calls and context assembly server-side across three API formats. Standalone OCR, speech recognition and speech synthesis are also available. Tool usage carries additional fees, and public-facing applications must enable safety checks.

Thinking Machines Details Native Four-Bit Training for Inkling 2 Small

New developments: Thinking Machines published the training recipe behind Inkling 2 Small, extending last week’s model announcement with technical evidence. The lab uses NVFP4 for expert-layer forward passes and MXFP8 for backward passes, rather than four-bit arithmetic everywhere. It reports an 8% pretraining speedup and 20% reinforcement-learning speedup over full MXFP8 training in its largest jobs.

OpenAI Introduces Agent Tools for Security Patching and Red-Teaming

OpenAI’s Daybreak Defense Factory investigates vulnerabilities and develops patches for engineers to review and test before merging. Its companion Daybreak Red Team, offered in early access, uses coordinated agents to test web applications, validate reported vulnerabilities and check fixes. OpenAI describes isolated environments and Guardian review as scope controls; the product pages do not establish independent effectiveness measurements.

Cognition Launches Private Web Applications on Devin’s Cloud Computer

Devin Apps lets teams describe an internal tool, have Devin build it and share a stable link with authorized colleagues. The applications run on Devin’s cloud computer with access to existing integrations and code, sleeping when unused and billing for awake time. Cognition says the single-machine design suits internal tools and dashboards, not high-traffic production services.

Cohere Plans Japanese Entity and Expands In-Region AI Infrastructure

Cohere announced plans for a Japanese legal entity, local partner training and expansion of its Model Vault inference infrastructure in Japan. The company says local compute keeps prompts and responses within the country. Its Fujitsu-developed Takane model is selected for government evaluation. The announcement references a Series E targeting up to CAD $7 billion, not a completed raise of that amount.
