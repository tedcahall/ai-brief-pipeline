🌐 TOP STORIES

Thinking Machines Opens Inkling 2 Small Weights, Trained End to End in 4-Bit

Thinking Machines Lab released the weights of Inkling 2 Small on Hugging Face under Apache 2.0 on November 12, then detailed how it trained the 594B-parameter mixture-of-experts model with a native 4-bit NVFP4 forward pass from pretraining through reinforcement learning. The lab reports an 8% pretraining speedup and a 20% RL speedup over full MXFP8 training. Arena ranks it the top US open model in Code Arena WebDev.

OpenAI Introduces ChatGPT Wallet for Agent Purchases

OpenAI introduced ChatGPT Wallet, built with American Express, Mastercard and Visa, which lets its dots agents pay with network-issued payment tokens instead of users' actual card details. Each purchase requires user approval with a security check such as Face ID. It is rolling out to US ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 and older, alongside a Commerce Partner Playbook for businesses. OpenAI says 1.2 billion people use ChatGPT each week.

DeepSeek Opens Its Full App Capabilities Through the API

DeepSeek opened the capabilities behind its consumer app to developers on November 13. Its changelog says the deepseek-flash model now supports web search, deep thinking search, file input, URL reading and a safety check across the Messages, Responses and Chat Completions APIs, with tool calls and context assembly handled server-side. Search, Fetch, OCR, ASR and TTS are also offered as standalone APIs, alongside an open-source frontend demo.

Mirendil Raises $1 Billion Series A for Agent-Driven AI R&D

Mirendil announced a $1 billion Series A led by Kleiner Perkins and Clearlake Capital, with participation from Battery Ventures, Google and others. The lab says one researcher working with agents pretrained its Mirendil-Base coding model in 3.5 months for under $10 million in compute. The release states no valuation, and the performance claims are the company's own. Crunchbase ranked it the week's largest US round, ahead of Mercor's $750 million Series D.

White House AI Task Force Urges 12-Month Liability Relief for Cyber Incident Reporting

The Trump administration's AI task force said after its second meeting that regulatory standards for acting against companies hit by cyberattacks may be outdated as AI changes the threat environment, Cybersecurity Dive reports. It urged agencies to limit regulatory liability for good-faith voluntary reporting of vulnerabilities and incidents for at least 12 months, and officials discussed a voluntary channel for reporting AI-related incidents.
