🌐 TOP STORIES

Google’s Project Suncatcher Gets Its First In-Orbit AI Response

Google says its Project Suncatcher prototype received its first AI response from a data-center-class TPU in orbit. During a ground-station pass, the team sent a prompt to the onboard open Gemma 4 model and received a haiku back on Earth. The company described the result as an early milestone; thermal and radiation testing and a planned twin-satellite test remain ahead.

OpenAI and ServiceNow Expand Their Enterprise AI Partnership

OpenAI and ServiceNow say they are expanding their partnership to connect ServiceNow workflows with ChatGPT and Codex and bring more OpenAI models and agents into ServiceNow products. A planned ChatGPT integration lets employees investigate IT issues and update records within configured permissions. Further work is planned in application development, cybersecurity and voice; these are announced plans and previews, not evidence of broad deployment.

OpenAI Adds Community Safety Reporting and New Protections for Teens

OpenAI announced additional ChatGPT restrictions concerning firearms content for minors and a planned Community Safety Report on violence prevention and safeguard effectiveness. The company says it made more than 200 law-enforcement referrals in the third quarter of 2026 and expects its first report, covering the second half of the year, in early 2027. The announcement describes planned transparency, not an already-published report.

Anthropic Expands Its AI for Science Program to Research Institutions

Anthropic says it is extending its AI for Science program through partnerships with 20 scientific foundations, institutions and research groups, providing Claude credits for high-impact projects. It cites work with the Allen Institute using teams of AI agents to analyze open biological datasets and a research-preview standard for agents operating lab instruments. Anthropic frames the effort as widening researcher access and supporting shared scientific resources.

Google Opens a Connected Apps Platform for Gemini and Search Developers

Google announced a preview platform for developers to build and manage connected apps for Gemini and Search. Developers can integrate through cloud-hosted MCP or Android MCP using AppFunctions APIs, submit apps through a console, and monitor traffic, latency and errors after launch. Google said access would open in phases and that more than 50 partner apps were already available across Gemini and Search.
