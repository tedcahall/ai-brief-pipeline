🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI Rolls Out GPT-6 Rosalind Research to Approved Life-Science Teams

OpenAI is rolling out GPT-6 Rosalind Research to a limited set of approved organizations for multistep life-sciences work. It is available in Work and Codex, with API access requiring separate approval, but not in the Chat tab. Enterprise access begins with a two-week, off-by-default Admin Preview. Discovery-tier approval does not unlock Research access, and existing GPT-5.5 Research workflows remain supported.

Google Runs Gemma 4 on an Orbiting TPU and Receives a Haiku

Google says its Project Suncatcher prototype received a ground-station prompt, ran the Gemma 4 open model on a data-center-class TPU in orbit, and downlinked a haiku near Svalbard. The team subsequently tried limericks and telemetry prompts. Thermal-stress and radiation tests come next, ahead of a planned twin-satellite milestone in 2027. This is a research demonstration, not a commercial deployment.

Meta FAIR Tests Routed Memory for Continually Learning Agents

Meta FAIR researchers propose Routed Continual Learning, which assigns prior agent experience to clusters and selects a memory system for each task. Across four models on an interleaved benchmark, the authors report reward gains of 15–30% of the maximum attainable normalized reward and estimated API-cost reductions of 65–72% versus one global history. The evaluation is sequential; concurrent updates to shared clusters remain untested.

Musk Details Colossus 2 Training and Inference Buildout

Elon Musk says Macroharder and Minihard each have 220,000 GB300 chips configured for training, with the Gun Range site planned to match them. He says the Spam facility will have about 200,000 GB300s configured for inference, requiring simpler networking and less than half the fiber. His projected operating dates are late November for Gun Range and December for Spam; these are company forecasts, not independently verified capacity.

DeepMind Expands PlanAssist Testing to 12 More English Councils

Google DeepMind says its Gemini-based PlanAssist cut average officer time on householder assessment tasks by 58% in early pilots across Barnet, Camden and Dorset. The tool is entering a 12-month beta, with 12 additional councils joining in cohorts. It checks documents and planning constraints and drafts cited reports, but qualified officers retain approval authority. Wider availability in 2027 remains conditional on successful testing.
