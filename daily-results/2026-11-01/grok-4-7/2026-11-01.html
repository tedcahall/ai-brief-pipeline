🌐 TOP STORIES

Apple Will Bring Apple Intelligence to China With Alibaba's Qwen

Apple said it will bring the first Apple Intelligence features to China with iOS 27.3, The Verge reported from Apple's October 31 newsroom post. A developer beta is set for November 4, and the public release is scheduled for December. The Verge said the launch covers earlier tools, including live translation, writing tools, and photo cleanup, not the newer Siri AI. Apple said it worked with Alibaba to add Qwen in some experiences.

OpenAI Publishes GPT-Rosalind-6 for Trusted Research Access

OpenAI published a GPT-Rosalind-6 system card addendum on October 30. The model uses the same weights as GPT-6 Astra, and OpenAI said it added no training data and did no further post-training. It is offered to qualified organizations as Research access, not as a general ChatGPT model. OpenAI said it is meant to help legitimate advanced life sciences work, including some dual-use topics general-access models may refuse, while still refusing severe biological weaponization requests.

Google Says Project Suncatcher Returned Its First Orbital TPU Response

Google said on October 30 that its Project Suncatcher prototype returned a prompt response from a data-center-class TPU in orbit. During a ground-station pass, the team uplinked a prompt to onboard Gemma 4 and asked for a space haiku. Google said the downlink over Svalbard was what it called the first AI response from a data-center-class TPU in orbit. The post describes a research test, not a commercial service, with twin satellites planned for 2027.

Judge Lets NAACP Suit Over xAI Colossus Turbines Proceed

A federal judge denied xAI's motion to dismiss an NAACP lawsuit over gas turbines at the Colossus data center outside Memphis, TechCrunch reported on October 31. The suit says xAI operated at least 35 combustion turbines without required air permits and violated the Clean Air Act. Judge Debra M. Brown rejected arguments that the turbines were exempt mobile sources and that the plaintiffs lacked standing. TechCrunch said it asked SpaceX, which owns xAI, for comment.

TBC Launches Mischief 1 as Software, Not a Living-Neuron Model

The Biological Computing Co. said on October 30 that it launched TBC mischief 1, an image-to-video model built on Wan 2.2 and available on fal and OpenRouter. The company said it is five times faster than that base model, matches its quality, and is the cheapest image-to-video option on both sites. Those claims are the company's, not independent results. TBC said the neurons stay in the lab, and customers run software on ordinary AI hardware.
