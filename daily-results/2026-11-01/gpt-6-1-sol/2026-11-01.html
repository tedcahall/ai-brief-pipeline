🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI releases GPT-Rosalind-6 for trusted life-sciences research

OpenAI released GPT-Rosalind-6 for approved research organizations, with the same weights as GPT-6 Astra and no additional post-training. Its deployment policy supports legitimate advanced biological work that general-access Astra may refuse, while retaining weaponization safeguards. OpenAI treats the launch as High capability in biology and chemistry; access controls and qualified oversight remain central to this restricted release.

Google receives an in-orbit Gemma 4 response from Project Suncatcher

Google says its Project Suncatcher prototype ran Gemma 4 on a data-center-class TPU in orbit, receiving a generated haiku back through a ground-station pass near Svalbard. The team subsequently tested limericks and telemetry prompts. Thermal and radiation tests come next; this is an experimental milestone toward interconnected satellites, not evidence of a commercially deployed orbital AI service.

SemiAnalysis reports an AMD inference-cost advantage on Kimi K3

SemiAnalysis reports up to twice the performance per dollar for AMD MI355X versus Nvidia B200 on Kimi K3 agentic inference. It credits AITER and MonoKernel optimization plus greater memory per server, which reduces cross-server traffic. The comparison concerns a specific workload and modeled ownership costs, not universal accelerator superiority or guaranteed savings at public cloud rental prices.

Routed Continual Learning reports lower agent-memory costs

A new research paper routes agent experience into separate memory clusters instead of one global history. Across four models on 398 interleaved benchmark tasks, the authors report reward gains of 15–30% of maximum attainable normalized reward and estimated API-cost reductions of 65–72% against global in-context history. These are benchmark results, not a demonstrated production deployment or proof of parallel-update safety.

Google expands PlanAssist to 12 more English councils

Google DeepMind is expanding its Gemini-based PlanAssist to 12 additional councils in England for a 12-month beta. In three pilot councils, Google reports a 58% reduction in officer time spent on householder assessment tasks. Planning officers retain final decisions and review generated assessments. The figure measures staff assessment work, not a verified reduction in end-to-end planning approval time.
