🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI Ships GPT-Rosalind-6 Research, Its Trusted-Access Biology Model

OpenAI released GPT-Rosalind-6 Research on October 30, a frontier reasoning model for biology, drug discovery and translational medicine. According to its system card addendum, the model uses the same weights as GPT-6 Astra with no additional training and is offered only to qualified scientists, research institutes, companies and government partners through a trusted-access program rather than open availability.

Apple Brings Alibaba's Qwen Into Apple Intelligence for China

Apple confirmed that Alibaba's Qwen model will power parts of Apple Intelligence for mainland China, including within Siri and Writing Tools, with the iOS 27.3 developer beta arriving November 4 and the full release in December. Reports note the first feature set reaches Chinese iPhones with Qwen integrated, while some Siri AI features are initially left out of the rollout.

Google's Suncatcher Satellite Returns First AI Response From an Orbiting TPU

Google's Project Suncatcher prototype satellite returned its first AI response from orbit: during a ground station pass the team uplinked a prompt to an onboard Gemma 4 open model running on a data center class TPU and received a generated haiku about being in space. Google describes the milestone as the first AI response from a TPU of this class in orbit and a step toward space-based AI compute, with twin interconnected satellites planned for 2027.

OpenAI Cuts Fundraising Target to About $20 Billion Amid AI Selloff

The Financial Times reported that OpenAI cut its latest fundraising target to about $20 billion from roughly $30 billion, with the reported valuation expectation reduced to around $1.25 trillion, after a sharp selloff in AI-linked stocks. CNBC and Bloomberg corroborated the report, which follows weeks of heavy AI infrastructure spending announcements and a broad pullback in tech valuations.

AMD Reports Up to 2x Better Performance per Dollar Than B200 on Kimi K3 Inference

SemiAnalysis reported that AMD's team achieved up to 2x better performance per dollar than Nvidia's B200 on Kimi K3 agentic inference, crediting software optimizations including AITER kernel tuning and MonoKernel decode plus the MI355X's larger per-server HBM capacity. The figures are AMD-attributed benchmark work relayed by SemiAnalysis rather than independent third-party measurements.
