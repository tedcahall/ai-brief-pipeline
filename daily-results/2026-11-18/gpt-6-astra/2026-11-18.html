📊 AI INDUSTRY PULSE

OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, while Microsoft introduced MAI models aimed at reducing the cost of production coding and security work. Both companies emphasize efficiency, but their benchmark claims describe specific tests rather than universal performance.

🌐 TOP STORIES

OpenAI releases GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol with lower task costs

New developments: OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol in ChatGPT Work, Codex and its API. The company says both complete demanding tasks with fewer output tokens; Astra’s standard input and output prices are 20% below GPT-6 Astra. Reported benchmark gains are OpenAI’s measurements, and the release introduces tighter restrictions on higher-risk cybersecurity assistance.

Microsoft launches MAI models focused on production economics

Microsoft announced a new MAI lineup spanning coding, cyber defense, images, speech and model routing. Its MAI Code 2 comparisons emphasize everyday engineering rather than fundamental science or very long-horizon work. Microsoft reports 98% of GPT-6.1 Sol’s performance at 40% of its price across four selected coding benchmarks; those results remain vendor-reported.

Tencent releases open-weight Hy4 flash

New developments: Tencent released Hy4 flash weights, including FP8 and base variants, under the Tencent Hy License. The model accepts text and images and supports a one-million-token context. Its model card targets everyday productivity and recommends the larger Hy4 for more complex, long-horizon software engineering. Open-weight availability should not be confused with an unrestricted open-source license.

Isomorphic Labs raises $5 billion for AI drug development

Isomorphic Labs announced a $5 billion Series C co-led by Greenoaks and Sequoia Capital, with participation from Andreessen Horowitz and continued investment from Alphabet. The company says the money will support its proprietary AI drug-design engine and therapeutic pipeline. This is new funding, not a valuation figure or evidence that its candidate medicines have achieved clinical success.

Google expands free Gemini Tasks to six countries

Google announced free Tasks access for eligible users in the United States, Japan, South Korea, Canada, Australia and India. Tasks handle longer projects and recurring schedules, rather than only conversational answers. Related official posts describe Google-app actions and advanced calling and payment features, with the advanced rollout starting in the United States and availability varying by region.
