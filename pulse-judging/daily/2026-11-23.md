**glm:** Best: R earns the top spot because it converts the day into a single story — OpenAI's perplexity proposal is framed as "a first attempt to turn safety pacing into a shared measurable standard," Microsoft's 0.819 is "the first cross-lab number offered under it," and each later item (Wang's Muse delay, Ant's open weights, the Nasdaq close) is tied back to pacing and AI exposure rather than merely delivered. E and O are close behind: E sustains narrative momentum across three well-proportioned paragraphs, and O is the clearest of the compact entries, explaining perplexity's purpose ("wants labs to agree on a measure of training progress before they agree on a limit") in language an executive can use. Worst: U loses because its second paragraph is a single sentence fusing "Ant Group open-sourced two medical AI models, Amazon threat intelligence reported... and vLLM and AMD showed" — three unrelated items with no throughline — and its closing on Meta "defending the company's safety record amid a public debate" is too vague to inform. C and M fail for similar reasons: C's "Microsoft is backing shared pretraining evaluations, while OpenAI leaders advocate common safety limits" says almost nothing concrete, and M spends its scarce words on disclaimers ("medical benchmarks are not clinical validation... is not an industry agreement") rather than news. F is dragged down by repeated hedging ("moderate-confidence," "attributed to their developers and specific test conditions"). No summary earned a 5: every entry had at least one list-y paragraph or an unexplained leap, and several wasted their best asset — the why-it-matters line — on vague framing sentences.

**astra:** F wins through lucid functional descriptions—"one built for reasoning through clinical tasks, the other for interpreting images and writing reports"—and the memorable coordination framing "before they agree on a limit." Its abrupt change of subject prevents a higher score. B loses because "Amazon is in advanced talks" becomes a long acquisition-and-intrusion sentence, then "Ant Group's InclusionAI released" launches an even longer inventory containing "GB300 NVL72" and "Kimi K3.1 Pro" with no explanatory hierarchy.

**grok:** P won because every sentence is a point a non-specialist can use: the models are 'one built for reasoning through clinical tasks, the other for interpreting images and writing reports,' and the safety news is a sequence rather than a dump — 'agree on a measure of training progress before they agree on a limit,' with Microsoft having 'put a number on the table' on 'the same test data and tokenizer.' That withheld number, and the unnamed metric, keep it off a 5. N lost because it opens on a disclaimer, 'medical benchmarks do not establish clinical effectiveness,' and closes on an absence, 'rather than independent evidence of driving safety,' with governors and Tesla lashed together by 'while.'

**dsflash:** E wins because it alone builds a narrative rather than a menu: the perplexity proposal is stated as a mechanism ("proposing perplexity (cross-entropy loss) as a common metric"), and the intrusion paragraph earns its detail with a sequence — "The agents went from mapping the application's API to writing files on the server in about 11 minutes, then planted a reverse shell to gain root access." It still pays for its close, where a funding round and a 2.8-trillion-parameter model are welded into one sentence. O loses because it is all posture and no content: "Both developments carry important qualifications: medical benchmarks are not clinical validation, and the proposed safety standard is not an industry agreement" spends a whole sentence on what the news is not, then closes with a three-way list of vague items ("Separately, researchers reported AI-assisted industrial cyberattack exercises"). N fails the same way in shorter form — "Microsoft is backing shared pretraining evaluations" and "with research-only limitations" tell the reader nothing measurable. L and G are the near-misses: both state significance ("a first attempt to turn safety pacing into a shared measurable standard"; "turning the proposal into an early test of cross-lab coordination") but L ends in a four-item pileup and G withholds every figure.
