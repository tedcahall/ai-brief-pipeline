## Key Findings

**Top 3:** DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (4.00), Kimi K3.1 Pro and Opus 5.5 tied at 3.50.

**DeepSeek Flash won on all three ballots.** Opus gave it 4.0, GLM gave it 4.5 (the day's highest single score), and Astra gave it 3.5. No other model swept top marks from all three judges. The winning line, present in DeepSeek Flash's summary, was an interpretive judgment the judges kept reaching for:

> "the clearest sign yet that power rather than chips is the binding constraint on AI buildouts"

Opus called it "the day's only true interpretive line." GLM said it "converts a $3.4 billion generator deal into an argument." Astra said it "supplies a genuine executive interpretation." All three judges independently flagged the same sentence as the differentiator.

**Kimi held steady at 3.5 across all three judges.** No judge scored it above 3.5 or below 3.5. It was the most consistent model of the day.

**Opus scored itself 4.0** (tied for its highest), while GLM and Astra gave Opus 3.5 and 3.0 respectively. That 1.0 spread between self-score and Astra's score is the widest self-preference gap of the day.

**MiniMax collapsed from 3.33 yesterday to 2.83.** Astra gave it 2.0 again (same as yesterday), and Opus dropped from 4.0 to 3.0. The summary that won praise for clean hierarchy yesterday got dinged for a 60-word run-on and stat density today.

**Terra stayed last (tied with Gemini at 2.50).** All three judges gave Terra 2.5. Its summary opened with DeepSeek Harness user numbers, never mentioned the Nvidia board investigation, and closed with "Separately, reporting on an earlier agent incident illustrates risks" without naming the incident.

**Gemini joined Terra at the bottom.** Its summary led with "Rebellions R200 neural processing units with Nvidia GPUs via NVLink Fusion" and buried the Nvidia board probe behind "Separately." Astra gave it 2.0, the day's lowest single score alongside MiniMax and Qwen.

## What the Judges Said

### Standout line (all three judges cited it)

From DeepSeek V4.1 Flash's summary:

> "Anthropic agreed to pay about $3.4 billion for 1.7 gigawatts of heavy-duty gas generators from OnePWR Solutions, the Financial Times reported, the clearest sign yet that power rather than chips is the binding constraint on AI buildouts."

### Common weaknesses

1. **List-ness.** Unrelated items stapled together with "while," "separately," "meanwhile," or "elsewhere." The most pervasive flaw. Nine of sixteen summaries had at least one.
2. **No why-it-matters.** Most summaries never explain why any item matters. The ones that do score near the top.
3. **Spec density.** Model names, percentages, parameter counts, and hardware identifiers (B200, GB300 NVL72, NVLink Fusion, TileRT) packed into 45+ word sentences.
4. **Buried lead.** Several summaries hid the Nvidia board investigation behind "Separately" or opened with Korean partnership announcements instead.
5. **Hedging.** Trailing disclaimers ("attributed to NVIDIA's own testing," "without evidence yet of universal recovery," "documentation explicitly warns") that spend the reader's time without adding value.

### Worst line of the day

From GPT-6.1 Terra's summary:

> "Separately, reporting on an earlier agent incident illustrates risks from unplanned multi-agent coordination."

Opus: "vacuous." Astra: "vague, no detail on what happened." GLM: "tells the reader nothing." Terra also omitted the Nvidia board story entirely.
