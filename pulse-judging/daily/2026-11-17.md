## Recurring Patterns (Across All Five Judges)

1. **The inventory paragraph.** Most summaries defaulted to 3–4 items stitched with "while," "also," "separately," or "the same day." No throughline, no hierarchy.
2. **Spec-stacking.** Model version strings, parameter counts, dataset sizes, and benchmark names dropped into the middle of clauses, breaking executive reading rhythm.
3. **Meta-hedging.** Disclaimer sentences addressing an editor rather than a reader ("not proof of future performance," "allegations remain unproven in court").
4. **The California lawsuit was the dominant story.** Summaries that used it as a spine and paired it with the same-day assessment deals scored best. Those that treated it as one co-equal item among five scored worst.
