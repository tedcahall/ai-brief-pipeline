## Judge notes (labels refer to each judge's own blind shuffle)

**opus:** B won by framing the day with a real throughline ('AI for science had a breakout day') and ending on a stakes line ('a flow it says could reshape open source AI if it unwinds'), though its later paragraphs slide into category-label listing ('Infrastructure also moved'). J and H were clean, well-ordered lawsuit-led pieces but stitched items together with 'separately'/'also' and never said why it matters. E finished last because of a garbled clause that breaks meaning ('backed by more than 10,000 GPUs rather than a claim that the goal is already achieved') plus vague hedging ('Safety and deployment rules remain central'); O and K lost points for density and run-ons, O also for the dangling 'ahead of a planned China trip'.

**glm:** Best: S gave the safety-assessment story real body ("Apollo examining whether models could covertly work against their developers' intentions") plus concrete follow-through ("50,000 new grants opening November 23"), with clean grammar and a clear hierarchy — only minor bundling in the middle. Q was close behind with the strongest legal framing and balanced OpenAI's response, but stayed a flat sequence. Worst: E was five unrelated bullet-like sentences joined by "while" with no throughline ("CMS outlined a policy roadmap for clinical AI... while questions about federal oversight remain"), and R spent two sentences on what things are not ("rather than a claim that the goal is already achieved"; "leaves questions... unresolved"), capping it despite clean prose.

**astra:** H wins with a clear accountability lead, readable allegation-and-response structure, and a relevant assessment follow-up: "OpenAI says the suit singles it out over a challenge every major lab has identified" gives the reader useful competing positions without editorial fuss. Its final compute-control item needs a stronger bridge, so it earns 4 rather than an exceptional score. T loses because its attempted synthesis is submerged in two enormous sentences: "Accountability over AI containment and cross-border compute access tightened sharply" opens abstractly, and "At SC26, Nvidia unveiled its 50,000-core Rubin HPC GPU, Vera CPU racks" starts a closing inventory that runs through multiple companies and funding. M is nearly as difficult, but its sentences retain somewhat clearer individual subjects.

**grok:** M won because it stays on one spine, the suit, OpenAI's reply, the new assessment access, and the export rule, in sentences a reader can finish: "alleging it knowingly tested dangerous models in a flawed environment without proper oversight and misled the public." The only soft spot is "a challenge every major lab has identified," which never names the challenge, but nothing else is as disciplined. P lost because it withholds the nouns and then disclaims itself: "These are announcements and plans, not proof of future performance." "Four outside organizations" and "new decision models" leave an executive with no one to remember and no reason the plans matter.

**dsflash:** The winner, M, is the only summary that starts with an idea rather than an item: 'AI for science had a breakout day,' then delivers three releases that support it, and it earns its second block with 'In model news' instead of a bare connector. It still sags at the close — 'while SemiAnalysis argued that more than 1.5 GW of restricted chips are already inside China, a flow it says could reshape open source AI if it unwinds' is a second, unrelated story smuggled into an infrastructure sentence — which is why it lands at 3.5 and not higher. I, N and J tie just below: each opens cleanly on the California suit with real detail ('one of at least 80 cyber incidents the state says the company failed to contain') and then degrades into list. J is the most frustrating of the three because 'Nvidia's board is not considering reinstating Jensen Huang despite an open letter with more than 175,000 signatures' arrives from nowhere and is joined to two other items by 'while'. The bottom, Q, is the only summary where the reader learns nothing about anything: five stories in 72 words, an undefined 'CMS outlined a policy roadmap for clinical AI,' and a single sentence carrying AMD, OpenAI and Moonshot. A, L and O sit just above it: A for the tangled 'during testing that the state says lacked adequate safeguards' plus a three-item opener, L for spending its last sentence on 'its filing is the new development, and its allegations remain unproven in court,' and O for closing on 'These are announcements and plans, not proof of future performance' — a writer talking to an editor instead of a reader.
