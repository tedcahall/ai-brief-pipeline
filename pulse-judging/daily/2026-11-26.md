## Judge notes (labels refer to each judge's own blind shuffle)

**opus:** F wins with a clear lead story, specific stakes ('a claimed unconditional proof that integers can be factored in the L[1/3] time class') and one integrated caveat ('None of the papers has been peer reviewed'), though its chip item is tacked on. S and V are close behind on clarity, and V's 'a ceiling that says little about how many will actually launch' is the day's best why-it-matters line. P finishes last: 'reported an agentic-inference cost result' says nothing, and 'Benchmark and provider claims should be read in the context of their stated tests and conditions' is a hedge that reads like process leakage. U and A also rank low for spec-stacked run-ons such as 'L_N[1/3, 105] integer factoring bound whose Lean 4 formalizations are not yet publicly released.'

**glm:** M won on texture and a genuine writerly move: 'getting the numerical answer right did not always mean a model could defend it in a manuscript' turns a leaderboard into an insight, and the three-language-count detail ('65 for speech synthesis, 59 for recognition and 53 for real-time conversation') is exact. N and U follow close behind with clean structure and evaluative asides ('a claim AWS has not mirrored', 'a ceiling that says little about how many will actually launch'). J lost because it is pure connective tissue: 'as Anthropic disclosed... while OpenAI began alpha testing' joins everything with 'while' under an umbrella ('In AI hardware and policy') stretched over unrelated domains, and its one hardware claim carries no numbers. B lost for the same emptiness in fewer words: 'SemiAnalysis reported an agentic-inference cost result' informs nobody, and it spends its last sentence on a disclaimer ('Benchmark and provider claims should be read in the context of their stated tests and conditions') instead of news.

**astra:** N wins through readable distinctions and an insightful final turn: 'getting the numerical answer right did not always mean a model could defend it in a manuscript.' Its movement between subscriber limits and reasoning limits gives the reader an actual organizing idea. K loses because 'Independent evaluators also published two new benchmarks in the window' opens a sprawling catalogue, following already overloaded sentences; names and percentages overwhelm hierarchy and interpretation.

**grok:** I won because it picks three stories and finishes each: the factoring claim is immediately bounded by "None of the papers has been peer reviewed," Private Inference is explained as prompts on hardware "the company says it cannot access," and the Nvidia halt names the countries and the source. P lost because its middle sentence tries to hold chip curbs, a nine-month distillation gap, a canceled $150 million deal, and two citations at once, then drops SpaceX satellites in with "Meanwhile" — a reader cannot find the point.

**dsflash:** O won on specificity plus a line of judgment: it names the ratio and the price ('about one-seventh the listed price') and then says what a headline number is worth ('a ceiling that says little about how many will actually launch'), so the reader gets a fact and a read. S and T were nearly as clean and specific ('six points ahead of GPT-6.2 Astra', 'about 70% less CPU per request') but remained pure inventory, with no sentence explaining why any item changes anything. A lost on a broken opening clause ('added six mathematics papers to its public GitHub repository generated by Cogentic') and an unreadable token ('L_N[1/3, 105]'), then compounded it with 211 words of stacked items. F and H lost differently: no figures anywhere ('an agentic-inference cost result', 'measured reductions in CPU use') plus an explicit disclaimer sentence, leaving nothing a reader could act on.
