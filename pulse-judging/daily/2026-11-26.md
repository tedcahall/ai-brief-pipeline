**astra:** R wins with accessible distinctions and the useful interpretive line "getting the numerical answer right did not always mean a model could defend it in a manuscript." **grok:** J won because it opens on a real throughline, 'Production AI infrastructure and enterprise privacy took center stage,' and the 70% CPU cut and 20% latency drop arrive in the first sentence, so a reader finishes oriented; its only flaw is a while-join of silicon and the RSI Safety Act. **dsflash:** W won because it is the only entry that organizes instead of enumerates: each paragraph carries a throughline sentence ("Model releases kept coming from outside the US.", "Policy moved toward disclosure rather than restriction") so the reader gets a thesis, not a roster, and its single caveat is genuinely useful rather than defensive ("a claim built on Amazon-submitted numbers").
