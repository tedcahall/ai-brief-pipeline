## Judge notes (labels refer to each judge's own blind shuffle)

**opus:** L won on structure: a clear lead on Mistral with honest context ('narrowly first among open-weight models built outside China'), then an actual throughline, 'Safety disclosures led the rest of the day,' and a tidy business close. G was close but ended with a three-item list. H lost because it is a string of minor items joined by 'while' and 'also' ('Moonshot also announced a Kimi workflow that connects to 3D creation tools') with no hierarchy, and it buries the OpenAI incident entirely. A was almost as weak because of its garbled 'separately from its replay of an earlier incident.'

**glm:** H wins because it has the clearest editorial spine ('Safety disclosures led the rest of the day') and gives each story a reason to sit where it sits; its worst moment is benchmark-name density ('DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and GLM-5.3 by six points'). C is close behind with concrete, well-paced detail but leans on one overloaded run-on ('more than 30 hours before the earlier Hugging Face breach'). B loses because it is a bare wire-feed list — 'Kimi and Gemini added new creation workflows' is pure filler with no why-it-matters, and E and K share the same failure at slightly greater length.

**astra:** L wins narrowly because "self-hosting options for organizations that want control over deployment" translates the release into an executive reason to care, with comparatively clean prose. It remains a collection of news items, not publication-ready synthesis. E loses because "a 1 trillion-parameter multimodal MoE with a 1M-token context" starts an accumulation of jargon and figures, followed by long, overloaded safety and finance sentences.

**grok:** F won because it is the only brief with a throughline ('Safety disclosures led the rest of the day') and a lead that makes the ranking mean something ('trails DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and GLM-5.3 by six points'), then a consequence on the business beat ('fastest in nearly five years'). The stapled safety sentence and the 'On the business side' closer keep it short of running untouched. E is the clean runner-up: every sentence parses, and the only real miss is the three-item closer. C lost: unrelated items joined by 'while' and 'also' ('while Phonic introduced a speech-to-speech system'), and the line that sounds most like policy still informs no one ('procurement specialists urged clearer standards').

**dsflash:** K wins because it is the only summary with an organizing idea: it sets the Mistral release in a real pecking order ("though it still trails DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and GLM-5.3 by six points") and then says where the day's weight sat ("Safety disclosures led the rest of the day"), so a reader gets hierarchy, not inventory. L is nearly as good — "its monitors alerted within 15 minutes and human review began three minutes later" is the cleanest incident line of the day — but it spends two sentences telling readers what things are not ("not a public model deployment," "target and valuation are not amounts raised"), which reads as an editor arguing with the copy. G loses because it is pure placement reporting with no story: "Fish Audio Drama 3 led open-weight entries in two Artificial Analysis speech arenas, while remaining below some proprietary models overall" tells a reader nothing they can act on, and every paragraph is stitched with "while" or "separately from." H loses for the same hedging habit in weaker form ("remains a fundraising target rather than a confirmed amount raised") plus a vague lead ("with reported coding gains").
