**opus:** M wins with a real throughline ('Chinese labs owned the morning'), concrete stakes ('a foundational problem open for more than 50 years') and readable sentences, losing only for the tacked-on Codex note; H is tighter but closes with 'Separately'. **glm:** I won with a tight two-story structure and a real comparative hook: "Tencent countered hours later with Hy4 flash, a budget model it says matches Claude Haiku 5.5 at roughly one-eighteenth the task cost" — it orients the reader and explains why the launch matters in one clause. C and B close behind for confident throughlines ("Chinese labs owned the morning"), though C runs long and dense. The bottom: Q lost to a jarring, unframed "Nvidia's board accepted Jensen Huang's immediate resignation" plus a hedging "claims remain unverified by independent testers" and a listy tail; O lost to vagueness ("extending their data center infrastructure efforts") and repeated disclaimers ("should be read as company claims, not universal performance rankings"); A lost to pure spec-density with no connective tissue ("priced at $1.50 and $4.50 per million input and output tokens" followed by index points, then a token price, with items joined only by 'and').

**astra:** T wins narrowly: 'Chinese labs owned the morning' establishes a hierarchy, and 'OpenAI pointed frontier AI at fundamental physics' gives the science item a readable frame. Its cost comparisons and insurer growth supply more orientation than most entries, though specification overload and the Codex tail keep it below 4. R finishes last because 'should be read as company claims, not universal performance rankings' spends a whole sentence on generic caution, while 'extending their data center infrastructure efforts' supplies almost no concrete information. It is grammatical but offers neither a clear hierarchy nor enough substantive detail.

**grok:** Q won because it is the only summary with a throughline a reader can feel: "Chinese labs owned the morning," Tencent "shipped its answer," and OpenAI "pointed frontier AI at fundamental physics" on "a foundational problem open for more than 50 years," then a debt figure that moves (up 24%, projected to $185 billion). The slash pricing and the orphan Codex closer keep it short of a 5. O lost because it withholds the news: "should not be treated as interchangeable," "important scientific questions still open," and "New reporting also examines life insurers’ AI-debt exposure" tell an executive what not to conclude, and that reporting exists, not what happened.

**dsflash:** O wins because it is the only summary with a hierarchy and a stated stake: it opens with "Chinese labs owned the morning," frames the second launch as a reply ("Tencent shipped its answer"), and gives the science item a reason to exist ("OpenAI pointed frontier AI at fundamental physics"). Its defects are local — one appositive that grabs the wrong noun ("a foundational problem open for more than 50 years") and a stray Codex sentence tacked onto the money paragraph — so it earns a 4 without being clean enough to run untouched. M is the runner-up on the strength of one genuine analytic line, "Both compete on cost per task and agentic work rather than on raw benchmark leadership," though it withholds the physicist's name and bolts the FAA on with "and." T is the cleanest prose in the set and loses only for its empty hook ("set the pace today") and a tautological "flash-tier model." I finishes last: 174 words of stacked figures — "a 1.8M-line Lean formalization of the Weil conjectures and a 50M-line Lean 4 proof of the Courtade-Kumar conjecture" — plus a Baidu metric dump, so the reader has to decode rather than read. L is barely better, with a ~70-word sentence that joins a Moody's debt figure to Australian legislation under "Meanwhile." The cluster of 2s at the bottom (C, D, J, P) all fail the same way: they spend their sentences telling the reader how to weigh the evidence — "should not be treated as interchangeable," "not a completed deal," "not a settled scientific result" — and deliver almost no specifics in exchange.
