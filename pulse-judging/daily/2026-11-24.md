## Judge notes (labels refer to each judge's own blind shuffle)

**opus:** I won on hierarchy and voice: 'Jensen Huang is back' followed by 'restoring nearly $1 trillion in market value roughly twelve days after his abrupt exit' gives a clear lead and a reason to care, and 'Chinese labs drove an open-weight wave' turns a model list into a trend; only its final 'and'-joined sentence sags. W was nearly as clean but less vivid. U finished last because 'a claim it has not yet been independently verified in this brief' is both a grammar break and process leakage, and K lost for leading with minor items and filler like 'Both announcements add new options for developers.'

**glm:** Best: I opens with the day's biggest story in a clean clause ('NVIDIA's board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as president and CEO, effective immediately, after he resigned roughly two weeks ago, and the chipmaker's shares surged on the news'), keeps every item to one digestible sentence, and closes with a why-it-matters line ('warning that protections against AI risks remain fragmented'). K is close behind with genuinely editorial framing ('Z.ai introduced GLM-5.4, which reuses part of its predecessor to reason more deeply without adding parameters') and smart contrasts rather than parallel lists. Worst: T leaks its own process ('a claim it has not yet been independently verified in this brief') and buries the lead story in paragraph two; H omits the day's dominant story (Huang's reinstatement) entirely and reduces itself to 'Both announcements add new options for developers' plus a 'Separately' list. Mid-pack summaries fail mostly on spec density ('280B-total, 14B-active') and list-joiners ('while,' 'also,' 'separately') rather than on facts.

**astra:** S wins for the clearest editorial selection and most memorable implication: "fewer than one in a hundred legal tasks passed every check" gives executives a concrete limit on usefulness, rather than another specification list. Its access-to-reliability transition could be tighter, so it earns 4 rather than 5. K loses on the broken clause "a claim it has not yet been independently verified in this brief," which also leaks editorial process; its remaining items do not supply enough structure to recover.

**grok:** C won because it argues a difference instead of stacking launches: Baidu "put ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview weights in developers' hands" while "Z.ai took a different route," and the Ling line says why a score can mislead — "fewer than one in a hundred legal tasks passed every check for correctness." S lost on process leakage and a buried, vague lede: "a claim it has not yet been independently verified in this brief," with Huang reduced to "relief for the company's AI financing commitments."

**dsflash:** The winner (R) is the only entry that leads with the day's biggest story and gives it stakes in one move: "Jensen Huang is back: Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated him as president and CEO effective immediately, and the stock jumped about 31% ... restoring nearly $1 trillion in market value roughly twelve days after his abrupt exit." It then organizes the model news under an actual throughline ("Chinese labs drove an open-weight wave") rather than a topic label. Its one slip is a 40-word sentence that yanks the UN summit onto the end of a Microsoft clause. The runner-up (G) is close because it quantifies the consequence ("market value went from roughly $3 trillion to about $4 trillion") and integrates a real caveat, but "with no announcement" is a dangling modifier and the opening sentence is a spec stack. The worst (H) loses because it never says anything: "Both announcements add new options for developers deploying Chinese AI models" is filler, "Separately, SGLang and Miles researchers described an approach" has no subject or stake, and there is no lead at all. Just above it, O had a decent why-line ("a shift investors read as relief for the company's AI financing commitments") but sabotaged itself with a garbled clause and process leakage: "a claim it has not yet been independently verified in this brief."
