## Judge notes (labels refer to each judge's own blind shuffle)

**opus:** E won on clean hierarchy and a usable frame: 'Meta answered on the low end with Muse Ember' ties the second story to the lead, and 'an internal kill switch that stops model workloads within 35 seconds' translates jargon for executives; its only real flaw is the 'Separately' join. G ran close with the best why-it-matters line ('cheaper tokens do not necessarily mean cheaper completed tasks') but went vague on Baidu and Google. O finished last: 'OpenAI described a system for stopping research model workloads' tells the reader nothing, and 'The performance and cost claims remain attributable to their respective authors' is pure process leakage. J nearly tied it by describing Muse Ember twice in back-to-back sentences.

**glm:** J won because it is the only entry that prices both ends of the market with comparative anchors ('five points ahead of Gemini 4 Carbon... at about a third of the cost per task') while closing on a genuine risk story; it reads like a brief with a point of view. I lost because it buries everything under process disclaimer and vagueness: 'The performance and cost claims remain attributable to their respective authors' is the writer's sourcing caveat, not news. C lost ground on an actual content error — Muse Ember described twice in nearly identical words — while N's run-on spec stacking ('AMD EPYC-hosted TPU 8tx... dual-chip TPU 8i Max with doubled compute and HBM bandwidth') made it hard work despite good raw material.

**astra:** O wins with an executive-relevant distinction, "cheaper tokens do not necessarily mean cheaper completed tasks," and a coherent close connecting operational controls to more capable agents. Its thin Baidu item keeps it below publication-ready perfection. J loses because "Meta introduced Muse Ember, its smallest and most efficient model" repeats information already delivered, while its opening and closing sentences overload the reader with unrelated announcements and specifications.

**grok:** E won because a reader finishes knowing what happened and why it matters: Artificial Analysis "ranked Fable 5.2 first on its Intelligence Index at 65," Control Room is "an internal kill switch that stops model workloads within 35 seconds," and Operation Snowball is a named intrusion rather than a vendor adjective. The Meta/Google and-join is a nick, not a collapse. K lost because it withholds every noun a reader could repeat: OpenAI "described a system for stopping research model workloads," and the closer spends itself on "The performance and cost claims remain attributable to their respective authors."

**dsflash:** The best entry wins on throughline and evidence: it pairs the release with "claiming state-of-the-art results in coding, science and knowledge work while cutting prices," then hands the ranking to someone else ("five points ahead of Gemini 4 Carbon") so the reader knows why it matters, and it frames the rest of the day as a market answering — "Meta answered on the low end." Its only real stumble is "Separately, Malbear Labs documented," which drops the intrusion story in cold. The next tier reports the same facts cleanly but leans on stacked clauses ("In prerelease testing, 1,000 Fable 5.2 agents fully formalized the proof... and the model solved two open problems") or bare transitions ("Infrastructure and safety news moved alongside the releases:"). The worst entry loses because it ends in a disclaimer rather than a finding — "These are vendor-reported results, and the evidence does not establish independent replication" — and spends its remaining sentences on hollow paraphrase ("targets automated AI research"). Close behind it, two entries are undone by a single broken sentence each: a dangling modifier ("discovering formally verified quantum programs with improvements of up to 290x") and a repeated, verbatim-style Ember claim restated one paragraph later. Mid-pack entries are all the same shape: correct, busy, and joined by "while"/"and," so the reader finishes informed of announcements but not of a story.
