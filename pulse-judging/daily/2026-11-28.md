V finished last: it hedges twice ("performance claims tied to their stated evaluations", "remain simulation results rather than demonstrated hardware gains") and spends its words on minor items in a vague frame ("Both developments expand specialized model options").

**glm:** The best summary won by doing the one thing no one else did: building an actual argument. Its closer — "the open question is less about any single release than about how fast capability is arriving relative to the safeguards, procurement rules and classroom policies meant to absorb it" — turns a list of headlines into a thesis, and its lede ("moved from rumor to mainstream science coverage") tells the reader where the story stands. The runners-up stay clean and oriented but remain item collections. The worst lost on density and garbled hedging: it stacks specs ("Trainium3 with vLLM outperformed Nvidia's GB300 on inference cost per token across the interactivity curve, trailing only Nvidia's Vera Rubin"), buries its lead item last, and muddles attribution with a clause like "while noting unverified rumors of dangerous breaks as neither OpenAI nor Anthropic confirmed any cryptography result" that no reader can parse on one pass.

**astra:** S wins because "it can return a ranked shortlist rather than a single choice" translates a release into a concrete capability with effortless syntax. Its final hardware item remains underexplained, so it does not earn a 5. G loses because "a 64K-context multimodal decision model" launches a sustained inventory of specifications and its final sentence piles sovereign infrastructure details onto school restrictions; the reader must supply both hierarchy and significance.

**grok:** S won because it is the only one with a real throughline: it turns the cryptography rumor into a stakes sentence ("what a polynomial-time factoring result would mean for banking, browsers and government communications") and then closes by tying the defense deal, the school ban, and the model release back to one question about safeguards lagging capability. H lost because the reader never gets a clean sentence: "tying Base Labs' Bison 31B for first place on the Artificial Analysis Decision Index v2.1 at roughly 61% lower cost per million tasks while adding option ranking and multimodal extraction" is a spec dump, and the Aaronson sentence hedges itself into nonsense ("poses no immediate threat... while noting unverified rumors of dangerous breaks").
