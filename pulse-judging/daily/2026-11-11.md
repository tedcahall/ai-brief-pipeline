**astra:** M wins because its progression from model access to "packaging AI into customer-facing and productivity workflows" supplies an intelligible business takeaway in readable sentences; its vague availability and licensing distinctions keep it below exceptional. F is the closest challenger, with a concrete confidentiality failure explaining stakes, but its financing detour weakens cohesion. P loses because its opening expands through "while Anthropic launched its IPO roadshow" into a roughly 75-word accumulation of prices, benchmarks and financial mechanics; subsequent paragraphs never establish a controlling idea.

**grok:** The best won because it has a throughline and a stake, not a catalog: "Alibaba answered within a day," and the close says why a reader should care, "let an attacker reach requests it had promised to keep confidential." The worst lost on the first sentence, which welds a price, three benchmarks, and an IPO into one wall: "claiming first place on CursorBench 4.0, Perplexity's WANDR and Zapier's AutomationBench, while Anthropic launched its IPO roadshow."

**dsflash:** E won because it is the only summary with a hierarchy and an argument: it leads with the Google release, gives it a price comparison a reader can use ("matching its cheaper Argon model"), supplies a competitive frame ("Alibaba answered within a day"), and closes on a genuinely different item with stakes ("The week's clearest caution came from NEAR AI"). Q is close behind on detail ("about 1 percent of the codebase needing human review") but its lead jams two stories into 55 words and garbles the price ("the same $2 and $10 per million token introductory price"). L lost because it withholds the facts that make a brief useful: an unnamed model ("an image model with downloadable weights"), an empty caveat ("but their licenses differ"), and no numbers anywhere. O and I lost on the same failure at the other extreme: O's lead runs 75 words and folds in an unexplained aside ("a day after Anthropic's Fable 5.2 launch"), while I stacks specifications until the sentence stops being a sentence ("280B-total, 14B-active ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview").
