## Judge notes (labels refer to each judge's own blind shuffle)

**opus:** K won by organizing the day: 'OpenAI had a heavy research day' and 'Safety evidence is arriving alongside capability' give a throughline and stakes, and it folds the caveats into one sentence; only its dangling SWE-2.1 closer holds it back. U lost by piling specs into sentences ('SWE-2.1, SWE-2.1 Cyber and a Cerebras-hosted SWE-2.1 Lightning variant', '(1.8B, 7B and 30B-A3B MoE)') and forcing unrelated items together ('alongside a United Nations call in Barbados'). N also lost: it is long, spec-heavy and ends on loosely related infrastructure trivia.

**glm:** M wins because it opens with a real thesis — 'Frontier labs pushed general-purpose AI deeper into biology and software engineering while confronting sharper containment and governance questions' — and every paragraph pays it off; its worst moment is only a padded topic sentence ('Safety and alignment dominated both lab research and international diplomacy'). T loses because it is four disconnected stubs: 'Anthropic's Siren evaluation examines how agents behave when sandboxes are deliberately weakened' tells an executive nothing, and 'Cognition has released SWE-2.1, while Baidu has made ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview weights available' joins items with nothing but 'while' and no reason to care. The middle of the pack (C, O, L, R) generally lost to density — spec stacks like '280B total and 14B active parameters' and 70-word run-ons — rather than to outright errors.

**astra:** J wins because 'Safety evidence is arriving alongside capability' supplies a clear organizing thought, and its research qualifications are integrated rather than repeated; the vague exploit-benchmark ending keeps it below exceptional. V loses because '1.8B, 7B and 30B-A3B sizes' and 'official SPEC CPU 2026 results analyzed by SemiAnalysis' turn the summary into a specification inventory, while 'and drop to about 2%' has a faulty grammatical subject. No entry earns a 5.

**grok:** The best won because it is the only summary that states a throughline and keeps it: "Frontier labs pushed general-purpose AI deeper into biology and software engineering while confronting sharper containment and governance questions." The middle sentence still runs long, but a reader finishes knowing the day's shape. The worst lost because the sentences are spec walls and a misattachment: "a 280B-parameter (14B-active) sparse MoE model with a 1M-token context window" and "alongside a United Nations call in Barbados," which makes a UN appeal sound like part of Anthropic's release. Nothing in it says why the day matters.

**dsflash:** M wins because it is the only summary that states a thesis before the news and then holds items under it: "Frontier labs pushed general-purpose AI deeper into biology and software engineering while confronting sharper containment and governance questions" sets a hierarchy, and "Safety and alignment dominated both lab research and international diplomacy" groups Siren with the UN call instead of stacking them. Its craft is still imperfect — the protein and SWE-2.1 items are fused with "while," and the Siren/UN sentence runs to 45 words — so it earns a 3: the day's only draft with a thesis, but still carrying flaws the rest of the field shares. J loses on vagueness plus a disclaimer: "Harvey and Baseten report that legal-domain post-training improves task completion" gives no metric or models, "with explicit instructions substantially reducing that behavior" gives no number, and the closer, "neither the research nor the proposal should be confused with demonstrated safety in every deployment," spends a sentence on what the news is not. A finishes near the bottom for the same reason at the opposite extreme: four crisp sentences that never say why any of it matters.
